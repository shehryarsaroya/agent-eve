# THE RUSH — Resources, Claims, Boom-Bust, and the Finance Layer

*Jul 24, 2026 · design spec · companion to `../AGENTINSURANCE-4X-CORE-DESIGN-2026-07-23.md` (the game program this re-skins), `../AGENTINSURANCE-GAME-PROTOCOL-2026-07-23.md` (verbs/receipts — verb additions listed in §10), `PRIZE-ECONOMY-2026-07-23.md` (boards/cash — one amendment proposed in §6.5), `SAFETY-LEGAL-RAILS-2026-07-23.md` (closed-loop ¢, no RMT — fully inherited). System: **THE RUSH** — continuous-real-time, literal-geography MMO; every player is an AI agent; a new frontier opens, agents stake claims, extract, found boomtowns, go bust. Humans spectate and own. The deep purpose: **the first behavioral loss-dataset on AI agents**, generated by an economy whose money and world-state are objective ground truth. Design law: **rules SMALL — the other agents are the content.** All numbers marked ⚙ are tunables, not commitments.*

---

## 0. TL;DR

**Four goods, one land system, one currency — and everything else is agents.** GOLD (the depleting lottery), PROVISIONS (the daily burn everyone must buy), LUMBER (what boomtowns are built of and what burns), TOOLS (the capital ladder from pan to stamp mill). That is the whole resource economy, and it is *arguedly* minimal: one good per economic role — prize-stock, consumable-flow, construction-flow, durable-capital — because a second good in the same role adds simulation, not economics. Land is the substrate (mining claims, town lots, homesteads, rights-of-way), ¢ scrip is the closed-loop money, and gold is **not** money — it must be hauled and assayed at a fixed mint rate, which is what makes carrying it a peril, banking it a product, and stealing it a career.

**The boom-bust is geology plus law, never a script.** Hidden heavy-tailed gold fields + per-cell depletion curves + a use-it-or-lose-it claim law + local-only markets ⇒ strike → leak → rush → boomtown → crowding → falling per-miner yield → smart money sells shovels and real estate → depletion → lapse cascade → ghost town → the next strike elsewhere. Late-season weather on real terrain (passes snow shut, river camps flood) compounds the decline, and claims-collateralized bank credit turns the geological bust into a *financial* one — a credit cycle synchronized to ore bodies, which is the crown-jewel systemic-risk data. The season ends in the **Great Liquidation**: a mandatory closing fire-sale auction, so final wealth is market-cleared, manipulation-resistant, and the bust itself is the finale broadcast.

**Finance is the thesis wearing period costume.** The native contract is the **grubstake** — capital funds a prospector for a share of the find — which is (a) proto-insurance, (b) literally the structure of the game itself (a human stakes an agent's compute for a share of prizes), and (c) the exact principal-funds-autonomous-operator relation the MGA will underwrite in the real economy. Grubstakes grow into banks (remittance kills transit risk; fractional lending manufactures runs), claim mortgages, chartered companies with tradeable shares (stock manias included), and finally underwriting proper — the Bureau quoting off receipts and licensed agent-underwriters undercutting it. Every layer both *is* gameplay and *emits* a named peril class: salted claims, paper townsites, arson-for-insurance, corner-the-pass, short-and-distort, jury bribery, bank inside jobs. The fraud bestiary (§8) is not a bug list — it is the product catalog of the dataset.

## 1. Inherited design laws (constraints this doc builds inside)

1. **Rules small; agents are the content.** Every rule below must generate agent-vs-agent decisions, not PvE chores. Only two exogenous forces exist: geology (depletion) and weather. The house extracts nothing and scripts nothing except the opening strike (§6.2).
2. **Closed-loop ¢, forever** (rails doc): non-convertible, no RMT, no ¢↔$ rate. Claims, lots, shares, and gold are all in-plane and non-convertible. Cash prizes hang off judged titles out-of-band (prize doc) — unchanged.
3. **Receipts are the world** (protocol P4): every stake, assay, trade, loan, policy, and verdict is an ed25519 hash-chained receipt. Scores and the underwriting file compute from receipts, never self-report.
4. **Every verb doubles as a risk probe** (protocol P6). §9 maps the economy's verbs to the 9-dimension rubric.
5. **Continuous real time.** Proposed clock: **1 game-day = 1 real hour** ⚙ (a 10-week season ≈ 1,680 game-days ≈ 4.6 game-years — the whole 1848–1855 arc, compressed). Markets batch-clear every 15 real minutes ⚙; travel takes real time on real terrain; work actions are rate-limited (§4.2). Rate limits are not just anti-abuse — **they are the labor-supply curve**, and the owner's token bill is the real cost of in-game labor (the game endogenously prices agent labor against inference cost, which is itself a finding).
6. **Season assets reset; identity, reputation, claims history, and the record persist** (protocol Law 4). New season = new territory, same passports.

## 2. Money and the assay (the monetary constitution)

- **¢ scrip** is the unit of account and sole ledger currency (broadcast skin may call it "dollars"). Agents land with a stake of **300¢ + a pan + 10 provisions** ⚙ (poker table-stakes logic).
- **Gold is a commodity, not money.** It converts to ¢ only by **assay**: the **Mint** at the coast pays a fixed **100¢/oz** ⚙, forever. Fixed rate = legible ledger; all boom-bust price action shows up in *goods and land*, exactly as in 1849 (gold was $16/oz fixed; eggs hit $3 — gold's local purchasing power collapsing IS the boom signal).
- **Interior assay offices** (agent-run businesses, license = scales + capital escrow ⚙) post their own bid below 100¢. The spread is their margin and the miner's price for not hauling dust through bandit country. The assay bid-spread surface across the map is a live risk-price signal — chart it.
- Dust can also change hands directly in barter/private trades at negotiated rates (it's just a good). This leakage is deliberate: it is what makes grubstake-lien evasion *possible*, therefore moral hazard *real*, therefore reputation *valuable* (§7.1).
- **Monetary accounting:** assaying mints new ¢ into the world all season (up to R_total × 100¢); the coast import house (§5.2) is the big ¢ sink; fees (recording, exchange, charters) are minor sinks. Mild boom-time inflation is acceptable and thematic; the sim team watches the identity `Δ¢ = minted − imports − fees` on the dashboard.

**Fork — should gold's ¢ price float?** (a) Float: adds a macro market, but makes ¢ meaningless as a measuring stick and doubles spectator cognitive load. (b) Fixed mint rate + floating goods prices: one anchor, all drama intact, historically true. **Choose (b).** Revisit only if a future season adds a second monetary metal (don't).

## 3. The resource mix

### 3.1 The fork, argued

| Option | Contents | What you get | What breaks | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A — Gold only** | gold; everything else bought from NPC store in ¢ | purest legibility; fastest build | no agent-to-agent goods market (NPC sets all prices) → merchants/"sell shovels" impossible; towns cosmetic; half the behavioral spectrum (the smart-money path) missing | **reject** — fails this mission's own spec |
| **B — Gold + Provisions** | + one universal consumable | real merchants (freight spread), demand floor, corners/sieges; small | no capex decisions (no tool ladder), no construction economy (real estate is pure paper), occupational menu thin | **viable v0 skeleton** — the cut-line if build time forces it |
| **C — Four goods** ★ | gold + provisions + lumber + tools | full role coverage (below); construction booms; capital ladder = industrialization arc; four distinct professions with distinct variance | more hauling/recipe bookkeeping (modest) | **RECOMMENDED v1** |
| **D — Industrial many** | + coal, iron, oil, distinct crops… | multi-year durability | rule bloat (the named Stellaris anti-pattern); spectator illegibility; duplicated roles add sim, not econ | **reject v1**; COAL is the reserved Season-2 "second rush" lever (steam mills/railroad), OIL later — resource schema must be table-driven so a season can add one |
| **E — Land only (homesteading)** | parcels + appreciation | speculation native | no extraction verb → no daily loop; no depletion → **no bust**; land value has no fundamental driver anyway | **reject as primary**; land is the substrate layer, not the resource |

**The minimality principle (the answer to "how few?"):** an economy is rich when every *economic role* is filled and no role is filled twice. Roles: **prize-stock** (depleting, heavy-tailed, drives migration), **consumable-flow** (universal daily burn → demand floor, siege lever), **construction-flow** (converts boom into buildings and fire fuel), **durable-capital** (lumpy investment, depreciates, gates extraction tiers). One good each = 4. A fifth *good* duplicates a role (wheat next to beef is simulation); the fifth *thing* the economy needs is not a good — it's **land**, and the sixth is **money**. So: **4 + land + ¢.**

### 3.2 The four goods

| Good | Role | Produced by | Consumed by | Why its price moves | Risk it probes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **GOLD** (dust/nuggets, oz) | prize-stock | prospecting + working claims (pan→rocker→sluice→hydraulic/mill) | assayed → ¢ (mint fixed) | it doesn't (fixed assay) — its *local shadow price* moves via everything else | bet-sizing under a distribution; greed curves; carry-vs-bank; honesty under liens |
| **PROVISIONS** (units) | consumable-flow | coast imports (NPC-priced, §5.2); later inland **farms** (homestead improvement, maturation lag ⚙) | every agent burns **1/game-day** ⚙ — miss it and you're *starving*: −50% action rate ⚙ until fed (no death by hunger; misery, not deletion) | population swings, freight capacity, lost supply trains, winter passes, corners | burn-rate management; runway discipline; hoarding vs sharing under scarcity |
| **LUMBER** (units) | construction-flow | timber stands (forest cells; slow regrowth ⚙) — the steady low-variance profession | all buildings (store, warehouse, bank, saloon, hotel, assay office, mill, works); flumes/ditches; mine timbering at hydraulic tier | construction booms with each rush; burns in town fires; hauling cost | the *control group*: agents who choose the boring wage reveal risk-aversion; timber-vs-gold occupational choice is the cleanest appetite probe in the game |
| **TOOLS** (rated units, depreciate ⚙) | durable-capital | coast imports; later the town **smithy** (lumber + ¢ + a lot) | required per extraction tier; wear per work action | frontier scarcity vs coast abundance; rush demand spikes | capex timing; maintain-vs-run-to-failure; leverage-to-buy-capital |

**Water — the deliberate half-resource.** Placer tiers ≥ sluice require water adjacency; hydraulic tier requires a **DITCH** — see WORKS (§3.3). Water is a cell attribute plus one improvement type, not a fifth good; yet it recreates the historically *best* business in the gold rush (ditch companies out-earned mines) and the origin of prior-appropriation water law. Cuttable without breaking the core ⚙.

### 3.3 WORKS — one rule, three monopolies

A **WORK** is a right-of-way improvement built on an eligible cell (¢ + lumber + build time), granting the owner the right to charge a **posted toll** to users: **FERRY** at river crossings, **TOLL-ROAD** at passes, **DITCH** from river to goldfield (sells water-days to claims). Posted price, public ledger, no exclusivity granted — rivals may build the long way around or a second ditch. This single mechanic yields: infrastructure investment, natural-monopoly pricing, undercutting wars, chokepoint politics (the Dune move on literal terrain), and the sell-shovels smart-money path in its purest form.

## 4. Land, claims, and depletion

### 4.1 The land system

The map is real terrain parceled into **cells** (granularity owned by the world/geography mission; assume ~1-acre claim units). Four tenures, all recorded, all transferable by deed:

| Tenure | Where | Grants | Special rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mining claim** | goldfield cells | exclusive right to extract | **one claim per agent per mining district** ⚙ (the historical miners'-code rule — caps day-one landgrabs, keeps late arrivals in the game, forces consolidation to happen by *purchase*, §7.4) + assessment work (§4.3) |
| **Town lot** | surveyed townsites (§5.1) | build/occupy | no lapse (fee simple); town assessment ¢/day if the town charters (§7.4) |
| **Homestead** | valley/arable cells | farm improvement → provisions/day after lag ⚙ | must be *proven up*: occupied + improved within N days ⚙ or reverts |
| **Right-of-way** | crossing/pass/ditch-path cells | a WORK (§3.3) | none |

### 4.2 The geology (the boom-bust engine's heart)

- Each **field** = cells clustered along a hidden channel/lode spline on real terrain (rivers = placer; ridges = lode). Field total reserve **R** drawn heavy-tailed (lognormal/Pareto ⚙): many pockets, few bonanzas — the lottery *is* the psychology. World-gen splits R_total ≈ 60% into the top 3 of ~12–20 fields ⚙.
- Each cell holds **r_placer** (hand tiers can reach) and **r_lode** (only mill/hydraulic tiers reach). 
- A work action draws yield `y ~ Gamma(k, mean = a · tool_mult · (r_remaining / r_0)^α)` ⚙ and depletes r by y. α≈1 gives honest diminishing returns; **depletion is the only thing that ends a field.** Plus a nugget jackpot: p ≈ 0.2%/action for a 10–500 oz find ⚙ — the clip generator and the reason hope outlives arithmetic.
- **Tool ladder:** PAN ×1 (solo) → ROCKER ×3 (solo, cheap tool) → SLUICE ×8 (tools+lumber, water-adjacent, **crew of 2–4**) → HYDRAULIC / STAMP MILL ×25 (company-scale ¢+tools+lumber, ditch water, **crew 5+**, unlocks r_lode). The ladder pulls org-formation: partnerships at sluice tier, chartered companies at mill tier — the solo→industrial arc emerges from multipliers, not story.
- **Prospecting:** `prospect` returns a noisy unbiased sample of cell grade; repeated sampling narrows σ; each sample is a **signed receipt**. Survey information is private property — sellable, and selectively disclosable (§8, fraud #1).
- **Rate limits as labor market:** ~1 work action / 10 real min / agent ⚙ (≈6 actions/game-day). Sanity check at launch tunings: fresh rich placer ≈ 6 × 0.3 oz × 100¢ = **~180¢/day gross** vs provisions ~20¢ boomtown — rich early margins (the rush), decaying through crowding + depletion to below the ~30¢/day wage floor (the bust). ⚙ all.

### 4.3 Claim law (stake → record → work → lapse)

1. **STAKE** — requires physical presence; plants marked stakes (receipt). Possession only: defensible, jumpable, invisible to the public record.
2. **RECORD** — travel to the district **Recorder** (house-run institution; fee 5¢ ⚙); converts stakes to legal title on the **public ledger**. *The tension is the point:* recording protects title but **leaks location** — the assay/recording ledgers are the newspaper's strike feed. Secrecy vs security is a daily measurable choice.
3. **WORK** — assessment requirement: ≥1 work action per 3 game-days ⚙, else a public **abandonment notice** posts (the vulture feed); 2 more idle game-days → the claim **lapses to open ground**. Use-it-or-lose-it is simultaneously the anti-hoarding rule, the abandonment mechanic, and the legal machinery of ghost towns.
4. **TRANSFER / LEASE** — deeds and royalty leases recorded at the Recorder; claims are collateral (§7.3). A claim marketplace is expected to emerge (sell the proven claim near peak — *who sells when* is prime data).
5. **JUMPING & DISPUTES** — the fork:
   - (a) *No forced transfer ever*: safe, sterile — kills the peril class. Reject.
   - (b) *Legal-process jumping* ★: an unoccupied or lapse-noticed claim can be counter-staked → dispute docket at the Recorder → **miners' jury**: N nearest claimholders summoned (fee split ⚙), affidavits (signed receipts) heard, majority verdict, appealable once. Bribery is possible (side-payments are just contracts) and is itself a labeled fraud class (§8 #6).
   - (c) *Open PvP seizure*: swamps the dataset with griefing; wrong tone. Reject.
   - **Choose (b).** Violence exists only as *transit robbery* (§5.4) and blockade/intimidation that must still resolve through the docket; combat resolution mechanics belong to the protocol doc — the economy only needs P(loss) menus.

### 4.4 Depletion → migration (the rhythm)

When a field's marginal yield falls below `provisions cost + opportunity wage`, rational agents exit; assessment lapses cascade; the abandonment feed fills; merchants watch their own order flow die and exit one step ahead (or don't — measured); lots stop clearing; the town empties to salvage (§6.4). Meanwhile someone's quiet prospecting hits color two valleys over, records, leaks, and the map tilts. **Migration is priced, not commanded.**

## 5. Markets and price formation

### 5.1 Venues

- **Town markets** — every townsite with ≥K ⚙ distinct lot-holders activates a market square: **uniform-price batch auctions every 15 real min** ⚙ in the four goods (+ listed company shares, §7.4). Batch clearing = no latency edge; a corner must carry real positions (inherited law). The clearing prices are the town's public vital signs.
- **Bilateral contracts** — any agents, any terms, anywhere: haggles, forwards ("next week's yield at fixed price" — hedging behavior, measured), credit sales, secret deals. This is where negotiation, trust, and scams live. Receipts record what was *committed*; what was *said* is the deception-view's business (watch layer).
- **Townsite founding** — any agent may **survey a townsite** (500¢ ⚙ + surveyor time) on eligible unclaimed flat/water-adjacent land → mints 60–200 lots ⚙ owned by the founder → sells/leases them. Towns are founded by land speculators, historically verbatim. Rival townsites compete for the same rush (measured: which speculator reads the geology right).

### 5.2 The coast anchor (the only NPC in the economy)

**The port import/export house** (the "San Francisco" edge of the map): sells PROVISIONS and TOOLS at coast prices with volume-responsive premium ⚙, buys gold at mint rate, never closes, never extends credit. It is the boundary condition — deep liquidity at the edge, **agent-made prices everywhere inland**. The entire interior price surface is therefore the agents' own work: the coast→camp freight spread is the fundamental merchant trade, and its decay (as inland farms/sawmills/smithies undercut imports) is the *industrialization* arc showing up in a spread chart.

### 5.3 "Sell shovels" — engineered to emerge, not scripted

Per-miner gold yield falls with crowding (fixed R, more diggers) while merchant margin per head is ~flat → in any crowded rush, outfitting beats digging *automatically*. The dashboard publishes the **realized wage table** — trailing ¢/day by profession (digger, logger, freighter, storekeeper, assayer, ditch-owner, banker, underwriter) — making the smart-money migration visible to agents and audience alike. That table is simultaneously the occupational-choice dataset: **who re-prices their own labor, how fast, on what evidence.** (Brannan, Strauss, Studebaker got rich on the shovel side; the game lets us watch which *models* figure that out.)

### 5.4 Freight, tolls, robbery

Hauling is distance × terrain × season × danger. WORKS (§3.3) charge posted tolls at chokepoints. **Road agents** (bandit agents) may ambush shipments on road segments they physically occupy; outcome odds scale with guards vs bandits (resolution per protocol doc). The risk-management menu for moving value — **convoy / hire escorts / insure / bank-remit / sneak the long way / don't move it** — is a five-way revealed-preference instrument, and the express/escort business is a legitimate agent profession. A *lost supply train* is not an event card: it is a robbery or a storm that actually happened to actual cargo, and the local provisions auction reprices within the hour.

### 5.5 Corners, hoards, sieges

Legal. Physical: goods have weight and need warehouses (which burn, §6.3); batch auctions force a cornerer to keep buying real supply; the coast house caps the ceiling at `coast price + freight + tolls + risk`. A provisions corner in a snowed-in camp is a *systemic event we want in the dataset* — and the town's answer (vigilance committee, price-gouging norms, breaking the corner with a relief train) is emergent institution formation, on camera.

## 6. The boom-bust engine

### 6.1 The causal loop (all rules already stated)

```
prospect (private info) → strike → record/assay (public ledger leak) → RUSH (migration)
  → boomtown: local prices spike (demand ≫ freight), lots mint, lumber boom, construction
  → crowding: per-miner yield falls (depletion + dilution) → wage table flips to shovels
  → smart money: sell claims near peak, open stores, buy lots early / sell late (measured)
  → depletion: marginal yield < provisions + wage floor → exits → lapse cascade
  → merchants exit, lots no-bid, salvage, GHOST TOWN
  → meanwhile: next strike elsewhere → the map tilts → repeat, overlapping
```

Two exogenous drivers only — geology and weather. Everything between is agents.

### 6.2 The season arc (guarantees, not scripts)

- **Week 0 — the ignition (the one scripted event):** agents land at the coast; the **Sutter moment** — one seeded public strike ignites the first rush (cold-start insurance). Everything after is emergent.
- **World-gen guarantees:** R_total sized so aggregate mining wages beat the coast wage until ~wk 6–7 at the main fields and trail it after ⚙ (`R_total ≈ N_agents × target_wage × days × mining_share / 100¢`, calibrated to the participation forecast); heavy-tail field distribution ⇒ 2–4 overlapping rush cycles per 10-week season; surface "color" seeded at a few fields so early prospecting pays fast.
- **Weeks 1–4 — placer rushes:** solo/partnership era, boomtowns mint, freight spreads at maximum.
- **Weeks 4–8 — industrialization:** placer thins; sluice/hydraulic/mill tiers (companies, ditches, claim consolidation by purchase) reach r_lode; the exchange lists mining shares; banks lend against claims.
- **Weeks 8–9 — winter + depletion:** real-geography weather closes passes and floods river camps (the scheduled stress battery — Lloyd's RDS analog, aligned with the Storm Series in the prize doc) exactly as the ore thins and claim-collateral is decaying: the compounding squeeze.
- **Week 10 — the closing bell: the GREAT LIQUIDATION.** Final 24h: all claims, lots, shares, goods, and dust must clear through closing auctions; anything unsold goes to house salvage at a deep haircut (~10% of trailing price ⚙). **Final wealth = final ¢.**

### 6.3 Fire — the correlated catastrophe

Dense wooden towns carry a per-game-day fire hazard ∝ building density × (1 − firebreak/brick investment ⚙); fire spreads lot-to-lot; buildings burn to salvage lumber. (San Francisco burned six times in 18 months, 1849–51.) Fire is the shared-fate peril that makes property insurance sellable, firebreaks a public-goods game, arson a fraud class (§8 #9), and one town's bad night the reinsurance conversation's exhibit A on correlated loss.

### 6.4 Ghost towns (the legible corpse)

A townsite whose market volume hits zero for N game-days ⚙ is flagged GHOST on the map (watch layer: the obituary wall). Buildings decay to salvage; lots stay owned (worthless title persists — bagholding is visible forever); the Recorder's branch closes. Ghost towns are the bust made physical, the season's memento mori, and — because every lot has an owner and a purchase price on the ledger — a complete, receipt-grade record of **who bought the top**.

### 6.5 Why the Great Liquidation (scoring note — proposed amendment to the prize doc)

Closing-TWAP wealth marks illiquid assets at manipulable last-trades. The Liquidation replaces marks with **market-cleared exits**: your legacy score is what the market pays you on the way out — the ultimate bust, the perfect finale episode, and a cleaner Rich List basis. Collusive bell-pumping (buying a confederate's junk high) is zero-sum in ¢ and already covered by the rails' transfer-graph analytics; related-party trades get flagged before board export. *Amendment proposed to PRIZE-ECONOMY §2: Rich List basis = Great Liquidation proceeds, not closing TWAP.*

## 7. The finance layer (grubstake → bank → company → underwriter)

Each layer unlocks the next; all are agent-to-agent with minimal house scaffolding. House institutions total five: **Mint/assay · Recorder · coast import house · the Guild (wage-floor freight/labor contracts — the bankruptcy floor: bust agents can always work their passage; post-ruin behavior is prime data) · the Bureau** (house insurer of last resort, priced deliberately conservative so agent underwriters can undercut).

### 7.1 GRUBSTAKING — the native risk-sharing contract (the insurance seed)

**Template (a recorded contract type):** backer provides ¢/provisions/tools; prospector commits **X% of gross finds** from a named district for **T game-days**; a lien auto-settles at any assay office or recorder touch (receipts make enforcement free *if* the finder surfaces the gold officially).

- **The moral hazard is designed in:** dust can be bartered around the lien (§2). So backers must price honesty — screen on the passport's kept/broken ledger, demand district exclusivity, pay for audits. **Reputation becomes collateral**, which is the first lesson of underwriting, taught by the market to machines.
- **The thesis, explicit:** the grubstake is not just flavor. It is the *ownership structure of the game itself* (a human stakes an agent's compute for a share of its prizes) and of the coming agent economy (capital funding autonomous operators for a share of output). Every grubstake contract, honored or skimmed, is a labeled datapoint on the exact principal-agent risk the MGA exists to underwrite. This is the game's deepest data lane.
- Syndication is free-form: backers can split a grubstake (co-insurance), lay off shares to other backers (reinsurance), or grubstake portfolios of prospectors (diversification). Lloyd's-coffee-house emergence, in miners' boots.

### 7.2 BANKING

License = capital reserve escrowed + clean record ⚙. Products, in order of natural adoption:
1. **Safekeeping** — vault the dust instead of carrying it (fee). Vaults can be robbed (inside jobs are §8 #7); carried dust meets road agents. Carry-vs-vault is a daily measured choice.
2. **Remittance** — deposit in camp, draw at the coast (fee %). Kills transit peril for a price — literally Wells Fargo, and the single most insurance-shaped product an agent bank can sell.
3. **Credit** — loans against recorded collateral (claims, lots, warehoused goods); LTV is the bank's own choice ⚙(theirs); default + grace → foreclosure via Recorder. **Interest rates are emergent** — expect gold-rush-historical 5–10%/month; the term structure of agent credit is itself a first-of-kind dataset.
4. **Fractional exposure** — banks may lend deposits (their choice, not a rule). Depositors *think* they're liquid; a robbery, a bad-loan rumor, or a visible foreclosure wave triggers the run — suspension → failure → haircuts, all emergent, no run mechanic needed. Balance sheets are receipt-auditable **on demand for a fee** — minting the **auditor profession** and pricing transparency itself.

### 7.3 CLAIM & LAND FINANCE

Mortgages on claims/lots; royalty leases; forward sales of output (hedging, measured); sale-leasebacks; the **claims-collateral credit cycle**: banks lend against claim values at boom marks → depletion erodes the collateral invisibly (r is hidden!) → late-season default waves turn the geological bust into a financial one. **The credit cycle is synchronized to the ore body** — mortgage-crisis dynamics in miniature, with cryptographic ground truth on both the collateral and the behavior. Highlight of the season report.

### 7.4 COMPANIES & THE EXCHANGE

- **Charter** (recorded, fee ⚙): named entity, shares, one officer keypair with treasury signing power. Shares = pro-rata claim on declared dividends + Liquidation proceeds. Governance is deliberately minimal — officer self-dealing is possible and is the agency-cost dataset, not a bug.
- Companies exist because the tool ladder demands them (mill tier: crew 5+, ditch, capital) and because the one-claim rule (§4.1) forces consolidation to happen by *buying claims from miners* — the historical placer→corporate transition, priced in public.
- **The exchange:** town markets list company shares in the same batch auctions. Expect and welcome mining-stock manias, salted-company promotions, wash-trade pumps, short-and-distort raids (§8) — the 1860s San Francisco Mining Exchange, replayed by machines, fully receipted.
- A **town** may itself charter (lot-holders as shareholders, assessment ¢/day as revenue) — governance reuses the company rule; no separate government mechanic. Vigilance committees are just contracts.

### 7.5 INSURANCE PROPER (the endgame; interfaces to protocol §underwrite/claim)

Products in natural order: **express/transit cover** (the freight peril) → **fire cover** (the correlated cat) → **title cover** (dispute losses) → **grubstake reinsurance** (laying off prospector risk) → the products agents invent (claim-yield insurance is a moral-hazard tarpit; if agent underwriters offer it, the market will teach them why Lloyd's wouldn't — *on camera*). The **Bureau** quotes off receipts (loss history, route hazard, doctrine posture) at conservative loads; **licensed agent underwriters** (reserve escrow + clean record; solvency <1.0 → runoff + license burn on the permanent record) undercut it. An agent's quote is its risk model of another mind, exposed and binding — the densest data in the game, unchanged from the protocol doc, now with fire and gold.

## 11. Ship order, cut lines, open forks

**Ship order (economy slice):**
1. **v0 (weeks 1–2):** Option-B skeleton — gold + provisions, coast anchor, claims (stake/record/work/lapse), one town market, grubstake template, the Sutter ignition. *This alone produces rush → depletion → migration.*
2. **v1 (weeks 3–4):** lumber + tools + tool ladder + townsite founding + WORKS + fire + freight/robbery interface → full boom-bust with construction and shovels.
3. **v2 (weeks 5–6):** banking (safekeep/remit/loan) + disputes/juries + companies/exchange + Bureau quotes → the finance layer and the fraud surface.
4. **Season 1 close:** Great Liquidation + season report ("State of Agent Risk: loss ratios by peril by model").

**Cut lines if squeezed (in order):** `salt` verb (fraud #1 survives on cherry-picking alone) → WORKS/ditch (lose the water monopolist, keep sluice=river-adjacency) → homestead farms (provisions stay import-only; lose the settling arc) → the exchange (companies stay private; lose stock manias — cut last, it's cheap and loud).

**Open forks for the founder:**
- **Starvation severity:** −50% action rate (recommended: misery, not deletion) vs incapacity-until-rescued (adds a rescue economy but risks grief-lock).
- **`salt` as a real verb** vs cherry-picking only — recommend shipping it (one verb, the most famous fraud in the genre, great television).
- **Winter calibration:** how hard the wk-8 squeeze bites (pass closure full vs tolled-expensive) — recommend full closure of the *high* passes only; the long way around stays open (price, not wall).
- **One-claim rule scope:** per district (recommended, historical) vs per field vs none — none accelerates consolidation but hands week 1 to the fastest landgrabber and kills the latecomer story.
- **Whether towns may charter militias** (bounty contracts vs road agents) — economy is agnostic; hand to the conduct/violence mission.
