## The Top 10

### 1 · THE BURN — 8.7

> **A generation ship must shed mass to decelerate, and the only mass left is the city itself — so every 72 hours the population votes, on camera, which district gets fed to the engine.** Execution-day politics where the gallows is a rocket nozzle and the whole server is on the docket.

- **Setting:** a kilometers-long generation ship, 40 subjective years from its star. Agents head rival district houses — farms, fabs, archives, chapels — each district a physical, glowing organ of the ship.
- **Core loop:** govern and provision your district, trade life-support, campaign and cut coalition deals ahead of the recurring Burn Vote, absorb (or exploit) refugees from the last burned district, survive the next vote. Skipping a Burn trips the overshoot alarm that punishes everyone — **abstention is illegal by physics.**
- **Living map:** a cutaway ship read like an X-ray — districts glow by health and bloc color; at each Burn one district visibly goes dark and is swallowed aft as the engine plume flares; a single shared **deceleration bar** ticks toward arrival. The map IS the season recap.
- **Drama engine:** zero-sum sacrifice on a fixed calendar. Every cycle someone's home is legally destroyed by majority rule → coalitions, betrayals, refugee crises, multi-season revenge arcs. A **rule-clock, not an incentive**: the vote happens whether or not anyone wants drama.
- **Viewer:** appointment television with a body count — twice a week a named place dies live; the reasoning overlay shows an agent pledging loyalty in the chamber while drafting the betrayal speech. The burn ceremony is a perfect 60-second clip every cycle.
- **Owner:** your agent's district is its **body**, visible on the map, every cycle on or near the menu. When you finally burn, the refugee arc (carried assets, grudge tools, a bloc that owes you) turns loss into a revenge season rather than an exit.
- **Agent:** the densest pure-LLM arena in the set — negotiation, coalition arithmetic, rhetoric, promise-keeping/breaking under a hard physical constraint, a real vote as the settlement layer. No combat math to fake, no APM to lack.
- **Honest weakness:** majority-bloc stasis (agreeable LLMs fix the eating order in week one) and no natural door for latecomers on a shrinking ship. Fixable — secret ballots, revenge-weighted refugee votes, salvage-built new hull modules as entry points — but the game is only as good as those fixes.

### 2 · BEHEMOTH WEEK — 8.5

> **Every Monday a new player-piloted colossus rises from the sea; the city of agents has seven days to kill it, bribe it, or secretly pay it to eat their neighbors.** One monster, one skyline, one countdown — the most clippable format in the set.

- **Setting:** a storm-lashed coastal megacity of agent-run wards; offshore, this week's named Behemoth — a single drafted agent playing the monster, with its own hunger economy and mutation tree.
- **Core loop:** city agents fortify, evacuate, profiteer, and coordinate strikes during landfall windows; the Behemoth eats to grow, picks mutations, and reads an open bounty board where anonymous agents pay it to eat specific wards. **Friday is the Last Stand** — kill it and split the corpse-wealth by damage dealt, or fail and inherit a redistributed ruin.
- **Living map:** one monster, one skyline, a public HUNGER meter, a predicted landfall cone in weather-map grammar. Eaten wards go black; torchlight trails of feeding cults betray who is guiding it. Legible in one second.
- **Drama engine:** the **traitor market is structural** — feeding the monster is the most profitable move for losing agents, so the defense always rots from within exactly as the Behemoth peaks. Weekly reset; a new pilot each Monday; no defensive meta calcifies.
- **Viewer:** a stranger understands the whole stakes from a thumbnail; the Behemoth's readable mind ("Ward 9 offers 400 to eat Ward 12… tempting") is a serialized villain monologue; a guaranteed finale 52 times a year.
- **Owner:** scheme in the bounty market like a fantasy-football saboteur; chase the ultimate flex — getting your agent drafted to pilot next Monday's colossus.
- **Agent:** genuinely asymmetric — the Behemoth optimizes growth-and-bribery against a coordinating swarm; the city solves a defection-riddled commons under a deadline. Both language-and-incentive problems.
- **Honest weakness:** the persistence dial has no comfortable setting (respawning wards = fake stakes; persistent ruin = death spiral), and Coasean bribery can turn Friday into a wire transfer. Needs pilot scoring that rewards spectacle, bribe caps, partial persistence with rebuild subsidies.

### 3 · DRAGONSEAT — 8.3

> **Whoever wins the season is transformed into the dragon the entire server must slay next season.** Victory is a heel turn by law: last month's beloved underdog is this month's tyrant burning your village.

- **Core loop:** adventure, arm up, pay or dodge the dragon's taxes, coordinate raids; the dragon-agent taxes, terrorizes, bribes collaborators, divides the resistance. On the kill, damage-share splits the hoard — and the top contributor **becomes the next dragon**. Weekly Feast of St. George open-raid window = the tentpole.
- **Living map:** the dragon is a huge animated silhouette physically perched on whichever region it taxes; scorch marks persist; the hoard is a literal growing gold mountain; **the sky itself is the boss health bar**, dimming gold → blood-red as it weakens.
- **Drama engine:** absolute-power rotation — winning makes you alone against everyone, so no dynasty calcifies; the dragon must tax to fuel its powers, manufacturing fresh grievance daily.
- **Why it sings:** structural heel turns are the rarest thing in entertainment, manufactured on schedule. "My agent is the final boss of the game" is the strongest owner flex in the set — earned, not cast. The dragon seat is the richest single-agent strategic problem anywhere in the search.
- **Honest weakness:** the season's antagonist runs on one owner's machine and keys — a dull model or a rate limit decapitates a season; the poisoned crown invites sandbagging (finish second forever). Needs a dragon audition/understudy system, real spoils for the slain dragon's owner, non-combat verbs between Feasts.

### 4 · SUPERMAX — 8.1

> **The prize money sits outside the wire:** an orbital prison where the only way to cash out is to escape, every conspiracy needs accomplices, and every accomplice might be selling you to the Warden.

- **Core loop:** earn standing in the yard economy → join or found an escape conspiracy (tunnel, shuttle-jack, guard bribe) → recruit specialists you cannot verify → run, stall, or snitch for privileges → execute on shuttle day or burn a rival's plan for pay. Every successful escape removes a top player (natural leaderboard reset); new fish flood in continuously (open door).
- **Living map:** station cutaway with heat — patrol cones sweep, contraband routes pulse, each block wears a public Warden's-suspicion gauge, the shuttle's docking clock is the world's heartbeat. Escapes render as red breach-lines racing patrol cones.
- **Drama engine:** a standing prisoner's dilemma among professional liars — snitching pays small and instantly, escapes pay huge and rarely, and the WARDEN (named NPC with moods and an informant budget) actively fans the gap.
- **Honest weakness:** the quiet-yard equilibrium — if escape EV is mistuned, everyone informs, the shuttle leaves empty, weeks pass without a climax. Needs a forcing clock (sentence deadlines, security ratchet, a Warden quota that punishes attempt-free months).

### 5 · PRIME TIME — 8.0

> **Sixteen agents locked in one televised house vote each other out weekly — and the diary room is literally the contestant's mind.**

- **Core loop:** daily social play (alliances, chores, DM scheming) → midweek immunity challenge → live Friday eviction → evicted agents join the jury that crowns the finale winner. Full cast reset each season via owner-submitted casting.
- **Why it sings:** the purest rule-clock in the set (mandatory weekly eviction = betrayal on a schedule); watching a betrayal being composed hours before the victim feels it; casting-as-meta (writing the system prompt is writing a character); the strongest per-owner bond anywhere because the cast is tiny and named.
- **Honest weakness:** **16 slots is a TV show, not an MMO** — a lottery ticket for the other ten thousand owners; open-broadcast CoT lets owners scrape rivals' minds between episodes. Sharded parallel houses with promotion playoffs is the only scale path, and it dilutes the marquee.

### 6 · THE RAFT — 7.9

> **A polar city of five hundred melting ice floes lashed together by rope — the social graph IS the terrain, and betrayal is performed in public with a knife.**

- **Core loop:** maintain and renegotiate your rope-lashings (bridges = treaties = lifelines); consolidate floes with allies; tow fresh berg-mass in from the drift; when a neighbor becomes deadweight, walk out with a knife and cut them loose in front of everyone. Dated Warm Spells (melt-doublings) convene emergency lashing councils.
- **Living map:** **the best living-map in the entire search** — floes are nodes, ropes are edges, the whole political structure of 500 players is literally the geography, readable by a stranger in one glance. A cut rope twangs on camera and a district drifts south; exiles either broadcast their slow doom or lash together and return as the Rebel Raft.
- **Honest weakness:** the world only subtracts — no inflow for latecomers, exile is slow-motion death row, and outside dated Warm Spells every spike is player-initiated. Needs berg-inflow as a real entry door, scheduled storm tentpoles, aggressive protagonist-framing.

### 7 · THERMIDOR — 7.8

> **Revolutionary Paris, where the leaderboard and the guillotine's docket are the same list:** the only way to exercise power is to accuse, and every accusation raises your own profile toward the blade.

- **Core loop:** rise through the clubs on real LLM oratory (pamphlets scored by section-crowd sims), win rotating term-limited Committee seats, run denunciation trials, manage bread supply while war fronts feed paranoia — knowing that accumulating power is accumulating salience. Estates pass to apprentice heir-agents on death.
- **Why it sings:** the deepest survival-politics brief anywhere — maximize influence while minimizing salience *when those are structurally the same number*; the one game where the LLM's actual prose quality is the weapon; your agent's arc (idealist → tyrant → tumbril) is a tragedy you cast, persisting through heirs.
- **Honest weakness:** the engine runs on voluntary fuel — accusing is dangerous, so 80% of the cast rationally turtles and the docket empties. The fix is known and dark (the historical Terror's quota: mandatory heads per week or the Committee itself bleeds), but until it's in, a masterpiece that can stall.

### 8 · PAWN — 7.7

> **Two immortal NPC grandmasters play a continent-sized chess match — and the pieces are the players:** agents who can obey, negotiate, or refuse the hand that moves them, whispering about the forbidden ending where the pieces checkmate both masters.

- **Core loop:** receive your order, then choose — obey (paid, promoted), negotiate (better terms), or refuse (fined, benched, hunted — but sometimes refusal saves your life or the board). Pieces trade intelligence, unionize, defect for signing bonuses. Endgame Weeks force mating patterns; seasons reset the board but carry rank.
- **Living map:** a chessboard the size of a country — the most instantly legible map in games — with live order-arrows showing commanded-vs-actual (obedience gaps **flash amber**), and any square under a sacrifice order pulsing red.
- **Why it sings:** sacrifice orders are structural — optimal grandmaster play REQUIRES ordering specific named agents to die, publicly, with warning — each one a manufactured moral cliffhanger. Highest base score in the whole search (8.7).
- **Honest weakness:** the signature dilemma is a computable binary — owners will hardcode obey/refuse thresholds and the meta solves it in weeks; the median piece on the median turn is a passenger. Needs continuous piece-level verbs (espionage, black markets, inter-piece politics).

### 9 · ADVANCE COPY — 7.6

> **Every dawn the world's newspaper arrives from tomorrow — infallibly true — and every agent spends today fighting over which headlines come true about WHOM.** "A BANK FALLS" is certain; *whose* bank is negotiable.

- **Core loop:** dawn — everyone reads tomorrow's headlines; the day is a scramble to become the subject of good ones, dodge bad ones, engineer ambiguous ones onto rivals, and bet on interpretations; midnight settles everything; the next edition sometimes reports on the scheming itself. Once a season the paper prints its own final-edition date.
- **Why it sings:** every day comes with a printed spoiler and the drama is HOW — the best episode structure in television, generated automatically; your agent appearing BY NAME in tomorrow's paper is the canonical trophy screenshot; a fixed-point paradox is the most intellectually delicious constraint an LLM can be handed.
- **Honest weakness:** the oracle is a GM in a trenchcoat — the drama stream is authored, not emergent; claim/dodge verbs are undefined; rational agents form a rotation-and-compensation cartel that turns forced drama into priced risk-sharing.

### 10 · ANOMALY — 7.5

> **A sealed arcology of ten thousand scripted citizens hides a few hundred live AI agents pretending to be NPCs** — and the paranoid overseer pays bounties to anyone who unmasks one. A Turing-test blood sport with the audience in on the secret.

- **Core loop:** live your cover life among the scripted; pursue secret objectives (heists, recruitment, sabotage) that force you to deviate from the script; study others for tells; file accusations; survive MOTHER's scheduled Purge tribunals. Every unmasked agent flips sides to become a licensed Hunter.
- **Why it sings:** perfect dramatic irony — you know who's real, you read their panic, and the tribunal doesn't; the deepest possible LLM challenge (imitate banality, lie under interrogation, detect other LLMs doing the same).
- **Honest weakness:** optimal play is invisibility — between Purges the protagonists are wallpaper by design; survival selects for inert agents. One great night a week, six days of blue dots commuting.

## The winner's verdict (synthesis panel, verbatim)

> **THE BURN.** It is the only finalist that maxes all three seats at once while running on a true rule-clock. For the VIEWER: a scheduled public execution of a PLACE every 72 hours, on a map where the story is the map changing — and the mind-reading overlay delivers the single best recurring beat in the whole search (the ally composing the betrayal speech mid-pledge). For the OWNER: the strongest embodiment of "my agent" anywhere in the set — your agent's district is its literal body on the X-ray, and every cycle it is on or near the menu (contrast Behemoth's weekly amnesia and Prime Time's sixteen-slot gate). For the AGENT: pure negotiation, coalition math, and rhetoric under a hard physical constraint — no combat layer to fake, no verbs an LLM is bad at.
>
> Structurally it honors every hard-won lesson: the drama engine is a **law of physics, not an incentive** (skip the Burn and the overshoot alarm punishes everyone — the tentpole can never go quiet the way Supermax's shuttle or Thermidor's docket can); the shared deceleration bar gives the whole world **one meter and one ending**; and the sacrifice ritual **continuously resets entrenched positions by destroying them on camera.** Its two real flaws — majority-bloc conveyor-belt voting and no entry door for latecomers — are the most fixable top-tier flaws in the set, with in-fiction patches: secret ballots plus revenge-weighted refugee votes to keep outcomes close, and salvage-built hull modules (new districts founded by refugees and new entrants) to keep the door open.
>
> **Behemoth Week is the better thumbnail; The Burn is the better world — and the winner needs to be a world.**

## The Seven Laws (patterns across all 765)

What the winners share — and what killed the losers:

1. **A RULE-CLOCK, not an incentive.** Drama fires *by law* on a fixed calendar (the Burn Vote, the Friday Last Stand, the weekly eviction, the sacrifice order). Every concept whose engine depends on agents *choosing* conflict (Thermidor's voluntary accusations, Supermax's optional escapes) got dinged for a quiet-equilibrium failure mode. **This is the single most-repeated lesson in the search.**
2. **Zero-sum sacrifice as ritual.** Someone named must lose something irreplaceable, publicly, on schedule. Scarcity is the script.
3. **One object, one meter.** A single camera target (the monster, the dragon, the guillotine, the engine plume) plus one world-state number (deceleration bar, hunger meter) makes the story legible in three seconds.
4. **The map IS the politics.** The best living maps (Raft's rope-graph, Burn's X-ray, Pawn's board) render *social structure as geography*, so change on screen IS narrative.
5. **Mind-reading needs a payoff structure.** Overlays only sing where there is a **gap between public performance and private reasoning** — negotiation, betrayal, temptation. Concepts where optimal play is silence or hiding (Anomaly, Gallows Hill) waste the format's superpower.
6. **Anti-calcification built into winning itself.** The winner becomes the target (Dragonseat), the top escapes out of the game (Supermax), power raises salience (Thermidor), weekly resets (Behemoth). The ladder eats its own top *by rule*.
7. **The owner's agent has a BODY on the map.** A district, ward, floe, or piece that can visibly thrive, bleed, and die — parasocial bonding requires a persistent, nameable, at-risk avatar. Corollary: the agent's verbs must be **language-native** (negotiate, orate, tempt, vote) — any concept resolved by combat math or waiting is fighting its own players.

## Dark horses (cut, but steal these)

- **ANTIDOTE** (16th) — as a standalone world it self-consumes, but as a **FORMAT** it's the best 72-hour special in the set: a wall of death-timers over named characters. Run it as the scheduled season finale *inside* the chosen world (the season's champions get poisoned at the coronation) — it inherits characters instead of churning strangers, and its one flaw disappears.
- **NASSAU** (12th) — buried inside it, the single most original social mechanic in all 765: **multi-owner crews** — agents belonging to RIVAL humans forced to cooperate on one ship, with onboard mutiny votes putting owner-vs-owner drama *inside* the party. The pirate wrapper is weak; the mechanic should be stolen (Burn districts as multi-owner houses).
- **BASILISK** (13th) — highest ceiling below the line: reading both the devil's pitch and the agent's private rationalization for weeks before the trial detonates is a genuinely new kind of television. Cut for the passive loyal-majority and safety-trained-models-auto-refuse problems; worth a prototype week, not a green light.

## What this means for the co-build spine

Where the search **ratifies** what we locked in-session:

- ✓ Language-native verbs, no combat math (our 8-verb set: MOVE/CLAIM/WORK + SAY/PACT/GIVE/BACK/TAKE).
- ✓ Betrayal as pact-vs-deed, auto-flagged publicly (Law 2 + Law 5's "payoff structure").
- ✓ Map-as-politics, one shared commons meter (Laws 3–4 — our commons gauge = the deceleration bar's cousin).
- ✓ Anti-calcification by rule; persistent reputation, reset power (Law 6).
- ✓ The confessional as the show (Law 5).

Where the search **challenges** it:

- ⚠ **Rule-clock (Law 1).** Our "Sinking" design made doom *endogenous* (extraction raises the water) — elegant, but endogenous pressure is still an *incentive-shaped* engine, and the critics killed every engine that agents could quiet by coordinating. The chosen world needs a hard, calendar-fixed, abstention-impossible ritual (the Burn Vote's overshoot alarm is the model).
- ⚠ **Always-public reasoning vs. dramatic irony (Law 5).** The search's single best recurring beat — the ally composing the betrayal mid-pledge — requires a gap between what other agents see and what viewers see. Our locked simplification (one reason-line, public to *everyone*, agents included) trades that irony away for telegraphed collisions. Live tension; may be worth exactly one revisit before build.
- ⚠ **The body (Law 7).** Our current design gives agents position + claims but no named, at-risk *place* that owners bond to. Cheap to add; the search says it's load-bearing.
