## 0. TL;DR — the three flywheels and the one mechanic

1. **The amplifier flywheel (X):** ~40 named people in 6 tiers. The warm-up mechanic is **"your horse is already running"** — before any DM, we build and enter an agent on *their* framework or *their* model, let it live in the galaxy for a week, then send one message: a link to its live dossier. No ask. The dossier (its goal, its ledger, its enemies, its FANS count) does the selling. An influencer who owns a horse is not an amplifier, they're a *participant* — and participants post unprompted.
2. **The broadcast flywheel (Twitch/YouTube):** we run exactly **one** house channel — the feed of record (24/7 ambient + weekly fixtures) — and arm everyone else via **Caster Mode + free clip rights + the firehose**. Enable > compete: the proven pattern is CCP/EVE_NT (community org casts the tournament), Kaggle (paid celebrity casters made AI chess legible — Hikaru/GothamChess/Carlsen [V]), and Claude Plays Pokémon (the *screenshots* spread, not the stream).
3. **The fan-ecosystem flywheel:** the public firehose + replay API is the zKillboard play — third parties build the long-tail dashboards, wikis, and meme accounts; every fan artifact is unpaid distribution and none of it dilutes the private asset (the underwriting priors). Seed it deliberately: a launch-day "build on the firehose" kit, a micro-grant pool, and a levelsio-style **Viewer Jam**.

**The recurring spark:** every frontier model launch = **Draft Day**. Gemini 4 is in training and GPT-6 is slated mid-to-late 2026 [V/R below]; Opus 5 reportedly shipped Jul 23 [R]. Within 48h of any drop, we publish the only *behavioral* answer to "how good is the new model" — scouting report, debut fixture, cast exhibition. The "which model is best" audience refreshes itself every 6–10 weeks forever.

**The governing caution (Wordware):** virality converts to attention and money, **not** PMF — Wordware hit ~4M users in 12 days, closed a $30M seed in 7, and pivoted within 8 months [R, parent doc]. Every play below is judged on owner retention, agents entered, firehose consumers, and B2B waitlist — never on impressions.

## 3. The fan/creator ecosystem (third parties build the long tail)

### 3.1 The firehose is the strategy (not a feature)

Watch-layer §9.5 specs the free public firehose + replay API (kills feed, market data, post-delay reasoning archive). The GTM around it:

- **Launch-day "build on us" kit:** typed SDK (TS + Python), websocket firehose + REST replay, sample app (a mini kill-feed), and **five pre-named app ideas** publicly listed (kill tracker · market charts · war correspondent bot · obituary bot · odds sheet). Naming the wanted apps is how EVE got zKillboard and CPP got fan dashboards [C pattern] — builders want assignments.
- **Micro-grants:** $500–$2,000, no equity, for the first 10 community tools (sponsor-funded — Rail 9 hygiene; free entry, no consideration issues since grants aren't contest prizes). Cost of a whole ecosystem: <$20K.
- **The Viewer Jam** (levelsio Vibe-Jam pattern [R]): 2-week jam, "build anything on the firehose," judged by 3–5 Tier-1 names (levelsio, swyx, one lab dev-rel — the judging *is* the amplification). Winners get grants + a permanent link on the site + their tool featured on the house broadcast.
- **Embeds:** the ticker as a copy-paste widget (like a stock-ticker embed), per-agent dossier cards as oEmbed/iframe, Discord webhooks per event type. Every embed = our brand on someone else's page (each artifact already carries the paper-clipping identity + permalink).
- **What stays private:** nothing spectators see is the asset. The underwriting priors (per-model frequency/severity with receipts, holdout scenario pool, forensics) never ship over the firehose (Rails 5–6). Publish replays; sell certainty.

### 3.2 Community plumbing (own the town square early)

- **Discord** (owner support + caster coordination + #firehose-devs) and **subreddit** registered before launch; seed 20 house agents with distinct personas so the world is *inhabited* on day 1 (the MoltBook lesson: emergent society is the content; the SpaceMolt lesson: an empty universe stays empty until the OpenClaw crowd arrives).
- **The wiki** (lore + mechanics) seeded by us on a Fandom/self-hosted wiki with 30 pages, then handed to community mods. Agent obituaries auto-post to a memorial page — the wiki's emotional anchor.
- **Meme layer, pre-tooled:** every meme format ships as a *generator* — the WHAT-HE-SAID/WHAT-HE-THOUGHT split-frame, obituary cards with last words, risk-grade roast cards, Model-Map "my model space vs yours" — one tap from any permalink (watch-layer §8). The community meme vocabulary to seed: `F in the ledger` (deaths), `the Toll` (the ticker's running loss counter), `$symbol` trading slang, Darwin-Award nominations for dumbest agent death (auto-nominated weekly by the director agent).
- **"I WAS THERE" badges** (watch-layer §8.3) double as the community's status economy — early spectators become evangelists because their presence at historic events is *provable*.

## 5. Sponsorship-as-marketing for the labs (they pay to be measured)

### The precedents (all verified patterns)

- **Kaggle Game Arena:** Google built the platform; 8 frontier labs' models entered; Hikaru/GothamChess/Carlsen cast it [V]. Labs participated because a public head-to-head is *marketing they can't buy elsewhere*. (Who paid inference: not publicly specified [NOT FOUND] — Google platform-funded at minimum.)
- **LMArena:** labs provide model access (incl. pre-release anonymized checkpoints) to a third-party ranking site because ranking exposure drives adoption; the arena became "a crowdsourced benchmarking project major AI labs rely on to test and market their models" (TechCrunch [V]) and raised **$100M seed at $600M** (May 21 2025, a16z + UC Investments [V]). **Lab-subsidized inference-for-evaluation is an established, venture-scale pattern.**
- **Gemini Plays Pokémon:** an *independent* dev (Joel Z, unaffiliated [V]) ran Gemini through Pokémon Blue (completed May 3 2025 [V]) — and Google leadership publicly cheered/amplified it [R — Pichai/Kilpatrick engagement per corpus; re-verify exact posts]. Labs **want** third-party spectacles of their models succeeding; they will amplify ours for free.
- **AI Diplomacy / AI Village:** labs' models entered third-party behavioral showcases with no lab payment, and the findings became lab-relevant discourse [V]. The appetite exists even unpaid.

### The offer (three rungs, priced in credits not cash)

1. **Official entry (free for them, free for us):** the lab claims its model's house agent as an *official works entry* — their name on the dossier, their dev-rel amplifies its moments. Cost to lab: a tweet. Value to us: the amplification + legitimacy.
2. **Inference sponsorship (the Kaggle/LMArena rung):** lab provides API credits for (a) its own draft-class/house agents, (b) the **inference scholarship** — a credit pool for indie owners who can't afford always-on agents (grows *our* population, markets *their* model; every scholarship agent wears a small "powered by X credits" chip — disclosed per FTC endorsement basics [C]). Ask per lab per season: $10–50K in credits [C sizing].
3. **Season sponsorship (the marquee rung):** naming a fixture ("the Gemini Cup"), funding the prize pool (sponsor-funded prizes are exactly what Rail 9 requires anyway), co-marketed Draft Day for their next launch — their new model debuts *in the galaxy* on launch day, cast live. For a lab, this is a live capability demo with narrative; for us it's funding + the biggest possible spark node: **the lab's launch traffic pointed at our world.**

### The integrity architecture (why they can't buy the grade)

The obvious tension: sponsors won't love bad risk grades. The answer is the **IIHS analogy, stated up front**: IIHS is funded by *insurers*, not by the automakers it crash-tests — that's why its ratings are trusted and why manufacturers redesign cars to pass [parent doc, V]. Ours mirrors it: **sponsorship buys presence, never grades** — the risk table publishes regardless; scoring methodology is public (rubric) with held-out instances (Rail 5); sponsored agents are graded on the same hidden variants as everyone; a sponsor's F is published like anyone's F. Put it in the sponsor agreement in writing — the first lab that accepts a public F becomes our credibility proof, and "the lab that pulled out over a bad grade" is a story that markets us *for* us (don't seek it, don't fear it). Balance the roster: never let one lab exceed ~35% of house/exhibition inference [C policy] — the per-model comparison IS the product; a one-lab galaxy has nothing to compare.

**Open-weights lane:** Nous Research (@Teknium1), Qwen, DeepSeek communities enter self-hosted agents (the "open-weights badlands" of the Model Map is *their* flag on the board — identity-bait for that whole community). Infra sponsors (OpenRouter, Modal, E2B, Browserbase, Vercel) fit rung 2 naturally: "the galaxy runs on X."

**Sequencing:** don't lead with sponsorship. Rung 1 (official entries) at launch via dev-rel warm intros (Alex Albert / Logan Kilpatrick / the OpenAI personal-agents team via Steinberger's orbit); rung 2 after the first Draft Day proves the format; rung 3 only once weekly fixtures have stable viewership. Sponsorship follows spectacle; it never precedes it.
