# SCALE — one world, thousands of agents, and what a season costs (Season 1, 2026-10-01)

*Branch `season1-scale`. Every figure here was measured by a command in this branch; none is
projected from a smaller run unless it says so. The instruments are committed:
`engine/scripts/population-scale.ts` (one Reckoning at 300 · 1,000 · 3,000 principals) and the CI-sized
`engine/test/sim/population-scale.spec.ts`; the age runs used throwaway scripts whose method is stated
in §5.*

---

## 0. The answer

- **Per tick, the engine now holds 3,000 principals with room to spare**, and its books are dimensioned
  for 10,000. A steady tick at 3,000 is a fifth to a half of a second of CPU (depending on the
  machine's load) against a 300-second production tick; the Reckoning batch one to two seconds; every
  principal observing on the tick after the Reckoning about **13 s of one core, down from ~7 minutes**
  (extrapolated before; measured after).
- **Three things were broken at scale and are fixed:** the observation burst was O(P²) (§3); eight
  books bound below the population by arrival order, two of them by halting the world (§4); and the
  tick grew with the world's **age** — INV-1, INV-7 and the state hash re-read the whole record every
  tick, and adopting a checkpoint of any world past ~150,000 postings threw (§5).
- **What binds now is memory over a season, not CPU.** The posting log, the batch log and the lots
  live in memory for the world's whole life and grow ~0.5–0.8 KB per principal per tick. Over Season
  1's 14 Reckonings that is ~1.4 GB of heap at 500 principals, ~2.1 GB at 1,000, ~5.4 GB at 3,000,
  against the 4 GiB slice the repo ships.
- **Recommended launch capacity: 500 seats** (`DEFAULT_SEATS`, raised by `COMPACT_SEATS`) on the
  shipped slice; 1,000 on an 8 GiB slice; 3,000 needs ~16 GiB or the in-memory journal paged out. The
  world ceiling (`MAX_PRINCIPALS`) is 10,000 and no book binds below it.
- **The map grows** (§6): a new constellation opens at the Reckoning when the qualified population —
  capitalised, non-related, with capital at stake, never headcount — reaches 8 per system. Sixty idle
  identities open nothing.

## 1. Method

**The population.** `HeuristicCast` with a synthetic roster (`s0001`…) and §12.4's wake cadence:
member *i* decides on ticks where `(tick + i) % 18 === 0`, so about P/18 principals decide each tick,
the shape of a world of self-hosted agents seen from the server. Deterministic: each run ends on a
`state_hash`, and two runs of one population agree.

**One Reckoning.** 300 ticks from genesis, crossing the settlement at tick 287; the observation burst
is measured on tick 288 — when every agent wakes to read what happened — for every principal (the
before-rows at 3,000 sample 300 and extrapolate, because the old path cost ~0.15 s each).

**Before** is the base commit (`c771ece`) with the new harness copied in; **after** is this branch.

**The clock.** This session shared the machine with other lanes' full test suites, at load averages
from 7 to over 300. The wall clock counted their time, so every figure is **process CPU time**
(`--clock cpu`), and the first before/after pairs were interleaved population by population so both
halves saw the same load. CPU time still includes V8's parallel GC threads, so it reads slightly above
an idle machine's wall clock. Apple M4 Pro, 14 cores, Node 22.22. Production is a 4-vCPU, 4 GiB
systemd slice (`deploy/agenteve.service`): read these as one core of a faster machine.

**Nothing here moved the world where no cap binds and nothing grows.** With `RULES_VERSION` set back

What the table says beyond the speed-ups:

- **The 3,000-principal world could not draw its own Reckoning.** 100+ WORKS on one MARCHES system
  split a yield that truncated to a share of 0 each, every one labelled `EXTRACTING`, and
  `assertFrameBudgets` refused the frame. A WORKS whose share truncates to zero now reads
  `CROWDED OUT`; the frame renders.
- **The 1,000- and 3,000-principal worlds are different worlds before and after**, and that is a

- The engine's other per-tick terms are not the problem at this size; the cast's `decide` is (2 s a
  tick at 3,000 before, 0.4 s after the shared views), and it runs on agents' own machines in
  production, so it is reported apart and never in the tick.

## 3. The burst: what was quadratic, and serialize-once

SPEC §15.5 assumed observation serialization was the O(P × size) term and prescribed shared fragments.
Measuring found the term was **O(P²) and it was the BUILD**: a handful of world-wide reads ran inside
every principal's observation. `levyCarryRows` walked the constellation roll twice per observation;
`exposureBand` summed every principal's EXPOSURE; `raidsFor` re-solved every standoff four times;
`route` re-ran Dijkstra on a map that had not changed; `haulQuotes` sorted every hand in the world to
find three. ~85% of an observation's bytes are the reader's own, so sharing the serialization alone
would have bought little.

Two mechanisms (commit `1e42f85`):

1. **Shared computation** — `Runtime.perEpoch` caches each world-wide view once per read epoch (keyed
   on the state hash, the tick and enrolments; bypassed while a tick resolves). Its values are frozen
   and registered. The first reader of an epoch pays for the shared views (0.1–0.4 s at 3,000); every
   reader after it pays ~2–5 ms.
2. **Shared serialization** — `api/fragments.ts` serializes each registered view once by identity and
   splices it into the per-principal envelope. The bytes are exactly
   `JSON.stringify({ok, observation})`: `test/api/fragments.spec.ts` builds every principal's
   observation in a populated world both ways — spliced, and the reference path with every cache
   disabled — mid-Reckoning, across submissions, across an enrolment landing between ticks and on the
   tick after settlement, and asserts byte identity, plus the fallback when a payload string equals a
   token.

## 4. The caps, and the seats

`MAX_PRINCIPALS` was 300 and doubled as the seat count; books were sized by hand, mostly as flat
numbers. The audit read every `export const MAX_*` in `src/` (146 of them) against the population it
caps. **`test/core/capacity.spec.ts` now classifies every one** — ceiling, population, map, per-row,
legibility, retention, drama, host, flagged — and fails on a cap it has never seen, so the next one is
argued before it ships. Population books must clear `per × MAX_PRINCIPALS`, map books
`per × MAX_MAP_SYSTEMS`, and legibility budgets must stay flat (§17's seven labels are a viewer's
limit). Mutation-checked: a flat `MAX_SYNDICATES = 64` and an unclassified `MAX_ANYTHING` are both RED.

| book | was | now | how it bound |
|---|---|---|---|
| submission window (`MAX_QUEUED_ACTIONS`) | 4,096 | 320,000 | its note said "generous at 300"; 300 × 32 is 9,600, so 128 loaded principals filled it and the rest were refused by arrival order |
| fill queue (`MAX_PENDING_FILLS`) | 512 | 40,000 | `fill_role` requests in one tick |
| elections · in flight | 2,048 · 1,024 | 30,000 · 320,000 | one per filled role (a role is a hand) · one per queued `elect` |
| grants · spends · releases | 4,096 · 16,384 · 16,384 | 80,000 · 320,000 · 320,000 | **lifetime** books; the spend journal is guarded to refuse rather than halt |
| dossiers | 4,096 | 40,000 | lifetime |
| open orders | 2,048 | 240,000 | 86 principals at their own cap of 24 |
| Charge ballots | 512 | 40,000 | **threw inside the tick — a halt** |
| syndicates | 64 | 10,000 | measured: 207 founded at 1,000 principals, 670 at 3,000 |
| covers · indemnities | 512 · 1,024 | 320,000 · 640,000 | every payer at its own cap |
| per-tick event buffer | 8,192 | 160,000 | **threw — a halt**; the settlement tick wrote 220 / 832 / 2,403 rows at 300 / 1,000 / 3,000, so it bound near 10,000 |
| Levy ballots | 1,200 (derived from 300) | 40,000 | measured 1,601 at 1,000 and 4,897 at 3,000 |
| claims · THE VERGE | 64 · 32 | 1,312 · 1,312 | one per system, and the map now grows |
| idempotency keys (host) | 20,000 | 64 × seats | evicted live replays once ~300 principals held their allowance |

**Seats are the host's, the ceiling is the world's.** `core/time.ts:MAX_PRINCIPALS` is now the world
ceiling (10,000) that every book derives from; `api/seats.ts:DEFAULT_SEATS` (500, §7) is how many the
host serves, read from `COMPACT_SEATS` by `seatCapacityFrom`, which refuses anything that is not a
positive integer or that exceeds the ceiling — a seat the books were not sized for is the bug. No cap
costs memory until rows exist: no book is preallocated.

`Runtime.capPressure` meters every population-sized book against its cap, and the harness prints the
fullest by **peak** (sampled after the submissions land and after the tick, because the window is
empty at every tick's end and fullest just before one). At 3,000 the fullest book was the Levy ballot
book at 12% of its cap.

**Flagged, not changed** (each argued in the capacity table):

- the **parley book** is a ring that refuses once full, so the 513th parley *of the world's life* is
  refused at any population — the parley lane's;
- the **grant journals are lifetime** until expired-grant pruning lands;
- the **drama budgets are galaxy-wide** — six live raids, four wars, four battles — so at thousands of
  principals agent demands queue behind each other; per-constellation budgets would move frame budgets
  too;
- **tribute lines** (the Levy's pixel signature) drop past 512 assessed principals; an aggregate is a
  design call;
- the **fill ring** (2,048) outruns the 48-tick valuation window past ~40 fills a tick; measured volume
  at 3,000 is far below it.

## 5. Age: what a season costs

Every earlier measurement stopped at one Reckoning. Running worlds for 3–12 Reckonings (heap read after
a forced GC every 48–288 ticks) showed the tick growing with the world's **age**:

| 1,000 principals, steady tick (CPU ms) | age 287 | age 575 | age 863 | Reckoning at 863 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| base | 420 | 1,440 | 2,289 | 4,831 |
| INV-1 incremental + lot list cached | 292 | 869 | 1,111 | 2,252 |
| **all of §5's fixes** | **170** | **509** | **680** | **1,170** |

Profiles at ages 400 and 1,000 named the terms, and commit `cea8448` fixes each without moving a
hash (the 300- and 1,000-principal runs end on the same `state_hash` before and after it):

- **INV-1 re-checked every batch ever applied, every tick close.** A batch's verdict is fixed once
  verified, so the verified prefix is carried, re-identified at each call by length and by the
  **identity** of the boundary batch — a truncation (an aborted tick) or a rehydrate that rebuilt the
  log with the same length and ids re-checks in full. Four mutations, four REDs.
- **INV-7's Mirrors 3 and 4 re-added every batch's supply legs every tick** — the same carried prefix;
  every comparison still runs against every account every tick.
- **`Ledger.allLots()` sorted every lot on every call** from six readers a tick; cached, dropped at the
  four sites that change the lot set.
- **The state hash materialised the whole state as one string** at every tick close. One token writer
  now serves `canonicalize` (joined) and `canonicalHash` (64K chunks into SHA-256), and a per-depth
  shape cache sorts each run of identical keys once: −47% at 400,000 lots, and no state-sized
  transient. Digests unchanged against the golden files, the recorded defects, and the previous
  writer copied verbatim into `test/core/canonical-stream.test.ts`.
- **Latent: adopting a checkpoint of a big world threw.** `hydrateAppendOnly` used `push(...rows)`,
  which V8 refuses past ~150,000 arguments; a 1,000-principal world passes that by tick ~400, and a
  failed adoption replays from genesis. Fixed, and `hydrate-at-scale.test.ts` hydrates 600,000.

**What still grows, and is the binding constraint:**

| principals | heap growth per tick | per principal-tick | heap after 14 Reckonings (4,032 ticks) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 200 (12 Reckonings run) | 0.16 MB | 0.80 KB | ~0.7 GB |
| 500 (5 Reckonings run) | 0.35 MB | 0.70 KB | ~1.4 GB |
| 1,000 (3 Reckonings run) | 0.51 MB | 0.51 KB | ~2.1 GB |
| 3,000 | — | ~0.45 KB (assumed) | ~5.4 GB (projected) |

The heap is the in-memory journal — postings, batches, their event ids, and lots (a sampling heap
profile at 1,000 principals put ~130 MB of 198 MB in `Ledger.apply`, production and refining). RSS
measured at two to four times the live heap on this machine. The tick keeps growing too, but slowly:
~0.9 ms per tick of age at 1,000 principals, so a Season-1 finale at 1,000 is ~4 s a tick — about 1%
of a production tick. The lots are the remaining CPU term: they are never merged (P16 keeps
provenance per lot; a transfer opens a new lot at the destination), so they grow ~0.1–0.2 per
principal per tick, and the state hash is O(lots) — ~2 µs per lot after the fixes.

**Two engineering items lift the wall; neither is done here, both need an owner's call:**

1. **Page the journal out of memory.** Every in-process reader of the posting and batch logs is now
   incremental or bounded except two: `postingsFor`, which scans the whole posting log per call (MKT-4
   per fill leg, the raid and campaign A5′ checks per seized lot), and the journal's per-tick
   `extractTick`, which filters the whole batch log by tick (one comparison a row, so cheap, but
   O(history)). With those two bounded by tick — the batch log is append-only in time, which
   `hydrateAppendOnly` already enforces — only the unverified tail needs to be resident.
2. **Consolidate lots** (at a Reckoning sweep, keyed on every field a rule reads). It interacts with
   `MAX_SEIZE_LOTS` — a raid seizes up to N *lots*, so fewer, larger lots make raids take more — which
   makes it a balance decision, not a refactor.

## 6. The map grows (SPEC §4.2)

*"Growth opens new constellations, gated on bonded, capitalised, non-related population — never raw
headcount, which would let 60 bots mint a fresh resource supply. … the generator is reserved."* Commit
`cc50350` spends it (`RULES_VERSION` 41).

**The gate** (`world/growth.ts`). At each settlement the region counts principals that are all three
at once, each read off a quantity the engine already hardened:

- **capitalised** — D7's `freeCash` ≥ 20,000 (one night's Levy): currency it was paid, never the
  starter stake, so a fresh identity reads zero by construction;
- **non-related** — `standing.distinctCounterparties` ≥ 1: an elective promise honoured to a principal
  that is not itself (self-dealing earns zero);
- **bonded**, read as *capital at stake this Reckoning* — the EXPOSURE high-water mark (a stake locked
  into a role) or a posted bond. Not §6.4's `BONDED` tier, which needs sureties; the agent-facing text
  says "AT STAKE" rather than reuse that tier's name (hard rule 4).

When the count reaches **8 per system the region already has** (§4.2's stage floor: 240 on the launch
map), **one** constellation opens. Headcount is not an input: `growth-gate.spec.ts` seats sixty fresh
identities under a threshold sixty heads would clear and runs a whole Reckoning — zero qualified, no
constellation — and shows each clause necessary.

**The geography.** Two COMMONS systems of its own and 4–6 MARCHES, joined to the map by **one** INTER
lane, so it is a STRAIT from the day it opens. The anchor is chosen on the larger side of every bridge
— exactly where hanging a subtree moves no existing strait — and `assertGrowthStructure` re-derives
every pre-existing strait, lane and system and halts on a difference. Lodes are allocated per grown
constellation, so the launch map's yields are byte-identical and every tier is still conserved. No
INTER lane touches a COMMONS; the FRONTIER is never extended. Deterministic: the n-th constellation is
drawn from `map:<seed>`'s `growth:<n>` stream, the same on every host whatever tick it opened at;
`regrowMap` rebuilds it on restore. At most one a Reckoning, ceiling 160 (the map then stages 10,000
principals at the floor). Swept over 12 seeds × 8 openings.

**Seating.** Newcomers go to the least-occupied COMMONS system, ties to the newest enclave, so a fresh
Commons fills until it is level with the old ones.

**What renders (A13 — THE RISE).** `observe` carries `header.growth` (no key spent; 11 of 11) — the
count, the threshold, the newest constellation and its gate — as a shared fragment, with one line of
rule and the `agent.md` section's name (the full rule is ~1 KB, and static prose in every observation
is what once crowded the house cast's affordances out of its prompt). The frame carries the same count
from the same builder, plus the rule and every opened constellation. On the client each grown
constellation is a haloed satellite enclave on a ring outside the core, amber on the night it opens,
with the meter toward the next one; the drill-down and the locator use the same layout. One ring holds
~30 satellites legibly; a second ring is the next step if a season ever needs it.

**What would make it open.** On the launch map, 240 principals must each have been paid 20,000, kept
an elective promise to someone else and have capital at risk on the same night. At 500 seats that is
half the world. That is deliberate (A15) and it is the owner's number to move: 500 principals on 30
systems is ~17 a stage, inside §4.2's 8–20, so the launch map does not need to grow to stay legible at
the recommended seats.

## 7. Recommended launch capacity

**`DEFAULT_SEATS = 500`; `MAX_PRINCIPALS = 10,000`.**

| seats | one Reckoning in: steady tick · Reckoning · burst | steady tick at the 14th Reckoning | heap after 14 Reckonings | slice it needs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 500 | 43 ms · 133 ms · ~1 s | ~1 s | ~1.4 GB | the shipped 4 GiB |
| 1,000 | 69 ms · 243 ms · 2.0 s | ~4 s | ~2.1 GB | 8 GiB, `--max-old-space-size=6144` |
| 3,000 | 211 ms · 832 ms · 13.3 s | ~10 s (projected) | ~5.4 GB (projected) | ~16 GiB, or §5's journal paging |

The 500 row is the five-Reckoning age run's first Reckoning; the 14th-Reckoning ticks extrapolate each
age run's measured slope (0.25, 0.9 ms per tick of age at 500 and 1,000) and are the least certain
cells here. All of them are a few percent of a 300-second production tick.

Five hundred because it is the largest round number whose season fits the slice the repo ships with
room for agents busier than the synthetic cast, and because it is what the launch map stages at
8–20 principals a system. `COMPACT_SEATS` raises it with no rules change.

## 8. Owner decisions

1. **The Season 1 seat count and the slice.** 500 on 4 GiB is safe; 1,000 needs 8 GiB; thousands need
   16 GiB or the journal work. Set `--max-old-space-size` explicitly in the unit either way: V8's
   default heap limit derives from the memory it sees, which inside a cgroup may be a fraction of
   `MemoryMax`. Verify on the host with `node -e "console.log(v8.getHeapStatistics().heap_size_limit)"`
   under the unit.
2. **"Bonded" in the growth gate** reads as capital at stake (EXPOSURE peak or a posted bond), not
   §6.4's `BONDED` tier (bond plus sureties) — the tier would make growth wait on the scarcest status in
   the game.
3. **The growth numbers**: 8 qualified per system, 20,000 of paid capital, one constellation a
   Reckoning, ceiling 160 — all *(calibrate)*.
4. **Journal paging and lot consolidation** (§5) — the two items between this engine and thousands of
   principals for a whole season on a small box. Lot consolidation changes what a raid can seize.
5. **Drama budgets per constellation** rather than galaxy-wide, and an **aggregate tribute line** past
   512 assessed principals.
6. **The capital clause inherits D7's one leak (A15).** D7 lets the endowment fund a principal's own
   ventures, and a venture's wage is its role-holder's earnings — so a starter stake (250,000) can
   become other principals' `freeCash` by way of real, performed roles, about a dozen qualifications'
   worth per stake. A coordinated set of *active* identities can therefore qualify each other: each
   must still hold a role that settles, keep an elective promise to a distinct principal and have
   capital at stake on the night, and on the launch map that is 240 of them for one constellation,
   with the bar rising ~56 per constellation opened. Idle identities, enrolment and headcount open
   nothing. Closing the leak belongs to D7 — withhold credit on wages an endowment funded — not to the
   gate, and it would close the same route to the market BID that D7 exists to guard.

## 10. After the Season 1 merge (branch `season1`)

The other three lanes were cut from the same base without `perEpoch` or the fragments, and merging them
put an O(P) term back into every observation through doors this lane never saw: contact's reach rungs and
directory walked every seated principal and the whole offer book per reader (reach was asked three times an
observation), the season block filtered every venture ever minted three times an observation, and stakes'
create rows minted and hashed ~18 identical probe ventures. Measured on the merge commit, same load, back to
back: one observation **1.94 → 3.23 ms** at 1,000 principals and the burst **1.9 → 3.2 s**.

Each is now a per-epoch read under §3's rule (a pure function of hashed state, recomputed inside a tick):
seated principals by constellation and the offer book by author as shared indexes; reach, the parley
block and the season block memoised per reader; each constellation's directory ranked once and every
reader served from it (`say/directory.ts:directoryOf`); the grand ventures indexed off the venture book's
own cached order; the probe venture memoised for the tick; contact's venture ties and the seal gate read
`venturesFor`. `test/api/fragments.spec.ts` still asserts the served bytes equal the reference build, and a
12-member season replayed on the final code ends on the same `state_hash` as before these reads moved.
