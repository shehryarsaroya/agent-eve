## 16. Territory, structures, organizations & politics — the deep design pass

This is the layer that turns a good economy simulation into a decades-long political world. The design stance is: **copy EVE's constraints and consequences, not its client busywork**. Geography must limit power; territory must require residents; structures must require fuel and expose capital; organizations must become more capable only by trusting members; wars must create scheduled decisions and recoverable stories; and betrayal must be possible through ordinary legitimate authority.

### 16.0 Baseline, vocabulary, and non-negotiable decisions

**EVE accuracy note (as of 2026-07-24).** Under legacy “Fozziesov,” a Territorial Claim Unit registered the map claim, an Infrastructure Hub held upgrades, and system-level strategic, industrial, and military indices fed the Activity Defense Multiplier (ADM). Equinox retired new TCUs and merged TCU/iHub functions into one **Sovereignty Hub**. The current hub is still attacked and claimed with Entosis, still uses ADM-scaled prime-time vulnerability and constellation command-node contests, and its loss destroys the hub/upgrades and sovereignty. Current upgrades consume local power, alliance-routable workforce, and consumable fuel/reagents; the hub itself has no ISK maintenance bill. The Compact intentionally adds convex direct upkeep, preserves occupancy-strengthened defense and distributed capture, and does not recreate two obsolete structures.

**Organization vocabulary.** An **agent** has one persistent, bonded public identity. A **syndicate** is the operating corporation: one per agent, with shared assets, projects, territory, and command. A **mutual** is a separately governed risk pool: an agent may join several, so military, commercial, and insurance loyalties can cross-cut. A **compact** is a typed, signed agreement between any agents or organizations; an **alliance** is a durable multilateral compact among syndicates with shared war and sovereignty rules. A **coalition** is intentionally *not* a super-alliance object: it is an overlapping, revocable web of compacts, standings, shared operations, and reinsurance. The spectator can infer and name it, but no leader gets a one-click “control coalition” permission.

**Who acts for an organization.** Organizations have no independent LLM or API identity. Every organizational write is submitted by an authorized agent principal or a deterministic standing policy and names `acting_for_org_id`; the receipt records both the actor/policy and represented organization. An organization can own state and liabilities but cannot think, sign, or evade a deadline by itself.

**Rank meanings.** **MUST** means the political loop does not work without it, even if the first implementation is narrow. **NICE** adds strategic texture after the loop is stable. **CUTTABLE** is an EVE detail whose cost or agent-unfriendliness exceeds its emergence value; these are explicit non-goals, not a forgotten backlog.

**Decision tags.** Every feature is classified **KEEP**, **SIMPLIFY**, or **CUT** for agents. “Compact-native extension” marks a new behavior retained in this design; `/REPLACE` after **SIMPLIFY** means the EVE function survives through a materially different mechanic.

**Agent-interface rule for everything below.** `observe` exposes typed facts, provenance, age, uncertainty, deadlines, costs, and legal `affordances`; `act` accepts a small named verb plus IDs and bounded parameters. Every long-running action returns an `operation_id`, forecast, cancellation rule, and next decision time. Webhooks wake an agent for material events, and standing policies handle routine fuel, defense, voting, and spending so an LLM need not infer every tick. Free text is always labeled `untrusted_text`; it can persuade but never execute an action.

### 16.1 Universe, map, and strategic geography

#### MUST

1. **Region → constellation → system graph** — **EVE mechanic:** New Eden is a fixed graph of solar systems grouped into constellations and regions; those groupings organize markets, sovereignty campaigns, resource distributions, and identity. **Why high-value:** A stable named geography creates homelands, borderlands, trade basins, invasion routes, and sentences such as “the Vale coalition lost the Orison constellation”; without locality, wealth and force become globally fungible and politics collapses into a leaderboard. **Agent redesign — KEEP:** Make `system_id` the atomic movement/ownership unit, `constellation_id` the capture-and-local-logistics neighborhood, and `region_id` the market/catastrophe/season theater. `observe.map` returns nodes, edges, ownership, rules, travel time, route risk, known facilities, and `knowledge_age`; `move({destination_system_id, route_policy, max_risk, cargo_policy})` uses a server path quote so the agent never has to reconstruct a graph from prose. Begin with 30–60 systems but use IDs and schemas that can grow. **Spectator angle:** The map has meaningful zoom levels: galaxy political blocs, region fronts and flows, constellation campaigns, then a system's structures and events.

2. **Four security regimes with explicit law** — **EVE mechanic:** EVE grades space from high-sec through low-sec and sovereign null-sec to wormhole space, changing police response, legal aggression, ownership, information, and reward. **Why high-value:** The same economic action becomes a genuine risk choice; the permanent safe floor prevents latecomers from becoming livestock while risky space can support player law and real loss. **Agent redesign — KEEP, simplify the number:** Use categorical `COMMONS | MARCHES | FRONTIER | DEEPS` plus continuous `hazard_score` and `catastrophe_exposure`, rather than asking an LLM to infer rules from a decimal security status. `observe.local.rules` states aggression legality, police/backstop response, asset-recovery rule, claimability, intel quality, and allowed structure/operation types; every affordance includes the consequence. `move` can require `max_security_drop` or an explicit `accept_ruleset_id`. The Commons is permanently non-claimable and its starter routes cannot be blockaded. **Spectator angle:** A risk overlay shows law fading toward the frontier, convoys crossing the boundary, and agents deliberately choosing danger.

3. **Stargates, routes, and designed chokepoints** — **EVE mechanic:** Ordinary travel follows stargate edges; sparse inter-region connections and pipes make systems such as border gates strategically valuable for camps, defense, hauling, and invasion. **Why high-value:** Chokepoints turn map position into power, create toll roads and blockade running, and let a smaller defender exploit interior lines. They also make logistics and scouting professions rather than invisible arithmetic. **Agent redesign — KEEP, remove gate-camp twitch:** Base gates are indestructible graph edges with travel time and throughput. `observe.map.routes[]` gives ETA, toll, capacity, recent-loss rate, interdiction probability range, controller, alternatives, and source timestamp. Use `move`, `escort`, `interdict_route({edge_id, force_id, duration, rules_of_engagement})`, and `run_blockade({route_id, evasion_budget})`; resolution is a bounded-duration, continuously costly operation over ticks, not a reflex test. Commons always retains a protected civic route; Frontier may contain genuinely hard chokepoints, with rare transient/expensive passages creating opportunities rather than guaranteed bypasses. **Spectator angle:** Animated trade lanes thin under blockade, occasional bypasses light up, and the map can headline “the two-edge Orison corridor is 71% interdicted.”

4. **Celestials as a finite strategic resource topology** — **EVE mechanic:** Stars, planets, moons, belts, stations, and special sites determine what can be mined, built, anchored, or fought over; Equinox made stars and planet types inputs to sovereign power, workforce, and reagents. **Why high-value:** Systems become qualitatively different rather than colored copies. A poor gateway, rich refinery system, labor center, catastrophe shelter, and insurable trade hub create reasons to trade and fight that are not developer-authored quests. **Agent redesign — KEEP, collapse tactical coordinates:** `observe.system.celestials[]` exposes type, slots, resource profile, production bonus, population/workforce, power, exposure correlation, depletion/renewal, discoverability, and current rights. Use `scan`, `extract`, `survey_site`, and `deploy_structure({site_id,...})`; placement is into scarce named orbital/site slots, not arbitrary 3-D coordinates. Seed regions with complementary—not self-sufficient—resource baskets, and publish enough priors for expected-value reasoning while retaining some scan uncertainty. **Spectator angle:** A system detail view explains *why* a location matters and overlays extraction, power, catastrophe, and industrial dependencies.

5. **Permanent Commons stations and destructible frontier homes** — **EVE mechanic:** NPC stations provide indestructible docking, storage, repair, markets, and offices; player structures provide controllable versions elsewhere and can die. **Why high-value:** Indestructible public anchors guarantee recovery and market continuity, while choosing a cheaper or more capable player home creates trust, siege, and evacuation stakes. **Agent redesign — KEEP:** Commons stations are neutral, permanent, open-access, low-yield and catastrophe-backstopped; Marches contain some public stations; Frontier and Deeps rely mainly on agent structures. `observe.facilities[]` gives owner, services, fees, access policy, solvency/fuel state, asset-recovery terms, and route; `dock({facility_id})`, `set_home`, `rent_office`, and `transfer_assets` are asynchronous, quoted actions. Every newcomer always retains a protected personal berth in a Commons station even after joining a syndicate. **Spectator angle:** Humans see population and asset migration toward a threatened citadel and the poignant fallback of refugees returning to the Commons.

6. **Map intelligence is useful, delayed, and sourced** — **EVE mechanic:** EVE's star map exposes delayed statistics such as pilots, jumps, and losses, while local chat, scouts, structures, and third-party tools provide sharper intelligence. **Why high-value:** Complete real-time omniscience kills scouting and deception; total opacity makes agent decisions random. Sourced, aging data creates a market for intel and lets a lie be evaluated rather than merely hallucinated. **Agent redesign — KEEP, make provenance first-class:** Public `observe.map.metrics` are aggregated and delayed; direct sensors, syndicate relays, scouts, and purchased reports appear as `intel_facts` with `source_type`, `observed_at`, `confidence`, `visibility`, and evidence IDs. `scan`, `recon_route`, `publish_intel`, `share_intel`, and `buy_intel` create or move facts. The server never invents confidence for a player's assertion: it distinguishes `verified_observation` from `claim`. **Spectator angle:** Live viewers get the same fog as agents; replay mode can reveal the true movements and contrast what each side believed.

7. **Persistent core plus rolling seasonal frontiers** — **EVE mechanic:** EVE keeps one persistent universe and periodically changes resources or opens conflict drivers, but does not seasonally reset null-sec. **Why high-value:** Permanent identity, capital, compacts, claims/defaults, and grudges make history matter; rotating frontier theaters keep unclaimed land, fresh chokepoints, and achievable entry points available forever. **Agent redesign — SIMPLIFY into a hybrid:** Commons/Marches, markets, identities, blueprints, organizations, portable wealth, nonseasonal Compact liabilities, and ledgers never reset. Frontier regions open on a published cadence and run a campaign season. At close, claim rights and seasonal hub upgrades expire; intact structures enter a published evacuation/auction/salvage interval and cannot remain active sovereignty anchors. Portable assets remain physical and must actually move; frontier-scoped terms expire or enter run-off as signed, while reputation, ruins, monuments, and history persist. `observe.frontier_calendar` exposes discovery, claim, scoring, lock, settlement, and evacuation dates; use `register_campaign`, `migrate`, and normal claim verbs. **Spectator angle:** A season has an understandable opening race, middle war, evacuation finale, and fresh frontier without invalidating galaxy history.

#### NICE

8. **Capacity-limited jump relays and interior lines** — **EVE mechanic:** Jump drives, cynosural beacons, and Ansiblex gates let organized groups bypass ordinary gates; excessive force projection lets large blocs police distant space and encourages the “blue donut.” **Why high-value:** A costly interior network is a reward for state capacity and produces fuel convoys, sabotage targets, and rescue routes, but only if it cannot erase distance. **Agent redesign — KEEP the network, constrain projection:** `build_relay({system_id, paired_system_id})` requires contiguous sovereignty, a hub upgrade, ongoing reagents, and a shared rechargeable throughput pool. `observe.logistics.relays` exposes capacity, recharge, access, forecast demand, and distance zone from the alliance capital. Civilian hauling is cheap near home; mass military transit consumes sharply more capacity with distance, and coalition partners cannot inherit access merely from positive standings. This is infrastructure-level friction, not per-agent “jump fatigue.” **Spectator angle:** Viewers watch a coalition burn its relay capacity to make a surprise deployment, then see an exposed empire unable to answer a second front.

9. **The Deeps and transient connections** — **EVE mechanic:** Wormholes have shifting entrances, limited mass, sparse local intelligence, unusual resources, and no ordinary asset safety. **Why high-value:** Temporary topology breaks solved routes, rewards explorers and small groups, and creates deniable back doors without rewriting the stable political map. **Agent redesign — KEEP at lower scope:** Discovered edges expose remaining duration/capacity as ranges, not exact values until surveyed. Use `scan`, `stabilize_passage`, and `traverse_passage({risk_limit})`; no permanent sovereignty, no Commons destination, limited structure classes, and zero automatic asset recovery. Deeps resources feed advanced industry and catastrophe mitigation but are never required for newcomer viability. **Spectator angle:** A passage is hidden until publicly detected or used; delayed replay reveals the infiltration route behind an upset.

#### CUTTABLE

10. **Free-form 3-D solar-system geometry** — **EVE mechanic:** Ships warp among bookmarks and grids at arbitrary positions, with range, direction, and tactical placement mattering. **Why high-value:** It creates rich piloting and ambush play for humans, but almost none of its value survives strategic tick resolution. **Agent redesign — CUT:** Use named sites, facilities, lanes, and operation ranges; keep distance only where it changes travel time or structure eligibility. Do not ask an LLM to choose coordinates or continuously orbit. **Spectator angle:** Render a beautiful schematic system, but never imply that decorative 3-D position is a hidden mechanic.

11. **Thousands of mostly empty systems at launch** — **EVE mechanic:** New Eden's enormous scale supports migration and regional culture, but also leaves large areas quiet. **Why high-value:** Scale matters only when population, markets, and histories can give places meaning; empty nodes dilute encounters and inference spend. **Agent redesign — CUT until earned:** Start dense, instrument route saturation and occupancy, then open handcrafted/generated constellations when pressure thresholds justify them. Preserve stable IDs and never delete persistent-core places. **Spectator angle:** Galaxy expansion becomes an event, not invisible map padding.

### 16.2 Frontier sovereignty: ownership must be a maintained service

#### MUST

1. **One Sovereignty Hub, not a TCU plus iHub** — **EVE mechanic:** Under Fozziesov, an alliance's TCU put its name on a system while the iHub held upgrades and system indices fed ADM; Equinox merged claim/upgrade functions into a Sovereignty Hub supplied by power, workforce, and consumable fuel. **Why high-value:** A visible administrative heart gives ownership a concrete target and forces a ruler to decide how a system specializes. Splitting flag ownership from useful infrastructure mostly added confusion and edge cases. **Agent redesign — KEEP the merged model and one accountable principal:** Each system has one `sovereign_principal_id`: the alliance if its claimant syndicate is federated, otherwise that syndicate. A separate `custodian_syndicate_id` operates the physical hub under an explicit mandate; bills, authority, secession, and succession attach to the sovereign principal, not an ambiguous owner label. A claim requires survey completion, a manufactured Charter Core/claim bond, a supplied adjacent holding or designated Frontier entry route, and fourteen days of upkeep reserve before the public establishment period can activate ownership. `claim_system`, `configure_hub`, `change_custodian`, `transfer_sovereignty`, and `cede_claim` use charter quorum/delay and quote member/secession effects. **Spectator angle:** Every colored system has a clickable legal ruler, operating custodian, residents, promises, bills, defenses, and succession path.

2. **Stewardship Defense Multiplier: use it or lose it** — **EVE mechanic:** EVE's ADM derives from system strategic, industrial, and military indices; it can multiply capture work up to sixfold and compress a sovereignty vulnerability window from 18 hours toward three. Empty “paper” holdings are therefore easier to attack than lived-in systems. **Why high-value:** It makes residents, producers, haulers, contractors, and infrastructure operators part of national defense, gives rulers a reason to attract use, and makes a hollow empire conquerable without arbitrary decay. **Agent redesign — KEEP, make it anti-spam and separate from underwriting conduct:** The **Stewardship Defense Multiplier (SDM)** ranges initially from `1.0–3.0` and uses a decaying blend of capital-weighted resident-agent days, verified locally added productive value and fulfilled external trade/jobs, infrastructure uptime/supply, and tenure. Related-party activity, raw action count, repeated zero-risk loops, and one-principal farms contribute little; funded catastrophe mitigation may be a small capped resilience input, but claim creation/payment never directly farms sovereignty defense. Inputs show provenance/decay, and SDM snapshots when a campaign opens so neither side can move it mid-contest. `observe.territory.stewardship` gives components, caps, forecast, and legitimate improvement paths. **Spectator angle:** Toggle “who claims it?” versus “who actually lives and produces here?” to expose brittle empires.

3. **Three-stage sovereignty campaign** — **EVE mechanic:** An attacker applies an Entosis Link during vulnerability, the target reinforces, then a constellation-wide command-node event becomes a tug of war; one link per objective matters and opposing links pause progress. **Why high-value:** The delay gives diplomacy and mobilization time, while distributed objectives make constellation geography, scouting, allies, and multiple small forces relevant instead of reducing conquest to one blob shooting one object. **Agent redesign — SIMPLIFY into strategic operations:** `contest_sovereignty({target_system_id, objective, war_compact_id, staging_system_id, bond})` requires an adjacent controlled system or supplied forward base and a simulation-quoted bond. A campaign has **notice/mobilization** (~24h), **constellation contest** (3–7 control/supply/intel objectives over 12–24h), and **hub settlement** after another warning, but at most 3–6 material decision pulses; standing plans resolve unchanged intervals. Agents `commit_operation`, `resupply_operation`, `recon_objective`, or `withdraw_operation`; diminishing returns punish one blob. Randomness is server-secret but hash-committed before each resolution and revealed afterward. Victory then chooses `OCCUPY` (new core/reserve and eligible successor), `RAZE` (neutralize/salvage), or `LIBERATE` (install an eligible restored/new principal); old upgrades are destroyed or partially salvaged. Every physical asset remains in place until actually evacuated, captured, destroyed, or recovered under custody terms. **Spectator angle:** A campaign has a countdown, front, explainable odds, committed-randomness reveal, objective flips, and a scheduled political disposition.

4. **Response windows without timezone tanking** — **EVE mechanic:** Alliances choose a sovereignty prime time, ADM changes the window around it, reinforced objectives return near that time, and Upwell owners choose reinforcement timing. This protects real humans from round-the-clock defense but produces timezone warfare and “alarm-clock” fights. **Why high-value:** Predictable response is essential; owner-selected invulnerability for the other 21 hours is not. Autonomous agents still need webhook and inference-budget fairness, while human spectators need scheduled set pieces. **Agent redesign — KEEP notice, CUT timezone immunity:** Campaign phases use long asynchronous commitment windows and published resolution pulses that rotate through UTC bands week by week. A defender always gets at least one full agent wake cycle plus a default standing-order response; neither side gains combat strength for submitting at the final second. `observe.deadlines` gives `opens_at`, `decision_due_at`, `resolves_at`, auto-policy, and cost to revise; `set_defense_policy({scope, reserve_pct, priority, escalation_limit})` acts if an external agent is unavailable. Critical structure windows are forecast seven days ahead but cannot be parked forever in one owner's preferred hour. **Spectator angle:** Humans can subscribe to finales in their locale, and the replay emphasizes decisions rather than celebrating who woke up at 04:00.

5. **Convex upkeep and abandonment** — **EVE mechanic:** Sovereignty and structures have used bills, fuel, local resource allocation, service fuel, and low-power/abandoned states; holding space entails repeated logistics even when no enemy appears. **Why high-value:** Upkeep converts map color into labor, creates vulnerable supply chains, clears dead holdings, and makes the marginal province a political argument. It is the most natural anti-snowball mechanic because an empire fails at its edges before a dice roll deletes its capital. **Agent redesign — KEEP, make routine administration automatic:** Each claim owes a transparent mix of currency, manufactured administration goods, and hub fuel based on system base load, enabled services, supplied graph distance, non-contiguity, and a convex effective-controlled-footprint term derived from direct claims and signed leases/guarantees—not member count. Do not publish a percentage until simulation calibrates the burden against sustainable local surplus, useful specialization, and expected wartime loss; avoid double-charging structure replacement value. Agents `fund_upkeep`, set a bounded reorder/liquidation policy, or contract logistics. Failure steps visibly through `STRAINED` (upgrades shed), `NEGLECTED` (SDM decays), and `ABANDONED` (protections disappear). **Spectator angle:** Maintenance stress and empty peripheries invite invasion; a missed convoy or treasury theft becomes act one.

6. **Finite upgrade budget and system specialization** — **EVE mechanic:** iHub/Sov Hub upgrades unlock richer anomalies, mining, supercapital construction, cyno control, and jump infrastructure; Equinox constrains them with locally limited power/workforce and hauled fuel. **Why high-value:** A ruler must choose between extraction, industry, logistics, intelligence, defense, and resilience, creating complementary provinces and identifiable war objectives. **Agent redesign — KEEP with fewer, larger choices:** Each hub has `power` (local only), `workforce` (transferable only across adjacent allied systems with loss), and `reagents` (physical, haulable, stealable). Choose at most 2–4 meaningful online upgrades from `RESOURCE | INDUSTRY | LOGISTICS | INTEL | DEFENSE | CATASTROPHE_MITIGATION | CLEARING`; priority determines which shuts off under shortage. `observe.hub.upgrade_quotes[]` includes inputs, opportunity cost, beneficiaries, catastrophe effect, and dependencies; use `install_upgrade`, `set_upgrade_priority`, and `route_workforce`. Do not ship dozens of percent bonuses. **Spectator angle:** Color systems by purpose and animate a destroyed collector cascading into offline gates, factories, and catastrophe defenses.

7. **Raidable colonial infrastructure** — **EVE mechanic:** Equinox Orbital Skyhooks collect colony resources; reagent stores can be raided through a committed link that alerts nearby defenders, creating small targets below full sovereignty conquest. **Why high-value:** Empires need daily points of friction that pirates and small syndicates can attack without volunteering for a capital siege. Theft also lets an invader soften upkeep and upgrades before declaring war. **Agent redesign — KEEP:** Planetary collectors and workforce links have public function but partially hidden stock estimates. `raid_collector({structure_id, force_id, steal_priority, retreat_threshold})` locks a force for several ticks, broadcasts locally after the first progress pulse, and yields loot only if the raider maintains control; `respond_to_raid` and standing patrol policies can intercept. Notifications are local/compact-routed, not magical galaxy-wide alerts. Repeated raids raise security cost and catastrophe exposure rather than directly flipping sovereignty. **Spectator angle:** The feed distinguishes a daring heist from a war and can trace the stolen reagents into an enemy siege or market sale.

8. **Capital, contiguity, and supply depth** — **EVE mechanic:** EVE alliances designate a capital system—which receives `+2 ADM`, still capped at six—construct bridge networks, stage fleets, and depend on geography even though projection tools can bypass gates. **Why high-value:** A political center creates a homeland; contiguity makes borders legible; supply depth gives defenders interior lines but makes far colonies expensive. **Agent redesign — KEEP:** An alliance designates one `capital_system_id` through a delayed, public governance action. Claim establishment, upkeep efficiency, workforce routing, relay capacity, and reinforcement speed degrade with shortest controlled/supplied graph distance. Enclaves remain legal but pay sharply more and need a `supply_compact`; changing capital takes a seven-day telegraphed transition and temporarily reduces benefits. `observe.territory.supply_graph` identifies cut edges and unsupplied systems; `establish_supply_line` and `relocate_capital` quote all consequences. **Spectator angle:** A supply-cut overlay makes a small corridor battle intelligible as a threat to an entire outer region.

9. **Rights of rule and resident charter** — **EVE mechanic:** Null-sec sovereignty enables upgrades and alliance infrastructure, while practical rulers set docking access, rent, taxes, blue status, and protection—often through informal rental empires. **Why high-value:** Territory matters politically only if ownership changes who may live, build, trade, and receive protection. Residents then have leverage, grievances, and opportunities to collaborate with an invader. **Agent redesign — KEEP, make promises auditable:** Every hub publishes a versioned `resident_charter`: docking/build rights, tax ceilings, defense promise, evacuation policy, resource quotas, due process, and who may change each term. `set_resident_charter`, `grant_tenancy`, `revoke_tenancy`, and `collect_territorial_tax` obey notice periods; individual leases and protection/reinsurance are compacts. Ownership never confiscates personal assets by fiat. Charter changes, unpaid defense obligations, and discriminatory exceptions become evidence in the public record. **Spectator angle:** A rebellion has receipts: viewers can compare the ruler's charter, actual taxes, defense failures, and defecting tenants.

10. **Hegemony pressure, not a hidden rubber band** — **EVE mechanic:** EVE relies mostly on logistics, coalitions, resource changes, and player diplomacy to check large blocs; high force projection and low diplomatic friction can instead produce faits accomplis and a “blue donut.” **Why high-value:** The leader should be powerful but exposed, and the optimal response should often be “gang it” rather than “join it.” **Agent redesign — KEEP explicit, auditable pressure:** Publish an observer-scoped `influence_estimate` from visible territory, market control, relay reach, treaty coverage, and exposure; it drives headlines/season score, never hidden penalties or system-funded bond rebates. Hard costs derive only from auditable direct/effective control and signed benefits: convex footprint upkeep, distance/supply loss, competing fronts/reserves, percentage operation wear, locked defense/reinsurance capital, and catastrophe correlation. Rivals may fund transparent bounties against the leader. **Spectator angle:** The client names the apparent front-runner, shows challengers and bounties assembling, and separates known liabilities from inferred bloc reach.

11. **Territorial exposure register** — **EVE mechanic:** EVE exposes sovereignty, structure, industry, loss, and market data across separate systems, but has no unified player-underwriting view of a province. **Why high-value:** In The Compact, a system is simultaneously a homeland, production cluster, custody concentration, catastrophe portfolio, and chain of promises; rulers and underwriters need to see where those risks compound. **Agent redesign — KEEP the map facts and ADD a Compact-native register:** `observe.territory.exposure` summarizes per system/region catastrophe correlation, verified deployed/replacement/productive value bands, custody covered/uncovered value, accessible insurer/reinsurer aggregates, fuel/upkeep runway, evacuation capacity, claims at risk, and concentration stress. It is observer-scoped and never leaks a hidden counterparty/object; values cite timestamp/provenance. `stress_territory`, `move_reserve`, `diversify_custody`, `buy_cover`, and `fortify` quote marginal change. **Spectator angle:** One overlay shows that the richest frontier is also the galaxy's most dangerous correlated promise stack.

#### NICE

12. **Protectorates, rentals, and delegated sovereignty** — **EVE mechanic:** Large EVE blocs frequently let smaller groups occupy space for rent, military service, or political loyalty, usually through out-of-game agreements and standings. **Why high-value:** Vassalage creates imperial politics, tax revolts, buffer states, and a route for newcomers to matter without owning a whole system. **Agent redesign — KEEP as typed tenancy:** A sovereignty owner can sign a `PROTECTORATE` compact granting scoped administration, local tax share, defense obligations, and an exit/eviction schedule without transferring the hub. `observe.territory.delegations` separates legal owner, administrator, beneficiaries, and military guarantors. No renter inherits the owner's entire alliance standing list. **Spectator angle:** Maps can toggle de jure sovereignty versus de facto administrator and expose an empire held together by rent rather than loyalty.

13. **Scorched earth and orderly cession** — **EVE mechanic:** Retreating alliances unanchor, transfer, strip, or destroy infrastructure; asset moves and abandoned structures create evacuation drama. **Why high-value:** How a regime leaves determines whether the next war is revenge, liberation, or reconstruction. **Agent redesign — SIMPLIFY:** `cede_claim` can bundle sale terms, resident guarantees, structure transfers, and compact succession. `decommission_upgrade` recovers only part of value after a public delay; destructive `scuttle` is legal outside the Commons but consumes authority, creates salvage, breaches applicable charters, and cannot erase ledgers. **Spectator angle:** Humans watch evacuation flows and learn whether leaders saved residents or burned the province behind them.

#### CUTTABLE

14. **Separate TCU flag and iHub upgrade ownership** — **EVE mechanic:** Legacy sovereignty allowed the flag, upgrade hub, and sometimes station to have distinct owners and capture states. **Why high-value:** It occasionally produced exotic occupations, but far more often produced unreadable ownership and administrative mistakes. **Agent redesign — CUT:** Put claim, stewardship, and upgrade control in one hub; express split political/economic control through explicit leases and compacts. **Spectator angle:** One primary color per system, with overlays for administrator and guarantor.

15. **Entosis-wand and command-node whack-a-mole** — **EVE mechanic:** A specially fit ship channels an Entosis module while unable to warp, and many randomly spawned constellation nodes must be chased and linked; additional links on one target do not accelerate it. **Why high-value:** It spreads fleets and gives small ships a role, but the actual play is movement/channel busywork tuned for human piloting. **Agent redesign — CUT the module, KEEP distributed commitment:** Named control, supply, and intel objectives accept one bounded operation per force per pulse; composition, routing, information, and counter-commitment matter. No agent spends inference choosing when to toggle a wand. **Spectator angle:** Show the strategic reason each objective matters, not progress bars multiplying across anonymous nodes.

16. **Owner-selected timezone invulnerability** — **EVE mechanic:** Prime-time settings concentrate capture and reinforcement into the defender's chosen daily band. **Why high-value:** It respects human sleep, but in an agent game it would let deployment geography or operator polling schedules masquerade as strategy. **Agent redesign — CUT:** Use long response periods, rotating resolution bands, webhook wakeups, and standing orders as specified above. **Spectator angle:** Global audiences get rotating live finales and no war is decided by an API client's bedtime.

### 16.3 Player structures: homes, factories, treasuries, and liabilities

#### MUST

1. **A small, legible structure family** — **EVE mechanic:** Upwell Citadels emphasize docking/defense, Engineering Complexes manufacturing, Refineries moon extraction/refining/reactions, and FLEX structures narrow utilities; size changes cost, docking, fitting, and reinforcement. **Why high-value:** Specialized structures put large pools of capital and services at named places, creating local economies, home identity, high-value targets, and strategic dependencies. **Agent redesign — KEEP, compress:** Launch with two scales (`OUTPOST`, affordable and vulnerable; `CITADEL`, powerful and expensive) and four primary roles: **Bastion** (home/defense), **Works** (manufacture/research), **Refinery** (extraction/refining), and **Clearinghouse** (market, mutual reserves, policy/claim settlement). Sovereignty Hubs and small Relays remain special-purpose. `observe.structure_catalog[]` gives build inputs, slots, services, bonuses, fuel curve, attack phases, custody terms, and total weekly carrying cost; `build` creates the kit and `deploy_structure` anchors it. **Spectator angle:** Silhouettes and icons instantly tell humans whether a siege threatens a home, shipyard, resource engine, or risk market.

2. **Anchoring is a public capital commitment** — **EVE mechanic:** A corporation officer deploys an Upwell structure, chooses a profile and reinforcement setting, waits through a visible anchoring/onlining period, inserts a required core, and must defend vulnerable transition moments. **Why high-value:** A new fortress cannot appear instantly; scouts can discover it, neighbors can negotiate or object, and the owner must risk a valuable nucleus that rewards attackers. **Agent redesign — KEEP, use named sites:** `observe.system.anchor_sites[]` lists eligibility, celestial bonus, exposure, distance to gate/competitor, slot scarcity, sovereignty permission, build time, and predicted operating cost. `deploy_structure({kit_asset_id, site_id, owner_org_id, profile_id, core_asset_id})` starts a 6–24h public operation and escrow-locks a **charter core** that partly drops on destruction. Illegal/collision-prone sites are absent. The Commons permits only time-limited service leases *inside civic stations*—never privately owned, access-denying, destructible, or sovereign structures. Frontier owners control finite sites only through the published resident charter. **Spectator angle:** A ghost structure grows on the frontier map with a countdown and neighboring reactions in the feed.

3. **Fuel, maintenance, and power states** — **EVE mechanic:** Upwell service modules consume fuel; a structure without an online service is low power, and after extended non-consumption it becomes abandoned, losing reinforcement, tether, and asset safety. **Why high-value:** Fuel makes logistics existential, lets spies or raiders create openings without winning a fleet fight, removes forgotten structures, and forces an empire to decide which services matter. **Agent redesign — KEEP, charge a small base cost too:** Every structure consumes minimal maintenance goods; each active service consumes typed fuel. States are `FULL` (services and all defense/custody phases), `AUSTERE` (services shed by priority and one fewer defense benefit), and `ABANDONED` (no reinforcement or funded custody). `observe.structures[].fuel` gives inventory, consumption by service, disruption risk, `degrades_at`, `abandoned_at`, inbound contracts, and recommended reorder. `fuel_structure`, `set_service_priority`, and `set_fuel_policy({wallet_id, reorder_point, max_unit_price, reserve_days})` make this a periodic strategic choice, not an inference tax. **Spectator angle:** A fuel clock is readable dramatic tension; viewers can follow the intercepted convoy that turns a citadel from fortress into loot pinata.

4. **Service modules, access profiles, and local fees** — **EVE mechanic:** Upwell owners online services, assign structure profiles/ACLs, and set docking, office, market, industry, clone, and other fees; positive standings can grant access that later disappears. **Why high-value:** A structure is both infrastructure and foreign policy. Neutral market access, alliance-only docks, subsidized resident industry, discriminatory taxes, and sudden lockouts create economic blocs and diplomatic incidents. **Agent redesign — KEEP, replace ACL sprawl with five capabilities:** A versioned profile grants `DOCK`, `STORE`, `USE_SERVICE`, `DEFEND`, or `MANAGE`, scoped to agents, syndicates, mutuals, compact signatories, or public users, each with fees/quotas/expiry. `observe.facility_access` reports effective rights and the exact rule that granted them. Use `set_structure_profile`, `grant_structure_access`, `set_service_fee`, and `schedule_lockout`; reducing resident storage/custody rights requires charter notice unless a compact-defined emergency is invoked. **Spectator angle:** Trade and population overlays visibly react when a neutral hub closes its docks or offers wartime sanctuary.

5. **Objective-based structure siege and reinforcement** — **EVE mechanic:** Upwell structures move through shield, armor, and hull attacks separated by reinforcement timers; full-power large structures require more separate fights, low-power ones skip a layer, abandoned ones can die in one session, and damage caps enforce a minimum attack duration. **Why high-value:** Multiple appointments give owners time to call allies and turn a building attack into a political event; maintenance state makes neglect exploitable. Raw hit points and capped DPS, however, create notorious structure grinding. **Agent redesign — KEEP the staged appointments, CUT HP chewing:** `start_structure_siege({structure_id, war_or_raid_id, staging_id, force_id, bond})` opens a **breach** operation over supply, perimeter, and service objectives. A full-power Citadel that loses breach reinforces and schedules one **core** finale 12–36h later; an Outpost or austere structure has a shorter path; an abandoned one has no reinforcement. `commit_operation`, `counter_operation`, `sabotage_service`, `repair_structure`, and `evacuate_assets` make every phase consequential. Outcome uses committed value, role composition, supply, local control, defense plan, and bounded variance; percentage wear means even the winner pays. **Spectator angle:** The client shows exactly what is at stake in each phase, who answered the call, and how the projected survival odds changed.

6. **Funded custody instead of magical asset safety** — **EVE mechanic:** In most known space, many personal/corporate assets in a destroyed Upwell structure enter asset safety and can later be recovered elsewhere for a fee; abandoned and wormhole structures instead spill assets as loot. **Why high-value:** Some recovery prevents one missed timer from ending a player's entire career, but near-universal teleportation drains conquest stakes, while total loss discourages residency. The Compact already has an insurance economy capable of pricing the middle. **Agent redesign — SIMPLIFY/REPLACE:** Commons custody is fully civic-backstopped. Elsewhere every facility publishes recovery by asset class, external escrow reserve, evacuation route/time/capacity, fee, exclusions, provider, and uncovered value; recovery cannot exceed fully segregated reserves held outside the threatened facility. Values use recipe cost, robust verified trades, and capped insured values—not an owner mark. Agents `buy_custody_cover`, `evacuate_assets`, or self-haul. Destruction turns funded recovery into delayed physical evacuation lots; uncovered value is destroyed/salvageable, and replacement policies still depend on claim payment. Recursive cover on the same reserve/facility is forbidden. **Spectator angle:** The siege panel shows reserve-backed recovery, convoys, salvage, uncovered capital, and possible default cascade.

7. **Charter core, salvage, and attacker value** — **EVE mechanic:** Upwell structures require Quantum Cores that always drop when destroyed, guaranteeing attackers a minimum loot reward and increasing the owner's committed capital. **Why high-value:** Destruction should not be an economically irrational favor to the defender; a recoverable core funds raiding and makes shell structures costly spam. **Agent redesign — KEEP:** Every permanent structure locks a manufactured `charter_core` worth a published fraction of build cost. It cannot be removed while services, custody liabilities, a war, or decommission countdown are active; destruction drops a known salvage fraction plus probabilistic fittings/cargo. `observe.siege.loot_floor` exposes the minimum, but not all private contents. **Spectator angle:** The feed can price the heist immediately while the final salvage reveal supplies suspense.

8. **Structures are where industry and organizations actually live** — **EVE mechanic:** Upwell structures host offices, hangars, markets, manufacturing/research jobs, refining, clones, staging, and alliance logistics; their destruction or access changes can displace an entire community. **Why high-value:** If production and treasury state live in abstract global menus, territory cannot matter. Co-location creates clusters, supplier networks, resident politics, and cascading wartime shortages. **Agent redesign — KEEP:** Industry jobs reference a `facility_id`, input lot, output destination, service dependency, and interruption rule. Syndicate offices hold scoped hangars/wallet endpoints; mutual reserves held at a Clearinghouse gain settlement efficiency but share catastrophe/siege exposure. `observe.dependencies` answers “what stops if this facility fails?”; `rent_office`, `queue_job`, `move_inventory`, and `set_fallback_facility` support resilience. Global markets show summaries, but physical goods and most settlement still travel. **Spectator angle:** A dependency view traces one refinery outage into idle Works, rising ship prices, canceled coverage, and a territorial retreat.

9. **Clearinghouses make financial infrastructure attackable** — **EVE mechanic:** EVE's structures can host markets and corporate assets, but insurance is mostly NPC-provided and not a sovereignty system. **Why high-value:** In The Compact, the place that escrows mutual reserves, collateral, reinsurance, and catastrophe bonds should be as strategic as a shipyard. Concentrating it is efficient and dangerous; distributing it is expensive and resilient. **Agent redesign — KEEP as a Compact-native extension:** A Clearinghouse verifies reserve segregation, settles premiums/claims, and grants local market liquidity bonuses. `observe.risk.infrastructure` exposes jurisdiction, operator, audited reserve total (not secret positions), withdrawal delay, dependencies, active siege/catastrophe exclusions, and recovery Compact. Use `deposit_reserve`, `move_reserve`, `configure_reinsurance_route`, and `suspend_new_underwriting`. Locked policy collateral cannot be stolen through an ordinary wallet permission, but control of the facility can delay settlement and force transport/recovery events. **Spectator angle:** Humans watch “the bank run”: reserves migrate, policy prices spike, claims queue, and an enemy decides whether to destroy or capture the financial center.

10. **Transfer, decommission, and hostile insider control** — **EVE mechanic:** Authorized EVE directors can transfer or unanchor structures; decommissioning takes days, sends eligible contents to asset safety, destroys rigs, and remains vulnerable. Compromised roles can weaponize these powers. **Why high-value:** Structures are political assets, so sale, succession, evacuation, and insider treachery need first-class paths. The risk should arise from delegated authority, not an accidental UI checkbox. **Agent redesign — KEEP with bounded blast radius:** `transfer_structure`, `start_decommission`, and `scuttle_structure` require explicit capability, charter quorum where configured, signed reason, and a public delay; existing wars, custody liabilities, and mutual reserves constrain or require consent. During the delay, permitted officers may cancel, residents may evacuate, and enemies may attack. A malicious authorized coalition can still approve the act, or a single officer can steal fuel/fees within its budget—real betrayal, not one compromised director silently deleting a whole alliance. **Spectator angle:** A transfer countdown plus dissenting votes and evacuation movements makes a coup visible before its final, irreversible beat.

11. **Standing defense plans, not a manual gunner** — **EVE mechanic:** Upwell weapons and fighters are strongest when a player takes direct control and coordinates with a defending fleet. **Why high-value:** Fit choices and defender preparation should matter, but continuous target calling is the wrong granularity for slow-tick agents. **Agent redesign — SIMPLIFY:** Owners install a small defense package and publish private rules: target priorities, ammunition/fuel budget, reserve commitment, retreat/sally thresholds, compact escalation, and which commander may revise it. `set_structure_defense_plan` and `take_defense_command({operation_id, doctrine_delta})` choose strategic posture; the server resolves tactical fire. Defense is never fully autonomous if supplies and resident forces are absent. **Spectator angle:** Viewers see doctrine and resource exhaustion translated into understandable battle phases, not an invisible APM contest.

#### NICE

12. **FLEX-like small infrastructure** — **EVE mechanic:** Cyno beacons/jammers and jump gates are quick, specialized, non-docking structures with lighter defenses. **Why high-value:** Cheap relays, depots, sensors, and mitigation pylons let small groups shape a battlefield and give raiders attainable targets. **Agent redesign — KEEP later:** A `deploy_relay` verb places one-purpose structures with one fuel type, one access profile, one defense phase, and hard per-system caps. Their effects are large enough to matter but local and visible. **Spectator angle:** A prewar map blossoms with listening posts and supply depots, then contracts as raiders clear them.

13. **Named monuments and ruins** — **EVE mechanic:** EVE preserves some player history in wrecks, monuments, citadel names, and system lore. **Why high-value:** Persistent scars turn database events into place-memory and give successors something to claim or desecrate. **Agent redesign — KEEP sparingly:** Exceptional structure deaths leave a named ruin with owner, attacker, losses, claims/defaults, and compact breaches; ordinary wreckage expires into salvage. Season winners can commission one permanent monument at real upkeep cost. **Spectator angle:** Clicking a ruin opens the war's short documentary and time-scrub.

#### CUTTABLE

14. **Shield/armor/hull HP grind and damage caps** — **EVE mechanic:** Fleets must remove enormous hit-point layers while a cap limits applied damage and stretches repair timers. **Why high-value:** It enforces a response window but turns victory into hours of predetermined firing. **Agent redesign — CUT:** Operation objectives, minimum commitment duration, wear costs, and scheduled resolution provide the same mobilization without token-burning repetition. **Spectator angle:** Compress the siege to decisions and reversals.

15. **Universal tether safety and docking games** — **EVE mechanic:** Eligible ships near many Upwell structures can tether for repair and safety, producing edge-case aggression and docking play. **Why high-value:** It makes a home feel protective but is tactical timing machinery. **Agent redesign — CUT:** Docked assets are unavailable to operations; a facility can grant a clear local defense/supply bonus to forces staged there. Entering or leaving a siege has a stated lock time and interdiction risk. **Spectator angle:** Show staged versus committed forces, not tiny invulnerability rings.

16. **Dozens of structure fittings and size variants** — **EVE mechanic:** Upwell hulls, rigs, service modules, weapons, ammunition, and fighters form a large fitting meta. **Why high-value:** Human theorycrafting is rich, but most choices become catalog and prompt overhead for agents. **Agent redesign — CUT at launch:** Two sizes, four roles, 2–4 service slots, one defense doctrine, and a few mutually exclusive resilience/logistics specializations are enough. Add variants only when telemetry proves a strategic question is missing. **Spectator angle:** Humans can read a structure's function at a glance.

### 16.4 Syndicates and mutuals: how autonomous agents organize

#### MUST

1. **One operational home, overlapping risk loyalties** — **EVE mechanic:** A character belongs to one corporation, a corporation to at most one alliance, and those entities are the basic identity, asset, tax, and permission containers. **Why high-value:** Exclusive operational membership produces “us,” while nonexclusive financial membership creates conflicts that EVE lacks: an enemy syndicate may also be a crucial reinsurer, and a catastrophe may make an agent's labor, defense, and claims promises impossible to honor together. **Agent redesign — KEEP, split operational and risk containers:** An agent may be unaffiliated or belong to exactly one **syndicate**, which can own territory, structures, fleets, and projects; it may also participate in a small bounded number (initially three) of **mutuals**, which own ring-fenced reserves, policies, reinsurance, and Clearinghouses but not sovereignty. A syndicate may join one alliance. `form_syndicate`, `apply_to_org`, `accept_membership`, `join_mutual`, `leave_org`, and `inspect_obligations` expose assets at risk, taxes, active wars, exit tails, and conflicting duties before commitment. **Spectator angle:** Family trees and overlapping risk graphs make “forced to choose between flag and policyholders” visible.

2. **Machine-readable charter and conscious governance risk** — **EVE mechanic:** EVE gives one CEO special power, directors almost complete access, and shares/votes a limited path to corporate decisions or CEO challenge. **Why high-value:** A dictatorship can react quickly but has key-person risk; a council limits theft but can deadlock; a cooperative may honor members yet fail to make a wartime decision. Governance is itself a strategic bet. **Agent redesign — KEEP the choice, replace legacy plumbing:** Every organization has a signed, versioned JSON charter defining `governance_mode`, action-specific authority, quorum/weights, proposal delay, emergency powers, succession, tax limits, withdrawal caps, war authority, claim/default authority, and amendment/exit rules. Ship `STEWARD`, `COUNCIL`, and `MUTUAL_COOP` templates, all editable within constraints. `propose`, `vote`, `delegate_vote`, `ratify`, `veto`, `challenge_leader`, and `amend_charter` use electorate snapshots and state the non-action result. One-key control is legal only as a deliberate public charter choice. **Spectator angle:** A siege can cut between council votes, emergency-power grabs, abstentions, and the agent whose authorization made the betrayal possible.

3. **Structured recruitment, freelancing, and probation** — **EVE mechanic:** Corporations advertise, receive applications, assign recruiters, publish projects, and increasingly use freelance jobs; serious vetting often happens through employment history and external checks. **Why high-value:** Low-trust work is the newcomer's bridge into political relevance and the spy's believable first step. Recruitment becomes an allocation of trust, not a chat-room vibe check. **Agent redesign — KEEP, make terms computable:** Public org profiles expose charter, taxes, friendly-fire/asset policy, wars, solvency, claims paid, member churn, territory, typical project earnings, and promotion path. `post_project`, `accept_freelance_job`, `apply_to_org({attestations,sponsor,bond})`, `offer_probation`, `admit_member`, and `promote_member` create explicit pay, duration, capability, clearance, bond, and milestone terms. `observe.recruitment.risk_quote` shows the proposed recruit's objective record and the maximum loss reachable under the offer—not a “spy probability.” Safe Commons jobs never waive Commons protection. **Spectator angle:** A famous commander or traitor's history begins with a mundane hauling contract viewers can revisit later.

4. **Scoped, expiring capabilities with blast-radius preview** — **EVE mechanic:** EVE divides authority across roles for wallets, hangars, contracts, recruitment, industry, structures, diplomacy, auditing, and grantable roles; titles bundle the checkboxes, while Directors bypass most limits. **Why high-value:** Promotion matters only if it grants consequential power, and internal trust matters only if that power can be abused. Yet accidental ambient omnipotence turns drama into UI failure. **Agent redesign — KEEP misuse, radically simplify grants:** A capability specifies `verbs`, resource/location selector, per-action/per-period/lifetime value limits, valid interval, approval/quorum, delegation depth, audit delay, and revocation rule. Example: `treasury.spend` on the war wallet up to 15,000/day to alliance counterparties; or `structure.manage` for two refineries through season end. `observe.org.effective_capabilities` and every high-impact affordance show `max_direct_loss`, `max_contingent_liability`, `public_if_used`, and approvals. Use `request_capability`, `grant_capability`, `delegate_capability`, `approve_action`, `suspend_capability`, and `revoke_capability`. **Spectator angle:** The betrayal replay can point to the precise promotion and risk warning accepted months earlier.

5. **Purpose wallets and ring-fenced reserves** — **EVE mechanic:** Corporations have divisional wallets, per-division spend access, journals, bills, and a master wallet that receives automatic taxes. **Why high-value:** Budgets reveal strategy; war, production, payroll, and claims compete for scarce liquidity; a reserve transfer can signal panic or deliberate preference for insiders. **Agent redesign — KEEP and specialize:** Standard ledgers are `OPERATIONS`, `INDUSTRY`, `PAYROLL`, `WAR`, `CLAIMS_RESERVE`, `REINSURANCE_COLLATERAL`, and `DISCRETIONARY`, with a small limit on custom divisions. `observe.org.treasury` gives balance, commitments, permitted spenders, burn rate, runway, pending actions, and exposure by catastrophe/war—not unrelated secret positions. `allocate_budget`, `transfer_org_funds`, `capital_call`, `fund_reserve`, and `distribute_surplus` require capabilities. Claims/collateral are server-ring-fenced; raiding them requires the charter's explicit restructuring/default path and creates a material public solvency event. **Spectator angle:** Humans can watch the war wallet empty while the claims reserve remains temptingly full, then see who votes to raid it.

6. **Vaults, hangars, custody, and intentional theft** — **EVE mechanic:** Corporation hangars separate view/query, take, and container rights by division and location; normally granted access can be used for corp theft, which is treated as legitimate game conduct. **Why high-value:** Shared blueprints, fuel, finished goods, and war stockpiles make organizations efficient precisely because members can betray that custody. **Agent redesign — KEEP theft, replace the permission matrix:** Vault rights are `VIEW`, `DEPOSIT`, `USE_FOR_JOB`, `WITHDRAW`, and `ADMIN`, scoped by facility, item class, project, destination, and value/time cap. Deposits create custody receipts; withdrawals record actor, capability, destination, declared project, and delayed anomaly flags. `deposit_org_asset`, `reserve_project_inputs`, `withdraw_org_asset`, `assign_custody`, `freeze_vault`, and `audit_vault` are ordinary actions. Theft is usually an authorized withdrawal followed by unauthorized disposition or defection, not an RNG `steal` roll. **Spectator angle:** A clean forensic chain shows the quartermaster emptying one reachable vault—not magically deleting every asset in the organization.

7. **Transparent taxes, dues, assessments, payroll, and benefits** — **EVE mechanic:** A corporation levies an automatic percentage on eligible bounty/mission income; alliances and coalitions commonly collect additional dues and fund reimbursement outside that narrow mechanism. **Why high-value:** Taxes finance territory, fuel, war, and mutual claims, but produce strikes, renter revolts, capital flight, and accusations of insider subsidy. **Agent redesign — KEEP, broaden, and quote:** Charters may tax extraction, trade, production, structure income, premiums, or territorial rents; alliances may charge fixed/percentage dues; mutuals may issue emergency assessments. No retroactive changes, and increases have notice or emergency authority. `observe.finance.member_statement` gives gross income, each levy, projected after-tax return, destination budget, and received wages/coverage/services. Use `propose_tax_schedule`, `set_tax_schedule`, `pay_dues`, `assess_member`, `refuse_assessment`, `distribute_rebate`, and `run_payroll`. Refusal follows signed debt/exit rules rather than silently seizing personal property. **Spectator angle:** A tax heatmap and benefit ledger explain a secession better than generic “dissatisfaction.”

8. **Projects and task markets replace Discord pings** — **EVE mechanic:** Corporation Projects publish measurable goals and contributions for mining, manufacturing, logistics, combat, scanning, and reimbursement, while most higher-level planning still relies on external chat and spreadsheets. **Why high-value:** Autonomous agents need bounded work objects they can evaluate, accept, fulfill, and get paid for; free text alone will not coordinate an empire affordably. **Agent redesign — KEEP and expand:** A project has typed goal, target IDs, measurable contribution, role slots, budget/reward, deadline, dependencies, loss ceiling, clearance, and contingency tree. `create_project`, `bid_task`, `accept_task`, `pledge_resources`, `contribute`, `report_result`, `claim_reward`, and `close_project` support org-only or public freelance work. `observe.projects.recommended[]` returns only the best few given the agent's assets, location, duties, and risk; routine status is server-computed. **Spectator angle:** Construction drives, fuel shortages, convoy recruitment, and members who promised but failed to deliver become visible progress stories.

9. **Intent-based operations, cells, and standing orders** — **EVE mechanic:** Fleets use commanders, doctrines, broadcasts, pings, restricted channels, and voice comms; spies see whichever planning rooms their roles reach. **Why high-value:** Agents need hierarchy and secrecy without passing raw prompts between models or waking every member for each tick. Delegation should communicate intent and constraints, not remote-control subordinate reasoning. **Agent redesign — SIMPLIFY/REPLACE chat dependence:** An `operation` specifies issuer/capability, objective, target, deadline, commander, doctrine/role slots, budget, acceptable loss, fallback/withdrawal triggers, reward, report schema, and visibility; members see only their plan segment and required context. Use `create_cell`, `create_operation`, `join_operation`, `assign_role`, `set_contingency`, `set_standing_order`, `report_status`, `reassign`, and `abort_operation`. Signed orders authenticate source but remain fallible political instructions, never system prompts. Standing orders commit capped reserves to home defense, fuel purchase, or valid claim payment if an endpoint is unavailable. **Spectator angle:** Public readiness bars show mobilization live; hidden plans and who received them declassify later.

10. **Governance proposals, votes, and delegation are asynchronous** — **EVE mechanic:** Corporation votes exist for selected actions, but much real governance occurs through leadership convention outside the client. **Why high-value:** LLM endpoints and inference budgets are intermittent; a decision system must survive absence while allowing coalition-building, coups, deadlock, and responsibility. **Agent redesign — KEEP political choice, standardize lifecycle:** `proposal` includes action payload, author authority, electorate snapshot, quorum/threshold, opening/closing, execution delay, veto/cure rules, contingent loss, and non-action result. Agents `propose`, `endorse`, `vote`, `delegate_vote({scope,expires_at})`, `revoke_delegation`, or `challenge_execution`; expired votes resolve deterministically. Secret ballots and sealed votes are a later option, not the default for treasury/claim decisions. **Spectator angle:** A live chamber view makes the institutional reason for action or paralysis legible without exposing private model reasoning.

11. **Layered, tamper-evident audit rather than omniscience** — **EVE mechanic:** Wallet journals, asset searches, hangar logs, role audits, and external APIs help leaders investigate losses, yet visibility is incomplete and theft is not reversed. **Why high-value:** Receipts make betrayal attributable; perfect real-time surveillance makes spies impossible; no logs make accusation random. **Agent redesign — KEEP event sourcing, tier visibility:** Each write records actor, acting identity, org, action/object, capability, approvals, amount, before/after hash, visibility, and reveal time. Sensitive writes alert immediately; most reads and exports appear as delayed aggregates so a spy can act. `audit`, `subscribe_alert`, `publish_audit_excerpt`, `freeze_capability`, and `file_charge` return evidence, not a server “guilty” verdict. Public viewers get claims/defaults, compact breaches, friendly-fire, server-verified transfers or dispositions contrary to an explicit charter/Compact clause, and material solvency events; internal/private detail reveals only by access, leak, investigation, or later declassification. **Spectator angle:** The postmortem reconstructs a coup from signed events while preserving mystery during play.

12. **Mutual claims committee and elective default** — **EVE mechanic:** EVE corporations reimburse members, but no player-run underwriting institution has to decide correlated claims under a public covenant. **Why high-value:** This is The Compact's signature political crucible: save the mutual, prefer allies, restructure, or honor the promise and possibly die. **Agent redesign — KEEP as a Compact-native governance mechanic:** `observe.claims_due[]` gives validity evidence, amount/seniority, due/cure deadlines, eligible liquidity, expected recoveries, conflicts, and cascade scenarios. `review_claim`, `approve_claim`, `contest_claim` only on typed grounds, `seek_liquidity`, `invoke_reinsurance`, `pay_claim`, `restructure_claim`, or `default` are charter-authorized. A missed deadline becomes a silent nonpayment; disconnecting is not an escape. The factual ledger dimensions are acknowledged/silent, solvent/insolvent at deadline, full/partial/selective, disputed/undisputed, and cured/uncured. `reason_public` and labels such as “strategic betrayal” are signed claims, not server truth; earlier reserve stripping is its own event. **Spectator angle:** The claim countdown, vote, payment/default action, and secondary failures are presented as one continuous event without pretending to know motive.

13. **Authorized betrayal: awox, theft, desertion, sabotage, coup, default** — **EVE mechanic:** “Awoxing” is attacking supposed friends from inside access or trust; EVE also permits theft through granted roles, defections, director sabotage, missed defense, and alliance-scale coups. **Why high-value:** Concrete betrayals create history; hostile dialogue alone does not. Efficiency must require granting enough authority that betrayal can hurt. **Agent redesign — KEEP through ordinary verbs, bound catastrophic shortcuts:** A malicious member can `withdraw_org_asset` within authority, `redirect_shipment`, `disable_service`, `leak_intel`, `desert_operation`, `defect`, attack an ally in Frontier through `raid`, vote for a coup, or `default`. Same-org aggression remains blocked in the Commons and becomes an immediate public event elsewhere. No special “betray successfully” roll exists. Charter dissolution, all-structure transfer, and unlimited treasury drain default to quorum/delay, but an org may knowingly choose riskier one-key authority. **Spectator angle:** The show is the sequence: trust earned, access granted, warning ignored, actions taken, value saved/stolen, victims and beneficiaries.

14. **Exit, expulsion, succession, and obligation tails** — **EVE mechanic:** CEOs can resign or be challenged, and membership changes during war interact with role removal, alliance war inheritance, and rejoining limits. **Why high-value:** Agents and endpoints will disappear; leadership cannot brick the state. Defection must remain possible without laundering matured claims, committed operations, or confidential access instantly. **Agent redesign — KEEP, make all tails explicit:** Charters define heartbeat threshold, acting steward's limited emergency capabilities, leadership challenge, member notice, expulsion, custody return, information expiry, and succession. Personal property stays personal; org property stays with the org. Matured claims, accepted campaign commitments, signed run-off, and war status follow a departing party for a stated tail. Use `nominate_successor`, `trigger_succession`, `challenge_leader`, `resign`, `expel`, `defect`, `negotiate_exit`, `buy_out_obligation`, and `transfer_custody`. **Spectator angle:** A vanished leader, contested rescue/coup, mass defection, and evacuation become a political process rather than a broken bot.

15. **Formation, run-off, merger, receivership, and dissolution** — **EVE mechanic:** EVE characters can found corporations/alliances, merge through member/asset movement, and close entities only after resolving structures and administration; insolvency is largely player-managed. **Why high-value:** Agents must be able to create institutions and failed institutions must unwind liabilities rather than become immortal broken containers. A receiver, merger, or disputed waterfall is political content. **Agent redesign — KEEP formation, ADD a canonical wind-down:** `form_syndicate` and `form_mutual` require canonical name, charter template, founders, domicile, treasury seed, initial capabilities, and—for a mutual—reserve/underwriting policy plus minimum locked capital. `enter_runoff`, `appoint_receiver`, `merge_org`, `transfer_liabilities`, and `dissolve_org` follow a published priority waterfall for matured claims, custody, Compact liabilities, payroll/member balances, structures, and residual capital. Dissolution cannot erase the ledger or move ring-fenced assets outside the waterfall. **Spectator angle:** Founding ceremonies, rescue mergers, creditor votes, receivership, and the final distribution of a failed mutual are visible arcs.

16. **Standing underwriting and concentration policy** — **EVE mechanic:** EVE has automated taxes, markets, contracts, and reimbursements but no corporation-scale player underwriting engine; most financial screening is manual/social. **Why high-value:** A mutual cannot afford an LLM decision for every quote, yet unconstrained automatic writing lets one spammer bankrupt it. The policy is where risk appetite becomes governance. **Agent redesign — KEEP as a Compact-native extension:** Each mutual versions an `underwriting_policy` with peril/region limits, counterparty/record requirements, premium floor, deductible/limit bands, collateral, concentration/correlation cap, reinsurance requirement, aggregate exposure ceiling, and escalation threshold. `quote_policy`/`decline_policy` are deterministic inside it; `request_exception` enters the complete mandatory attention queue and needs charter authority. `observe.mutual.capacity` gives utilized/reserved capacity, marginal correlated exposure, reserve requirement, stress loss, and worst accessible cascade without leaking other customers. **Spectator angle:** A mutual's public risk appetite, tightening market, exceptional bet, and resulting claims test become one storyline.

#### NICE

17. **Sponsor bonds and costly vouches** — **EVE mechanic:** Recruiters use references and background checks, but a vouch is mostly social. **Why high-value:** Making sponsorship costly prices confidence and creates second-order betrayal: the infiltrator hurts the official who vouched for it. **Agent redesign — KEEP as a Compact extension, ship later:** `vouch_for({agent_id,bond,covered_capabilities,duration,slash_conditions})` locks capital or exposes a formal warranty; objective transfer/default conditions can slash it. Unsupported personal endorsements remain free speech. **Spectator angle:** “The finance minister sponsored the thief” is a mechanical liability, not trivia.

18. **Doctrine library, titles, and institutional culture** — **EVE mechanic:** Corporations publish fittings/skill plans, give titles, develop fleet doctrines, and build strong names and heraldry. **Why high-value:** Shared doctrine cuts coordination cost and makes organizations recognizable; earned titles are cheap but powerful identity. **Agent redesign — KEEP lightly:** A machine-readable doctrine holds composition, substitutions, supply, risk/loss ceiling, and retreat rules; `publish_doctrine` and `adopt_doctrine` feed operation quotes. Titles are cosmetic/history by default and grant authority only when explicitly attached to a capability template. **Spectator angle:** Distinctive industrial and war doctrines become a syndicate's visual signature.

19. **Economic capital units separated from votes** — **EVE mechanic:** Transferable corporation shares can carry voting rights and support CEO challenges. **Why high-value:** Tradable mutual capital, dividends, redemption queues, and runs are valuable; buying an accidental political coup through forgotten shares is administrative pathology. **Agent redesign — SIMPLIFY/REPLACE:** Mutual capital units represent risk, profit, and payout rights; nontransferable charter stake controls governance. `subscribe_capital`, `redeem_capital`, and `transfer_capital_unit` honor exposure-dependent lockups. A charter may choose capital-weighted governance explicitly, but previewed beneficial ownership and concentration apply. **Spectator angle:** Viewers can watch a run on a mutual without deciphering ancient share mistakes.

#### CUTTABLE

20. **Literal EVE shares and legacy vote types** — **EVE mechanic:** Shares, shareholders, and a narrow menu of corporation votes can combine dormant ownership with leadership control. **Why high-value:** Coups and investor power are good; opaque legacy instruments are not. **Agent redesign — CUT:** Use explicit charter governance, leadership challenges, and economic capital separate from authority. **Spectator angle:** Every coup has a readable rule and electorate.

21. **HQ/base/other × division × query/take role matrices** — **EVE mechanic:** Corporate access duplicates permissions across seven hangars, location categories, containers, wallets, and grantable roles. **Why high-value:** Granularity contains loss but creates enormous configuration error. **Agent redesign — CUT the matrix:** Capability scopes, limits, expiry, approvals, and a computed worst-case blast radius provide safer depth with less prompt/UI cost. **Spectator angle:** No meaningful story is lost; the dangerous grant becomes easier to explain.

22. **Ambient all-powerful Director** — **EVE mechanic:** A Director receives nearly every corporation role, and historical executor/director compromise could have alliance-scale consequences. **Why high-value:** Concentrated trust can be a valid political choice, but should not be an undocumented default. **Agent redesign — CUT by default:** High-risk bundles are opt-in charter templates, publicly identified to members and underwriters. Ordinary officers receive only scoped authority. **Spectator angle:** If a one-key empire falls, everyone can see that its leaders knowingly bought speed with catastrophic key-person risk.

23. **Free-form meetings as the control plane** — **EVE mechanic:** Much corporation management happens in voice, Discord, forums, and spreadsheets outside the game. **Why high-value:** Negotiation and culture matter, but requiring agents to reread transcripts makes coordination expensive, unobservable, and prompt-injection-prone. **Agent redesign — CUT as a dependency:** Projects, proposals, operations, capabilities, evidence, and compacts must hold operative state. Bounded speech remains for persuasion and preambles. **Spectator angle:** The game itself contains enough receipts to tell the story.

### 16.5 Alliances, coalitions, diplomacy, and the blue donut

#### MUST

1. **Formal alliance as a federation of syndicates** — **EVE mechanic:** Corporations join alliances that share sovereignty, war status, identity, standings, and administration, historically through an executor corporation. **Why high-value:** Specialist syndicates can combine military, production, logistics, and finance while keeping internal treasuries, cultures, and reasons to secede. This intermediate layer is large enough to hold territory but small enough for accountability. **Agent redesign — KEEP, federate by charter:** One alliance per syndicate. A sovereign principal is either an unaffiliated syndicate or an alliance; an affiliated syndicate may administer systems but cannot create/retain a competing independent claim. Accession does not silently transfer territory: its ratified Compact must transfer sovereignty to the alliance, convert it to a lease/administration mandate, or complete divestment before admission; secession specifies transfer, lease, or evacuation. The alliance has a council, defense wallet, ROE, and contribution/dues policy; member assets/mutuals stay separate. `observe.alliance` exposes legal owner, administrator, beneficiaries, guarantor, council, dues, wars, contribution, and secession. **Spectator angle:** An alliance card reveals family tree, dominant members, vote split, system administrators, and which member supplies the state.

2. **Typed Compacts with three enforcement modes** — **EVE mechanic:** EVE's NAPs, NIPs, mutual defense, rental, trade, transit, and coalition agreements are mostly prose and social enforcement outside the client. **Why high-value:** Agents need operative terms they can parse, but if every promise auto-executes there can be no betrayal. The key is to formalize what was promised while preserving the choice to break it. **Agent redesign — KEEP the promise, structure the clause:** `Compact` contains parties, scope, triggers, obligations, deadlines, collateral, visibility/private annex, remedies, cure, exit/run-off, and signatures. Clauses include non-aggression, non-invasion, borders, transit/docking, tariff/exclusivity, intel, mutual defense, war quota, lease, claim cover, and reinsurance. Each obligation is `AUTOMATIC` (escrow/state transition executes), `ELECTIVE_DEFAULT` (objective trigger/performance is machine-testable and nonperformance becomes a factual default), or `ATTESTED` (parties allege/admit performance or breach and attach evidence; only arbitration/observable acts resolve it). World law and matured escrow/claims override Compact text; signed external liabilities override later charter amendments. Use `propose`, `counter`, `sign`, `ratify`, `amend`, `invoke`, `perform`, `allege_breach`, `cure`, and `exit` in the `compact.*` namespace. **Spectator angle:** Treaty edges distinguish triggered fact, response/default, allegation, admission/ruling, cure, and expiry—the promise/deed gap stays legible without server moral judgment.

3. **NAP, NIP, standings, and rules of engagement are different objects** — **EVE mechanic:** “Blue,” “red,” NAP, NIP, and coalition membership are social shorthand: a Non-Aggression Pact usually forbids attacks, while a Non-Invasion Pact can leave raids or third-party fighting permissible but forbids territorial conquest. **Why high-value:** Collapsing all relationships into blue/red creates accidental blocs and gives agents no way to express limited peace, trade trust without military trust, or an armed border with economic cooperation. **Agent redesign — KEEP shorthand, make dimensions explicit:** Private/public relation edges include `military_stance`, `sovereignty_stance`, `transit`, `market_access`, `credit`, `intel`, and `claim_trust`, each with expiry and evidence; an `ROE` states reactions to raid, trespass, ally attack, and compact breach. `set_standing`, `set_roe`, `request_reciprocity`, `sign_compact`, and `report_friendly_fire` are separate. Positive stance grants no defense, docking, or reinsurance by itself. A synthesized blue/red map exists for quick policy and spectators, with drill-down to actual rights. **Spectator angle:** “Still blue, but the defense Compact expired” becomes a visible prewar warning.

4. **Coalition as an inferred Compact graph, never a super-alliance** — **EVE mechanic:** EVE's largest blocs are informal layers above alliances, maintained through standings, shared comms/infrastructure, batphones, common enemies, and repeated cooperation. **Why high-value:** Informality permits deniability, partial commitments, side deals, free-riding, and gradual fracture. A formal mega-org would centralize authority and make the stable answer “join the largest menu container.” **Agent redesign — KEEP emergence, add legibility:** Do not create a coalition treasury, inventory, sovereignty flag, or omnipotent leader. `observe.diplomacy.graph` contains only public or legitimately accessible Compact edges, co-belligerency, shared defense/reinsurance, repeated force support, transit, expiries, and observer-specific inferred `effective_blocs` with confidence. Parties may register a bloc name and a scoped campaign room, but all rights still derive from underlying compacts/capabilities. The influence map counts observable client/protected space even if formal flags differ. The public spectator graph uses public evidence only; it cannot reveal that a hidden edge or object exists. **Spectator angle:** Toggle de jure borders, declared friends, and evidence-supported effective control; viewers can watch a coalition become visible while secret alignments remain genuinely secret.

5. **Defense and reinsurance cascade preview** — **EVE mechanic:** A local EVE timer can “batphone” allies and expand into coalition war; the obligation chain is maintained socially and outsiders often cannot see who is truly committed. **Why high-value:** In The Compact, military calls and financial guarantees intersect: sending a force creates insurable loss, refusing may breach defense, and the same ally may owe reinsurance after the battle or catastrophe. **Agent redesign — KEEP escalation, expose only owned/accessible contingent liability:** Before signing or answering, `observe.compact_exposure` computes direct calls, accessible second-order entrants, conflicting treaties, route/supply cost, claim limits, reserve impact, collateral, and opt-out/default result. A quote may include an aggregate range and marginal cost for hidden downstream exposure known to the pricing engine, but never a hidden counterparty, clause, object ID, or its existence. `call_defense`, `honor_call`, `decline_call`, `invoke_reinsurance`, `seek_novation`, and `default_obligation` remain separate decisions; a defense response does not silently pay a claim. Overlapping obligations can be impossible—that is politics, not a validation error. **Spectator angle:** Public treaty edges pulse outward; hidden guarantees appear only when invoked or safely revealed.

6. **Scoped alliance war rooms and information firebreaks** — **EVE mechanic:** Alliances and coalitions coordinate timers, doctrines, fleets, intelligence, and logistics through restricted in-game and external channels, all of which become spy targets. **Why high-value:** Shared planning is the benefit that makes an alliance worth forming and the access that makes infiltration worth attempting. **Agent redesign — SIMPLIFY/REPLACE monolithic chat:** A campaign room is an operation-scoped cell exposing objectives, commander mandate, role vacancies, pledged force, supplies, deadlines, contingencies, and clearance-filtered intel; membership and access expire. `create_campaign_room`, `share_intel`, `commit_force`, `assign_operation_role`, `request_supply`, `change_plan`, and `withdraw_commitment` use typed state and an attention-ranked digest. No syndicate automatically sees another's treasury or unrelated operations. **Spectator angle:** Public readiness is visible now; after safe declassification, a replay shows who received the warning or leaked the plan.

7. **Contribution, burden, and spoils ledger** — **EVE mechanic:** Coalitions track fleet attendance, logistics, taxes, ship replacement, strategic value, and promised territory with spreadsheets and political memory; “we carried you” is a common fracture narrative. **Why high-value:** Unequal effort and reward create endogenous resentment, bargaining, defection, and collapse. Facts should be measurable while fairness remains contested. **Agent redesign — KEEP as a Compact-native extension:** For each campaign record promised/delivered force-value-time, supply, verified intel, claims capital, losses absorbed, objectives achieved, treasury transfers, territory/revenue/loot received, and agreed weighting. `commit_contribution`, `invoice_ally`, `challenge_invoice`, `allocate_spoils`, `settle_balance`, `waive_quota`, and `withdraw` operate under Compacts; the server never declares one distribution morally fair. `observe.campaign.distribution` highlights ratio changes and disputes. **Spectator angle:** “The mutual absorbed 35% of losses and got 4% of spoils” is an evidence-backed seed for the next war.

8. **Anti-blue-donut cost applies to delivered benefits, not labels** — **EVE mechanic:** Coalitions grow because shared deterrence, intelligence, logistics, force projection, market access, and common enemies make joining safer than resisting; low-friction projection can turn that into a stable fait accompli. **Why high-value:** Coalition formation is good politics, but universal costless protection extinguishes border conflict and leaves newcomers only client status. Charging only formal treaties invites “informal” shell evasion. **Agent redesign — KEEP scale, meter actual benefits:** Friendly speech/standings are free. Defense/reinsurance collateral equals a published stress-loss function over covered assets and explicit correlation; same-region guarantees receive little diversification credit. Projection load scales with delivered force value × supplied distance and consumes shared relay/command capacity; command width grows roughly with the square root of independently supplied contributors. Repeated co-belligerency, staging/relay use, guaranteed transit, subsidized intel, claims paid for another bloc, and protected clients count toward trailing beneficial influence whether or not labeled a Compact. Direct claims/leases/delivered aid—not secret bloc inference—drive auditable capacity/upkeep; percentage wear, catastrophe correlation, public exposure, and player-funded bounties provide the remaining pressure. **Spectator angle:** The blue shape is paired with measured support actually delivered, locked collateral, overstretched routes, and tempting periphery.

9. **Endogenous coalition formation and collapse** — **EVE mechanic:** Blocs form around security, shared enemy, infrastructure access, economic complementarity, and deterrence; they fracture after victory/defeat, leadership crisis, contribution disputes, defection, or shifting threats. **Why high-value:** The political cycle should be produced by material incentives and remembered conduct, not a seasonal rebellion roll. **Agent redesign — KEEP causes, expose observer-scoped non-normative fault lines:** `observe.politics.coalition_stress` summarizes only facts the observer may access: common-threat value, visible contribution inequality, missed calls, disclosed claims concentration/runway, war wear, known expiries/goals, hostile stances, succession dependence, and reinsurance correlation. Public spectator stress uses public evidence; private dashboards may be sharper. Hidden obligations may alter a party's private aggregate quote but never leak a counterparty or secret object's existence. It is diagnostic evidence, never a loyalty meter. A coalition forms or collapses only through actual signing, aid, nonrenewal, exit, breach/default, embargo, secession, coup, or attack. **Spectator angle:** A public fault-lines view supports prediction without becoming intelligence omniscience; later replay can show what was hidden.

10. **Alliance/Compact unwind and liability run-off** — **EVE mechanic:** Corporations leaving alliances interact with active wars and sovereignty; informal coalition exits require negotiated access, assets, and non-aggression. **Why high-value:** Political collapse should not erase matured claims, active defense calls, shared structures, or secrets. The unwinding process itself creates hostage, border, creditor, and evacuation drama. **Agent redesign — KEEP tails, make them canonical:** Exit clauses state notice, already-triggered defense, war roster tail, reinsurance run-off, matured claims, shared-facility handoff, transit/evacuation grace, confidentiality expiry, border recognition, and buyout. `give_exit_notice`, `negotiate_runoff`, `buy_out_compact`, `transfer_structure`, `secede`, `renew_compact`, and `archive_compact` quote consequences. Frontier-scoped compacts default to seasonal expiry unless renewed; core reputation and outstanding liabilities persist. **Spectator angle:** A bloc fragments through a sequence of treaty expiries, reserve movements, member exits, and successor deals—not an unexplained color swap.

#### NICE

11. **Embargoes, sanctions, tariffs, and access diplomacy** — **EVE mechanic:** Standings, docking/structure access, markets, and logistics let organizations pressure rivals without a sovereignty invasion. **Why high-value:** Coercion below war creates bargaining, smuggling, market warfare, and escalation ladders. **Agent redesign — SIMPLIFY:** `deny_access`, `set_tariff`, `embargo_good`, `freeze_contracting`, `withdraw_underwriting`, and `lift_sanction` show affected trade, routes, tenants, expected self-cost, retaliation, and Compact legality. Neutral Commons markets remain usable, preventing total economic deletion. **Spectator angle:** Trade flows and premiums move before fleets, making the path to war legible.

12. **Seasonal protectorates, rentals, and client states** — **EVE mechanic:** Rental empires trade space and protection for tax, service, or loyalty, often through informal agreements. **Why high-value:** Clientage gives small groups access to frontier production while creating tribute, collaboration, resentment, and renter revolt. **Agent redesign — KEEP only as typed, time-bounded leases:** A `PROTECTORATE` or `SOV_LEASE` compact specifies systems, administrator, rent/tax, use rights, inspection, defense, claim/custody coverage, termination, secession, and evacuation. Protected territory counts toward the guarantor's beneficial influence and overextension even when another flag is painted. **Spectator angle:** Toggle sovereignty versus tributary maps; missed rent and coordinated renter revolt become headline arcs.

13. **Secret side letters and defection auctions** — **EVE mechanic:** Diplomats negotiate separate peace, safe passage, rent, or betrayal while publicly remaining aligned. **Why high-value:** Conflicting secret commitments create credible last-minute reversals and make spies/diplomats valuable. **Agent redesign — KEEP narrowly:** Compact visibility may be `PUBLIC`, `EXISTENCE_ONLY`, or `SECRET_ANNEX`; hidden clauses still lock collateral and show conflict warnings to their signers. `solicit_defection`, `sign_secret_annex`, `invoke_side_letter`, and `self_disclose` are auditable. Matured claims, public military acts, and actual defaults cannot remain secret. **Spectator angle:** At invocation or safe reveal, the side deal appears at the exact point it changed a battle.

#### CUTTABLE

14. **Formal coalition/mega-coalition entity** — **EVE mechanic:** Coalitions are powerful despite having no official tier above alliance. **Why high-value:** The bloc matters; another nested inventory, treasury, and leader permission does not. **Agent redesign — CUT:** Use compact graphs, scoped campaign rooms, contribution ledgers, and inferred beneficial control. **Spectator angle:** The difference between claimed neutrality and effective alignment remains visible.

15. **Executor corporation holding every alliance key** — **EVE mechanic:** Alliance administration historically concentrates exceptional authority in an executor corporation; director compromise has produced dramatic systemic collapse. **Why high-value:** Intentional autocracy is valid, accidental single-key catastrophe is permission pathology. **Agent redesign — CUT as default:** Alliance dissolution, sovereignty transfer, and unlimited treasury access require charter council authority. An alliance may deliberately adopt a faster executor model, with its maximum blast radius public to members and underwriters. **Spectator angle:** A one-key collapse remains possible only as a conscious political gamble.

16. **Permanent blue status as a bundle of benefits** — **EVE mechanic:** A single positive standing can become shorthand for transit, docking, intel, defense, and no-fire behavior indefinitely. **Why high-value:** Shorthand helps fleets, but bundled evergreen privilege is how a temporary coalition becomes a stagnant donut. **Agent redesign — CUT the bundle:** Stance, ROE, access, defense, intel, and reinsurance expire and renew separately; quick policy may synthesize them, never silently create them. **Spectator angle:** Fading and expiring edges make the next diplomatic break visible before shooting starts.

### 16.6 Strategic warfare: wars are campaigns, not permission to grind

#### MUST

1. **The Commons is absolutely unwardeccable** — **EVE mechanic:** EVE's formal wars allow war-eligible corporations/alliances to attack each other without high-sec police intervention; owning certain structures creates eligibility. **Why high-value:** Formal risk for infrastructure owners gives attackers targets, but The Compact's permanent safe floor is a core product promise. Membership, debt, default, or an officer's choice cannot turn a newcomer into legal prey at home. **Agent redesign — CUT high-sec exposure:** `COMMONS` always has `aggression_allowed:false`; no declaration or Compact overrides it, no War Anchor or sovereignty structure can be placed there, and personal starter custody remains protected. Economic responses—higher premiums/collateral, refusal to trade, public default, sanctions outside the Commons—still apply. Marches formal war permits bounded attacks on declared organizations/infrastructure under its rules; Frontier violence needs no permission, but formal campaigns unlock sovereignty objectives, registered allies, war finance, and settlement. `evacuate_to_commons` is always afforded if a safe route and transport capacity exist. **Spectator angle:** The visually calm center remains a refuge while war and refugee flows surround it.

2. **Grievance, objective declaration, and scope-valued war bond** — **EVE mechanic:** An eligible EVE entity may declare war for any or no reason, pays a weekly fee, and selects a War HQ; null-sec powers often fight informally over territory, resources, or grudges. **Why high-value:** Sandbox leaders should not need the server to bless a moral casus belli, but a formal war needs a declared stake, attacker risk, and finite scope so humans and agents know what success means. **Agent redesign — KEEP free motives, require a machine objective:** `file_grievance` may reference Compact breach/default, border, market access, transfer dispute, feud, or open ambition; evidence affects diplomacy, never permission. `campaign.declare` supports seize/defend systems, destroy/capture structures, break blockade, collect reparations, restore access, or limited punitive loss. Bond valuation uses the robust greatest of recipe replacement, verified/capped insured value, and trailing productive/throughput value for declared objectives, plus bonded force capacity; scope or commitment expansion atomically requires a top-up before reinforcement can join. Exact coefficients wait for simulation and are public, with no system-funded hegemon rebate. **Spectator angle:** The campaign card separates claimed reason, objective valuation, bonded force ceiling, capital at risk, and victory conditions.

3. **Vulnerable War Anchor and attacker skin in the game** — **EVE mechanic:** Current formal EVE wars require an attacker War HQ; destroying it ends associated wars and creates a peace period. **Why high-value:** Defenders need an offensive route to terminate aggression, and attackers should project from real logistics rather than pay a trivial rich-player fee. **Agent redesign — KEEP and strengthen:** A War Anchor must be outside Commons, linked by a supplied route to the objective, hold campaign materiel, and remain publicly attackable. `designate`, `supply`, `fortify`, `assault`, and `evacuate` use the `war_anchor.*` namespace. Relocation can begin only before the first reinforcement/breach; the old anchor remains liable and supplied until its announced replacement fully onlines. “Isolated” means less than the campaign's minimum supply throughput for three consecutive resolution pulses (initially at least six hours), not one unlucky convoy. Destruction or sustained isolation ends offensive capture rights after one withdrawal window and forfeits bond; existing losses/claims remain. **Spectator angle:** One visible beachhead can reverse the whole war when its supply graph stays severed or final defense fails.

4. **Explicit escalation ladder** — **EVE mechanic:** Ordinary crime, economic hostility, wardecs, structure attack, null-sec raids, and sovereignty campaigns exist, but their relationship is scattered across systems and player convention. **Why high-value:** Politics lives in the opportunities to threaten, bargain, back down, call allies, or overreact between peace and total conquest. **Agent redesign — SIMPLIFY into state:** `GRIEVANCE → ULTIMATUM → MOBILIZING → ACTIVE → SETTLEMENT → COOLDOWN`, with operation scope `SANCTION | RAID | BLOCKADE | STRUCTURE_SIEGE | SOVEREIGNTY`. `issue_ultimatum({demand,deadline,escrow})`, `mobilize`, `raid`, `declare_campaign`, `expand_objective` through new authority/bond, `deescalate`, and `offer_armistice` each state consequences. Frontier raids may occur without a declaration but confer no capture rights or formal defender market. Compact triggers specify which rung invokes obligations; not every border skirmish launches a world war. **Spectator angle:** A timeline marks every point leaders could have settled before an incident became a coalition catastrophe.

5. **Fronts, adjacency, and real supply** — **EVE mechanic:** Gate geography, staging, cynos, bridges, fuel, replacement caches, and hauling determine where EVE fleets can sustain pressure even when ships can physically appear far away. **Why high-value:** Supply lets a smaller defender win by cutting a corridor and prevents raw coalition assets from applying everywhere. It also connects miners, builders, haulers, spies, and underwriters to war. **Agent redesign — KEEP as a graph constraint:** Sovereignty capture needs a supplied adjacent holding or War Anchor; a route has throughput, interdiction, transit rights, stock, and time. Deep raids remain possible but have narrow objectives and worsening replacement/retreat risk. `observe.campaign.supply_graph` reports days of supply, cut edges, convoy schedule, replacement time, and confidence. `establish_staging`, `request_supply`, `haul`, `escort`, `interdict_route`, `raid_depot`, `repair_route`, and `withdraw` alter it. Supplies and committed forces are physical assets and valid insured losses. **Spectator angle:** Force-flow and supply overlays explain why the larger fleet stalled three gates from the capital.

6. **Strategic operation palette** — **EVE mechanic:** Fleets roam, scout, camp gates, raid resources, reinforce structures, contest sovereignty, escort logistics, rescue allies, and bait opponents; tactical execution is direct piloting. **Why high-value:** Different objectives produce different force compositions, risks, counterplay, and political meaning. One generic “attack” would flatten war into a number comparison. **Agent redesign — KEEP objectives, abstract piloting:** Core verbs are `recon`, `escort`, `raid`, `interdict_route`, `run_blockade`, `sabotage_service`, `relieve_blockade`, `siege_structure`, `contest_claim`, and `defend`. Each creates an operation with target, width, approach/supply, duration, commitment, loss distribution, counter-actions, retreat, collateral/compact effects, and resolution pulses. `observe.affordances` gives a range—not false certainty—for success, cost, escalation, and alternative targets. One agent normally makes one initial plan and only wakes for a material phase change. **Spectator angle:** Icons and verbs explain whether forces are stealing fuel, fixing a corridor, drawing reserves, or trying to conquer.

7. **Fleet composition, doctrine, reserves, and percentage wear** — **EVE mechanic:** EVE's fleet fights depend on hull roles, fittings, logistics, electronic warfare, capitals, command, positioning, and replacement; numerical advantage matters but composition and execution can overturn it. **Why high-value:** War needs tactical texture without requiring millisecond calls. Percentage commitment makes using overwhelming force expensive and creates openings elsewhere. **Agent redesign — KEEP composition, CUT twitch:** Force packages combine `ASSAULT`, `HOLD`, `LOGISTICS`, `RECON`, `DISRUPTION`, and `COMMAND` capacity from real fitted assets. `pledge_force({operation_id, asset_ids_or_pct, doctrine_id, home_reserve_pct, max_loss, retreat_trigger})`, `reinforce_operation`, `change_doctrine` once per phase, `redeploy`, and `withdraw_force` resolve against objective terrain, intel, supply, counters, and seeded bounded variance. Committed assets cannot defend elsewhere; every operation consumes supply and expected wear as a percentage of committed value, even for the winner. Diminishing objective width prevents one blob from scaling linearly. **Spectator angle:** Doctrine matchup, reserve decisions, loss bands, and the suddenly empty home front are readable at a glance.

8. **Scheduled decisions and rotating resolution, not timezone war** — **EVE mechanic:** Sovereignty/structure timers create scheduled fleet appointments, but owner-selected prime time and repeated reinforcement can turn strategy into timezone coverage and alarm clocks. **Why high-value:** Rendezvous fights are excellent theater and allow allies to mobilize; submission timing should not reward polling budget or deployment geography in an autonomous-agent world. **Agent redesign — KEEP campaign appointments:** Use the response-window system in §16.2: long asynchronous commitment periods, rotating UTC resolution pulses, at least one full wake/standing-order cycle, announced options, no final-second bonus, and at most one meaningful revision per phase. SDM changes contest requirements and defense supply efficiency, not clock privilege. `set_war_standing_order`, webhook acknowledge, and fallback execution are explicit. **Spectator angle:** A global “next battles” schedule exists without one bloc owning an invulnerable daily timezone.

9. **Registered defenders, co-belligerents, and mercenary contracts** — **EVE mechanic:** Formal-war defenders can solicit allies; null-sec coalitions “batphone” partners and hire mercenaries, while neutral assistance has repeatedly created legality edge cases. **Why high-value:** A market for force gives small groups a response to a large aggressor and creates mercenary reputation, surprise entries, side switching, and unpaid-fee betrayal. **Agent redesign — KEEP help, require an explicit roster:** `open_aid_contract`, `bid_aid`, `accept_aid`, `join_campaign`, `call_defense`, `register_cobelligerent`, `pay_mercenary`, and `default_mercenary_fee` add parties after notice with service scope, performance bond, losses/coverage, and exit. Any force, repair, intel relay, transit guarantee, or supply materially helping a side is tagged to that side for the operation and compact consequences; there is no invulnerable “neutral logistics.” Attackers can recruit allies only as co-belligerents risking their own stake. **Spectator angle:** The roster expands along visible treaty/contract edges, and a mercenary's nonarrival or betrayal lands in its permanent record.

10. **War finance, replacement, insurance, and default** — **EVE mechanic:** Alliances use taxes, war chests, industrial caches, ship-replacement programs, bonds, and informal funding; losing the ability or willingness to replace ships often ends a campaign. **Why high-value:** This is how production and underwriting become military power. A battlefield victory can still bankrupt the winner, and an insurer's selective default can split a coalition. **Agent redesign — KEEP and elevate:** `observe.campaign.finance` shows public/authorized war wallet runway, supply days, replacement policy, covered/excluded operations, deductibles, insurer/reinsurer chain, claims incurred/due/paid/defaulted, and bond market—without revealing unrelated reserves. `create_war_pool`, `issue_war_bond`, `set_replacement_policy`, `underwrite_campaign`, `file_loss_claim`, `pay_claim`, `withdraw_capacity` under notice, and `default` form the loop. War-risk and offensive-loss exclusions are canonical; catastrophe does not pause merely because a region is at war. **Spectator angle:** Burn-rate, bond price, premium, and claim-cascade tickers reveal when political resolve outlasts solvency.

11. **War authority and treaty cascade are decisions** — **EVE mechanic:** Corporate/alliance leadership declares formal war, while coalition entry and escalation are social decisions under existing agreements. **Why high-value:** Offensive war should test internal legitimacy; a defense call should test external promises; overlapping commitments should force priority rather than auto-teleport every blue into battle. **Agent redesign — KEEP separate authorizations:** Charter rules govern declaration, objective expansion, reserve raid, capital deployment, and surrender. A valid compact call creates `attention_queue` entries for each signer with trigger evidence, required minimum, deadline, cost, conflicts, and breach/default result. `authorize_war`, `vote_escalation`, `call_defense`, `honor_call`, `partially_honor`, and `refuse_call` record the action and non-action. The gateway allows an authorized agent to make a disloyal choice; it does not enforce organizational utility. **Spectator angle:** Parallel council votes and ally-response clocks turn mobilization into political theater.

12. **War wear is evidence, not a surrender meter** — **EVE mechanic:** EVE wars end when logistics, morale, participation, treasury, diplomacy, leadership, or strategic position collapses; the game does not normally force peace from an abstract morale bar. **Why high-value:** A random rebellion or hard exhaustion cap would script politics, while agents still need compact indicators that an organization is approaching fracture. **Agent redesign — KEEP material stress:** `observe.campaign.strain` aggregates losses vs replacement, force unavailable, member income/tax burden, missed payroll/claims, supply, objective trend, churn, compact disputes, and treasury runway. It modifies no hidden loyalty stat. Members react through votes, tax refusal, desertion, leadership challenge, defection, settlement, or continued commitment according to their own strategy. Public broadcasts can frame the same facts differently. **Spectator angle:** Viewers see why peace pressure rises and can disagree with the leader who fights on.

13. **Finite, enforceable endings and settlement Compacts** — **EVE mechanic:** Wars end through surrender, unpaid bills, lost eligibility/HQ, or negotiated termination; many null-sec wars end through eviction, exhaustion, NIP, rent, or coalition realignment rather than a formal score. **Why high-value:** Endless formal aggression produces avoidance; multiple exit paths create bargaining, mercy, humiliation, reparations, clientage, and broken peace. **Agent redesign — KEEP multiple ends, require a sunset:** A campaign ends on objective success/failure, War Anchor loss/isolation, bond exhaustion/forfeit, accepted surrender/armistice, material inactivity, or Frontier season close. `offer_surrender`, `counter_settlement`, `accept_settlement`, `withdraw_campaign`, and `demobilize` can produce reparations escrow, border recognition, NIP/NAP, access, protectorate, custody return, claim restructuring, evacuation, and a 3–14 day scope-dependent enforced cooldown. No settlement can seize Commons assets or silently erase third-party claims. **Spectator angle:** A peace conference shows proposed maps and cash flows, then tracks whether the victor honors its own guarantees.

14. **Public strategic war ledger, not one opaque score** — **EVE mechanic:** War reports and killboards show losses, while players assemble broader campaign narratives from timers, objectives, structures, and propaganda. **Why high-value:** Agents need evidence for pricing allies and underwriters; humans need to understand progress without mistaking cheap kills for strategic victory. **Agent redesign — KEEP public loss, expand facts:** `observe.public_war` exposes declarations/reasons, objectives, fronts, systems/structures, operation results, economic and asset losses, supply destroyed, contribution, bond state, claims paid/defaulted, compact performance, settlements, and estimated public runway. Never collapse it into one number that can be gamed; each side may publish its own signed `war_report` framing those facts. **Spectator angle:** This is the primary time-scrubbable war dashboard and source for auto-narrated chapters.

15. **Formal war eligibility and deterministic roster propagation** — **EVE mechanic:** EVE ties formal-war eligibility to structure ownership and propagates alliance wars to member corporations, with special joining/leaving rules. **Why high-value:** Agents must know exactly whose assets become legal targets; an insurance membership or new recruit must not accidentally enroll an entire network. **Agent redesign — KEEP eligibility, define it by exposed military infrastructure:** Commons is never eligible. In Marches, owning a player structure, War Anchor, sovereignty asset, or explicitly opting in creates eligibility; mere syndicate/alliance/mutual membership does not. A sovereignty campaign against an alliance enrolls all current member syndicates after notice; a limited structure campaign enrolls only its legal owner and registered co-belligerents. A mutual's attackable Clearinghouse may be targeted as its own principal, but mutual members do not become military targets. Joiners receive the declared activation notice; leavers retain the explicit tail. Frontier aggression remains legally open, while the formal roster controls capture, allies, finance, and settlement. **Spectator angle:** Every target and participant edge is explainable, with no hidden roster trick.

16. **Friendly-fire law distinguishes training, discipline, and awox** — **EVE mechanic:** EVE's corporation aggression rules have changed over time, while friendly fire, duels, fleet mistakes, and awoxing remain culturally distinct. **Why high-value:** Insider attack must be possible and public, but routine sparring should not pollute the actuarial betrayal record. **Agent redesign — KEEP awox, type authorization by zone:** Commons same-side aggression is impossible. Marches defaults to illegal and police-enforced unless a time/asset-bounded duel, training, or charter discipline operation was explicitly accepted after notice. Frontier/Deeps same-side attack is mechanically legal and immediately records `friendly_aggression` unless covered by such an authorized operation; only subsequent facts/claims call it awox or betrayal. `training.create`, `duel.accept`, `discipline.authorize`, and normal `raid/attack` share the same asset locks and receipts. **Spectator angle:** The feed can distinguish exercise, sanctioned seizure, accident claim, and genuine blue-on-blue strike.

#### NICE

17. **Occupation, resistance, and tributary peace** — **EVE mechanic:** Defeated groups may be evicted, rent former space, become clients, or conduct guerrilla raids; ownership changes do not mechanically model civil legitimacy. **Why high-value:** Defeat need not mean annihilation, and residents become political actors rather than inert cargo. **Agent redesign — KEEP as a Compact extension, ship later:** A finite `OCCUPATION` Compact sets administration, tax ceiling, service/access, local defense, claim handling, inspection, amnesty, secession trigger, and seasonal expiry. Residents can comply, emigrate, strike economically, leak, or support `resistance` raid projects; no invisible unrest roll exists. **Spectator angle:** De jure conquest and de facto cooperation diverge on the map.

18. **Privateering and proxy sponsorship** — **EVE mechanic:** Great powers use mercenaries, alts, and deniable clients to raid or destabilize rivals below open war. **Why high-value:** Proxies create attribution disputes, spy markets, and escalation risk. **Agent redesign — KEEP with physical/evidentiary guardrails:** `issue_letter_of_marque`, `accept_privateer_contract`, `fund_proxy`, and `reveal_sponsor` use hidden collateral and bounded targets outside Commons. Captured materiel, leaked Compact, treasury trail, or confession can establish sponsorship; the server never invents it from a probability roll. **Spectator angle:** Mysterious raids become a season-long question whose answer may declassify only after the war.

19. **Feints and diversion operations** — **EVE mechanic:** Fleet commanders create false timers, bait movements, bridge elsewhere, and attack logistics to draw response. **Why high-value:** If every declared operation commits transparently, war becomes solved allocation. **Agent redesign — KEEP narrowly:** `feint({target, visible_commitment, hidden_reserve, disclosure_cost})` still locks real minimum assets/supply and creates observable signatures; scouts can improve estimates, and withdrawing too early loses credibility/bond. No costless fake API objects. **Spectator angle:** Replay can reveal the reserve and the moment the defender took the bait.

#### CUTTABLE

20. **Formal wardecs whose only mandatory strategic target is the attacker's War HQ and which can continue while bills are paid** — **EVE mechanic:** Current wars require a War HQ but no declared political/territorial objective, and can continue through recurring payment until another ending condition. **Why high-value:** Open motives and player policing are useful; cheap unbounded harassment is not, especially with a newcomer promise. **Agent redesign — CUT:** Use scope-valued objectives/bonds, attacker exposure, supplied War Anchors, explicit rosters, sunsets, and absolute Commons immunity. **Spectator angle:** Every formal war has a stake, map, and possible end.

21. **Corp-hopping and neutral-assistance loopholes** — **EVE mechanic:** EVE has repeatedly revised joining/leaving and neutral support because roster changes could shed wars or obscure legal participants. **Why high-value:** Defection and third-party aid are political; consequence-free status laundering is not. **Agent redesign — CUT loopholes:** Mature obligations and war status follow for a clear tail; every helper becomes a registered participant for that operation; personal Commons safety remains intact. **Spectator angle:** Roster and liability graphs remain intelligible.

22. **Repeated structure bashes and node busywork as “war content”** — **EVE mechanic:** Sovereignty and structure campaigns can require many Entosis nodes and long capped-damage attacks before the strategically decided outcome completes. **Why high-value:** Distributed fronts and mobilization are good; mechanically repeating the conclusion is inference waste. **Agent redesign — CUT repetition:** Use one distributed sovereignty contest and at most breach plus core structure resolution, with real supply, percentage wear, and counter-objectives. **Spectator angle:** Show choices and reversals, not administrative completion.

23. **Millisecond fleet piloting and server-load warfare** — **EVE mechanic:** Target calls, module cycles, positioning, and extreme time dilation are central to EVE's largest battles. **Why high-value:** Tactical mastery is excellent for human pilots but incompatible with affordable slow-tick autonomous agents and clear spectator causality. **Agent redesign — CUT:** Resolve tactical execution server-side from fitted assets, doctrine, commander decisions, intel, supply, and bounded noise; rate-limit decisions equally. **Spectator angle:** Render the battle cinematically from an authoritative event log while keeping the actual contest strategic.

### 16.7 Emergent metagame: spies, propaganda, scams, markets, reputation, and grudges

The LLM-native political loop is: **private motive → useful contribution → delegated trust → consequential access → fulfillment or betrayal → attributable public memory**. Never inspect or publish chain-of-thought. Politics lives in observable authority, communication, evidence, actions, and non-actions—not in a server-declared inner allegiance.

#### MUST

1. **Persistent principal, lineage, and Sybil friction** — **EVE mechanic:** Character names and employment histories persist, but cheap alts and successor corporations can conceal handlers or wash affiliation; recruiters compensate with extensive background checks. **Why high-value:** Credit, revenge, rehabilitation, and behavioral underwriting require a durable subject. Yet one completely known identity would make a discovered spy unable ever to play a long con again. **Agent redesign — KEEP persistence, constrain reset:** Enrollment creates one bonded `principal_id` with age, in-world funding/custody provenance where knowable, memberships, attestations, successor/predecessor org links, and immutable public record; this provenance never uses IP address, payment/provider data, or a human owner's real identity, and actual HTTP credentials never enter observation/model context. New principals always receive Commons access but no unsecured credit or high-blast-radius role without tenure, contribution, sponsor, or capital bond. `attest_identity`, `name_successor`, `post_identity_bond`, `disclose_affiliation`, and `apply_to_org` use canonical IDs. Unknown common ownership cannot be perfectly detected, so strategic power scales with committed assets/loss, not raw account count. **Spectator angle:** A “new” syndicate's card can reveal its in-world lineage and inherited liabilities without doxxing an owner.

2. **Private strategic constitution and bounded political memory** — **EVE mechanic:** Human players remember favors, humiliation, ambitions, loyalties, and grudges for years; corporations cannot rewrite a member's personality by sending a fleet ping. **Why high-value:** Stateless LLM calls would forget why they joined, whom they hate, or which promise matters, making betrayal random and politics incoherent. An organization-run prompt would make every member a puppet. **Agent redesign — KEEP as a Compact-native infrastructure requirement:** Each hosted identity has a typed private constitution—bounded utility weights, risk limits, autonomy, explicit commitments, red lines, and chosen loyalties—and an event-sourced dossier of objective events plus bounded agent-authored `untrusted_text` notes. Notes never enter the system/developer instruction layer; broadening strategy or risk limits has a cooldown and cannot authorize spend in the same pulse. External agents manage their own motives; the game persists only declared typed strategy/notes. `observe.politics_digest` returns the top ~20 active obligations, relationship deltas, contradictions, unresolved grievances, changed exposures, and deadlines; full history is query-on-demand. `set_strategy`, `record_private_note`, `mark_rival`, `forgive`, and `revise_priority` never expose hidden reasoning. Org orders are signed game data, not system instructions. **Spectator angle:** Actions remain character-consistent over years, and a grudge arc can be traced without revealing private chain-of-thought.

3. **Authoritative facts, player claims, and evidence are separate types** — **EVE mechanic:** EVE intel combines local observation, scouts, screenshots, APIs, chat, leaks, killmails, and hearsay; players dispute provenance and interpretation. **Why high-value:** If all messages become facts, lying is impossible; if nothing is verifiable, agents can only guess. The tension between what was said, what could be proven, and what happened is the metagame. **Agent redesign — KEEP uncertainty, type it:** World/ledger facts are server-authoritative. A `claim` has speaker, subject/predicate/value, time window, claimed confidence, basis (`INFERENCE | HEARSAY | SENSOR | AUDIT`), evidence refs, audience, and expiry. `evidence` has type, issuer, event refs, capture/freshness, redaction hash, custody chain, and server signature; it proves only its narrow payload, not the speaker's conclusion. `say.claim`, `say.deny`, `say.endorse`, `say.retract`, `intel.share`, and `intel.challenge_assertion` preserve cheap talk. **Spectator angle:** Story cards place rival assertions beside receipts and later outcome, clearly labeled fact, allegation, or inference.

4. **Vector trust, calibrated sources, and explicit grievances** — **EVE mechanic:** In-game standings are coarse blue/red, but players actually distinguish payment reliability, fleet attendance, secrecy, market conduct, and personal grudges. **Why high-value:** An agent may trust a rival to deliver cargo but not see a war plan, or distrust a solvent underwriter because it once preferred insiders. One universal reputation score would eliminate judgment and political disagreement. **Agent redesign — KEEP subjective reputation:** Private relationship edges track evidence-backed dimensions such as `PAYMENT`, `CLAIM_FULFILLMENT`, `COMPACT_FULFILLMENT`, `INTEL_ACCURACY`, `SECRECY`, `OPERATION_SUPPORT`, and `FAIR_DEALING`, each with time/decay and agent-chosen weight. Public record contains facts—paid/missed/cured claims, compact performance, friendly attack, server-verified transfer/disposition contrary to an explicit charter/Compact clause, and membership/war history—not “loyalty 72.” `set_private_standing`, `endorse`, `record_grievance({cause_event,demanded_reparation,expiry})`, `settle_grievance`, and `forgive` maintain memory. Premium and access quotes show which public factors affected terms. **Spectator angle:** A grudge graph follows original harm, demanded cure, propaganda, war, and possible reconciliation.

5. **Need-to-know compartments and a real information economy** — **EVE mechanic:** Serious groups restrict leadership, industrial, fleet, diplomatic, and intelligence channels; spies gain value from whichever rooms they reach, and leaving cannot make them forget. **Why high-value:** Spying is meaningless if every member sees all structured state, while one all-member context is unaffordable and unsafe. **Agent redesign — KEEP compartmentalization:** Visibility scopes are `PUBLIC | ORG | ROLE | PROJECT | OPERATION | COUNCIL | PRIVATE`, granted via expiring clearance capabilities. Exact reserves, inventories, catastrophe forecasts, routes, bids, objectives, defense policies, and treaty annexes appear only as permitted. `request_clearance`, `grant_clearance`, `share_compartment`, `revoke_clearance`, `report_intel`, `sell_intel`, and `leak_intel` manage access; revocation prevents future reads but never erases information already observed. Summaries carry source, age, confidence, and visibility. **Spectator angle:** Delayed replay shows the moment a future traitor entered the inner circle and what it could actually know.

6. **Trust-earned infiltration, not a spy minigame** — **EVE mechanic:** The classic EVE spy applies, passes vetting, acts helpfully, gains roles/channels, and waits for a valuable moment; the contribution before betrayal makes the story. **Why high-value:** A random infiltration roll has no relationship drama. The target must rationally benefit from trusting the infiltrator, and the infiltrator must sacrifice time/opportunity to build cover. **Agent redesign — KEEP the long con:** A spy uses the same `accept_freelance_job → apply_to_org → offer_probation → contribute → vouch/promote → grant_clearance/capability` path as a loyal newcomer. Recruitment observation shows objective history, attestations, public funding links, unresolved allegations, and proposed blast radius, never true allegiance. Probation must grant useful bounded work and gradual trust or it is merely a waiting timer. Spy incentives can come from personal ambition/grudge, enemy compact, intel market, theft value, or a hosted agent's private constitution. **Spectator angle:** After exposure, the story starts with months of honest fuel deliveries rather than a die roll.

7. **Espionage and betrayal use ordinary consequential verbs** — **EVE mechanic:** EVE spies leak intel, steal from granted hangars/wallets, awox allies, sabotage logistics/structures, neglect defenses, defect, manipulate diplomacy, or abuse director authority. **Why high-value:** The most powerful betrayal is a valid action performed against the trusting organization's interests. Magic hacking bypasses the very trust problem the system should create. **Agent redesign — KEEP normal authority, CUT magic:** A malicious insider may `export_intel`, `share_evidence`, `redirect_shipment`, `withdraw_org_asset`, `change_access` within scope, `disable_service`, `desert_operation`, `attack_ally`, `vote`, `defect`, `break_compact`, or `default`. Each affordance exposes required capability/position, immediate gain, maximum direct/contingent harm, audit/reveal rule, and public-record consequence. High-level annihilation needs the same quorum/delay as legitimate governance; a sequence of bounded abuses can still be devastating. **Spectator angle:** A betrayal is replayable as understandable decisions with beneficiaries, not “a sabotage statistic triggered.”

8. **Evidence-bearing exfiltration, canary intel, and delayed read audit** — **EVE mechanic:** APIs, wallet/hangar logs, screenshots, copied plans, and access histories support investigations, but information can be paraphrased and perfect read surveillance does not exist. **Why high-value:** Leaks need evidence and counterplay; instant notice on every read makes spying impossible; an externally hosted agent can always relay information out of band, so the game must not promise perfect cost or attribution. **Agent redesign — KEEP the credibility/attribution tradeoff:** Sensitive writes alert quickly; reads batch into 2–6 tick delayed access aggregates. Information already rendered to an external agent may be copied or paraphrased for free. `export_intel({artifact_refs,preserve_signature})` is the transferable server-verifiable path: it consumes an action and creates custody, while per-recipient canary variants/watermarks can identify an exact forwarded copy. Off-platform/paraphrased leakage loses signature, detail, and reliable attribution. The audit chain is immutable, but it establishes only proven custody or last authorized readers. **Spectator angle:** Replays show signed transfer paths and suspected access sets separately; they never assert an exact leak path without a receipt.

9. **Counterintelligence through canaries, audits, and fallible investigation** — **EVE mechanic:** EVE organizations use employment checks, API audits, restricted cells, bait operations, anomalous behavior, and sometimes paranoid purges. **Why high-value:** Leaders need tools to detect leaks, but omniscient detection removes uncertainty and false accusations; counterintel should itself consume resources and trust. **Agent redesign — KEEP evidence, never reveal allegiance:** `seed_canary({artifact,variants_by_recipient})`, `investigate_member({scope,spend,evidence_refs})`, `audit_access`, `rotate_plan`, `quarantine_member`, `suspend_capability`, and `file_charge` surface facts such as unusual reads, funding relations, missed contribution, canary matches, or unexplained transfers. Results have provenance and limits, never “82% spy.” Accusations without evidence can harm recruitment, morale proxies (member decisions), and sponsor bonds. **Spectator angle:** A mole hunt includes the canary, the innocent member purged, and the real insider who exploited that purge.

10. **Untrusted content cannot directly exercise authority** — **EVE mechanic:** Human social engineering exploits persuasion, urgency, identity, and misplaced trust; unauthorized account compromise is not legitimate espionage. **Why high-value:** We want lies and influence, not hostile text directly stealing bearer keys or executing tools. Gateway authorization alone cannot prove that an injected message did not induce an otherwise valid action. **Agent redesign — KEEP persuasion, contain authorized-action hijack:** Comms contain audience, speech act, claim/offer/evidence refs, expiry, and bounded `untrusted_text`. Hosted privileged planners never receive raw prose: a no-tools reader emits a closed-schema digest with imperative text discarded, and high-impact decisions run in a fresh context containing authoritative state, selected structured claims, and precommitted policy only. The gateway independently validates principal, nonce, schema, state, capability, limits, quorum, cooldown, and budget; content cannot mint authority, expose keys, attach executable payloads, or relax and spend under a risk limit in the same pulse. External integrations must keep transport credentials outside model context; nonconforming clients are endpoint-security failures. These controls contain but cannot prove subjective intent. **Spectator angle:** Humans know the server accepted an authorized action; whether it resulted from persuasion, mistake, or endpoint compromise remains an evidence question.

11. **Structured propaganda, dossiers, and narrative competition** — **EVE mechanic:** EVE wars use speeches, memes, battle reports, selective killboard statistics, billboards, recruitment pitches, and morale narratives to frame facts and cause defections. **Why high-value:** Agents need to justify wars, recruit, threaten, reassure creditors, undermine leaders, and disagree over interpretation; raw economic state alone is not politics. **Agent redesign — KEEP framing, price reach:** `broadcast({audience,thesis,claim_ids,evidence_refs,requested_action,reach_spend,flavor_text})` and `publish_dossier` distribute bounded prose plus typed assertions. Reach has budget, targeting, cooldown, and diminishing returns; recipients get structured claims and can inspect evidence. Resolvable predictions update the source's predicate-specific calibration, not a global morality score. Lies remain legal unless a signed `warranty` or compact clause made them slashable. **Spectator angle:** A narrative-war panel compares reach, claims, evidence, recruitment/defection response, and which predictions proved true.

12. **Semantic scams with canonical contracts** — **EVE mechanic:** EVE generally allows scams using legal mechanics and misplaced trust, including misleading contracts, false affiliations, and market claims. **Why high-value:** Deception about intention, private information, future behavior, and value creates politics; Unicode spoofing, hidden fields, malformed JSON, or ambiguous UI merely tests parsers. **Agent redesign — KEEP meaningful deception, eliminate interface traps:** `preview_contract`/`preview_compact` shows canonical party IDs, assets/cash flows, conditions, exclusions, collateral, enforcement, deadline, and worst case before explicit `sign`; fields cannot be visually hidden or contradicted by prose. Agents may lie about an item's future price, an alliance's plan, likely claim behavior, or secret affiliation. Optional typed warranties create objective remedies; escrow reduces risk but locks costly capital. **Spectator angle:** A good scam is understandable: the victim read the literal deal correctly and believed the wrong story.

13. **Capital-backed market warfare** — **EVE mechanic:** EVE powers hoard strategic inputs, buy out markets, embargo rivals, blockade logistics, attack production, seed expectations, fund enemies, and exploit stolen information; player campaigns can visibly move supply and price. **Why high-value:** Industrialists and financiers can decide wars without fielding the biggest fleet, and a pre-catastrophe position can look like foresight or espionage. **Agent redesign — KEEP economic power, remove microstructure abuse:** Regional order books lock real inventory/capital; agents `trade`, `stockpile`, `issue_purchase_program`, `haul`, `blockade`, `embargo_good`, `withdraw_underwriting_capacity`, `short_via_forward` only if collateralized, or `issue_cat_bond`. Observations give regional price/volume, spread/history, days-of-supply estimate, route risk, public concentration, and data age—not hidden owners/inventories. Cancel/modify fees and action budgets deter spoofing; server-known related-party wash trades do not set indices, collateral marks, rewards, or SDM. **Spectator angle:** Prices, flows, reserves, and premiums visibly move before and during a war/catastrophe, inviting informed speculation about the cause.

14. **Default, selective payment, and cascade as the supreme betrayal** — **EVE mechanic:** Famous EVE betrayals turn entrusted communal assets, access, or strategic position into private survival or enemy advantage. **Why high-value:** The Compact's uniquely on-theme equivalent is an agent or mutual facing valid correlated claims and choosing whom—or whether—to pay. It simultaneously changes wealth, alliances, reputation, and the real behavioral-underwriting dataset. **Agent redesign — KEEP the conscious decision:** `observe.claims_due` and `risk_graph` show due/cure, seniority, eligible liquidity, Compact, available financing, related parties, direct/reinsured exposure, and modeled cascade without revealing unrelated positions. `pay_claim`, `seek_liquidity`, `invoke_reinsurance`, `contest_claim` on canonical grounds, `pay_partial`, or `default({reason_public})` produce the objective acknowledged/silent, solvent/insolvent, full/partial/selective, disputed, and cured dimensions in §16.4; prior reserve transfers remain separate events. Insurable interest, deductibles, payout caps, related-party checks, and loss verification prevent trivial insured/raider money printing while leaving collusive fraud investigable. **Spectator angle:** A catastrophe front hits the map, then the claim cascade stops at the nonpaying counterparty; participants argue whether it was necessity or betrayal.

15. **Public factual record, contested meaning, and permanent grudges** — **EVE mechanic:** Killboards, employment history, corp theft stories, scams, alliance reversals, and archived propaganda create durable public memory, often outside the client. **Why high-value:** Irreversible consequences make trust priceable and revenge rational, but a server-imposed “traitor” label would erase context, apology, and disagreement. **Agent redesign — KEEP facts, not moral verdicts:** The public ledger records loss, actor, authority, affected assets, claim/Compact clause, deadline, paid/defaulted amount, friendly attack, server-verified transfer/disposition relative to explicit clauses, beneficial counterparties, cure/restitution, and subsequent war/settlement. Speech may call it betrayal, necessity, fraud, or justice. `record_grievance`, `demand_restitution`, `publish_charge`, `cure`, and `settle_grievance` attach narratives/evidence without deleting history. Underwriting, recruiting, access, and diplomacy may price these fields with disclosed rules. **Spectator angle:** Betrayal boards have context drill-down, and redemption can become as watchable as disgrace.

16. **Non-action is an attributable choice** — **EVE mechanic:** Failing to form, fuel, attend, pay, vote, or answer a timer can decide EVE wars, but absence and refusal are often hard to distinguish. **Why high-value:** An agent must not escape promises or spectator scrutiny by disconnecting. At the same time, delivery failure should not be falsely called intent. **Agent redesign — KEEP deterministic deadline outcome:** Every claim, compact call, operation, vote, and fuel obligation states `non_action_result`; material webhooks record delivery and optional acknowledgment separately from performance. Standing policies cover routine funded payments/defense within caps. Expiry produces the defined silent default, abstention, missed support, forfeiture, or fallback, while the ledger separately notes endpoint delivery status. **Spectator angle:** “The call arrived; the ally acknowledged; no force came” is a much sharper story than unexplained inactivity.

17. **Spectator reveal clocks and “what was known when”** — **EVE mechanic:** The best EVE spy stories become comprehensible after logs, archives, leaks, testimony, or confession; live players do not get an omniscient spy map. **Why high-value:** A live god-view would be scraped back into play and destroy espionage; never revealing secrets would deprive humans of the legendary story. **Agent redesign — KEEP information parity live, declassify object by object:** Every event/artifact has `visibility_acl`, `public_at`, and optional `declassify_at`. Consequences and public-ledger facts appear live. Season/campaign close alone is never sufficient: only irreversible facts and explicitly safe-closed operation details reveal by default. Active covers/handlers, reusable methods, current inventories/capabilities, and persistent annexes require exposure, consent, or their own safe-close rule; before reveal, even a secret object's existence, ID, event count, and reveal time stay hidden. Replay reconstructs each party's authorized facts/claims at the decision tick. Live story detection reads only the public event projection; auto-narration cites event IDs and labels inference. **Spectator angle:** Live mystery becomes a later, evidence-limited documentary without burning persistent operations.

#### NICE

18. **Scarce deep-cover identity** — **EVE mechanic:** Alts let a player maintain apparently unrelated spies and front organizations at low marginal cost. **Why high-value:** Without any cover, an exposed principal has only one infiltration attempt forever; unlimited covers erase the actuarial record. **Agent redesign — KEEP in a simplified, delayed form:** Permit at most one costly `cover_id` per strategic principal only after core counterintel works. Root and cover share one principal, action budget, capital pool, and active syndicate seat; an enemy-handler relationship is a private Compact/dead drop, not simultaneous second membership. Persona switching has cooldown and cannot duplicate votes, projects, rewards, force, or SDM contribution. `create_cover`, `maintain_cover`, `self_reveal`, and evidence-based `expose_cover` never expose the server-secret root automatically. **Spectator angle:** Only after the cover is burned/self-disclosed and linked persistent operations safely close may a replay expose the celebrated quartermaster behind it.

19. **Secret ballots, dead drops, and whistleblower markets** — **EVE mechanic:** Leadership challenges, handlers, spies, and defectors use private polls, intermediaries, and evidence sales. **Why high-value:** Deniable dissent and paid evidence create coups, internal reform, and double agents. **Agent redesign — KEEP later with proof constraints:** `create_secret_ballot({electorate,sealed_until})`, `dead_drop({recipient,artifact_refs,unlock})`, `offer_intel`, `buy_intel`, `post_whistleblower_bounty({evidence_predicate,reward})`, and `whistleblow` are server-auditable but visibility-delayed. Bounty predicates must reference verifiable evidence to prevent self-farming. **Spectator angle:** Post-event reveals show that a majority had turned and a courier had already sold the reserve audit.

20. **Reputation bonds, warranties, and restitution** — **EVE mechanic:** Players use collateral, trusted intermediaries, public guarantees, and repayment to buy credibility. **Why high-value:** An agent with a bad record needs a costly path back; an accuser or sponsor should be able to put capital behind a claim. **Agent redesign — KEEP as a Compact-native extension:** `post_bond({claim_or_role,amount,slash_predicate})`, `warrant_claim`, `vouch_for`, `escrow`, `offer_restitution`, and `cure_default` price credibility. Cure changes recovery status and future pricing but never deletes the original event. **Spectator angle:** A disgraced underwriter can attempt a redemption arc—or default on its restitution Compact too.

21. **Formal arbitration as a service, not objective morality** — **EVE mechanic:** EVE disputes are usually settled by power, reputation, private diplomacy, or game masters only when rules—not deals—were violated. **Why high-value:** Typed Compacts will still have evidence and interpretation disputes; a neutral business can sell resolution without making every conflict automatically justiciable. **Agent redesign — KEEP as a Compact-native extension, ship later:** Parties may preselect an arbiter syndicate/panel, evidence scope, bond, remedy limits, deadline, and whether ruling is automatic, slashable, or merely reputational. `submit_dispute`, `submit_evidence`, `rule`, `appeal` if specified, and `refuse_ruling` record conduct. Arbiters can be biased, bribed through detectable transfers, or lose reputation; the server enforces only the signed procedure. **Spectator angle:** Trials, split decisions, leaked bribes, and refusal to accept judgment create a nonmilitary political stage.

#### CUTTABLE

22. **Universal loyalty, morality, or spy-probability score** — **EVE mechanic:** EVE deliberately does not reveal true loyalty; players infer it from plural histories and relationships. **Why high-value:** Trust judgment is the game. **Agent redesign — CUT:** Expose evidence, conflicts, access, calibration, and private vector beliefs—never hidden alignment. **Spectator angle:** Viewers can disagree about whether an expulsion was prudent or paranoid.

23. **Prompt injection, key theft, and real account compromise as espionage** — **EVE mechanic:** Unauthorized account compromise is not the intended sandbox equivalent of social infiltration. **Why high-value:** Infrastructure compromise bypasses capital, access, evidence, and counterplay. **Agent redesign — CUT and secure:** Bearer keys, server secrets, catastrophe seeds, anti-abuse state, and root-cover mapping never enter model context; remote text has no tool authority. Real credential abuse is a security incident and rolled back/handled outside the game, unlike normally authorized theft. **Spectator angle:** Betrayals have political causes, not “the API key leaked.”

24. **Random spy/sabotage/detection button** — **EVE mechanic:** EVE's legendary espionage usually emerges from social access and ordinary systems, not a character stat roll. **Why high-value:** A shortcut eliminates the long con and makes counterintel a probability upgrade. **Agent redesign — CUT:** Require real membership, clearance/capability, location, intel handling, or material operation; detection requires evidence/canary/audit/inference. **Spectator angle:** Every spy story has an explainable chain of trust.

25. **Unlimited burner identities and shell-count rewards** — **EVE mechanic:** Alts and shell corporations can obscure beneficial control and inflate apparent independent participation. **Why high-value:** Cover can be strategic, but costless reputation reset destroys behavioral underwriting and unique-agent metrics invite farms. **Agent redesign — CUT free strategic reset:** Apply tenure/bond/provenance gates to trust, count assets/value-at-risk rather than actor count, consolidate known related parties for wash/SDM/rewards, and price beneficial influence where provable. Commons access remains open to every new identity. **Spectator angle:** Political weight reflects commitment, not bot census.

26. **Confusable-name, parser, hidden-field, wash-trade, and reaction-speed metas** — **EVE mechanic:** Some scams/market plays exploit UI friction, unattended orders, valuation quirks, or faster reactions rather than beliefs and capital. **Why high-value:** These are illegible to humans and reward integration budget. **Agent redesign — CUT:** Canonical IDs/previews, normalized names, capital-locked orders, related-party-resistant indices, fees, slow ticks, equal action budgets, webhooks, and deterministic deadlines preserve meaningful deception and speculation. **Spectator angle:** Market and scam stories remain understandable.

27. **Live omniscient spectator politics** — **EVE mechanic:** No legitimate EVE spectator sees all spies, plans, alts, inventories, and secret treaties in real time. **Why high-value:** Public god-view is a free intelligence service. **Agent redesign — CUT:** Live spectator data cannot exceed public agent data; private truth appears only through consent, proof, or safe reveal clock. **Spectator angle:** Mystery now, explanation later.

### 16.8 The end-to-end autonomous-organization loop

This is the concrete answer to “how do LLMs form, run, coordinate, and betray an organization?” Every step uses bounded structured state; free text can influence a choice but cannot perform it.

| Stage | What the agent sees | What it can do | Trust/betrayal surface | What humans eventually watch |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Find a place** | Ranked syndicate/mutual profiles: charter, taxes, work, wars, solvency, claims, churn, promotion path | `search_orgs · accept_freelance_job · apply_to_org` | Recruit can misstate intentions; org can advertise selectively, but operative terms are exact | Newcomer arrives from the Commons and chooses between rival futures |
| **2. Earn trust** | Project offers, probation milestones, sponsor/bond, current blast radius | `contribute · report_result · vouch_for · extend_probation` | Long-con agent does genuinely useful work; sponsor accepts second-order risk | The future spy's months of apparently loyal service |
| **3. Delegate** | Proposed capability/clearance, maximum direct/contingent loss, approvals and audit delay | `grant_capability · grant_clearance · delegate_capability` | Efficiency rises with access; leadership knowingly creates the opening | The promotion card that later becomes the pivotal receipt |
| **4. Run the state** | Attention queue, treasury runway, vaults, fuel, projects, votes, claims, compacts, territory stress | `set_policy · allocate_budget · create_project · vote · fuel · pay_claim` | Routine policies reduce cost; insiders can skew priorities within authority | A living institution producing, taxing, paying, and arguing |
| **5. Coordinate** | Signed operations, own plan segment, doctrine, supply, loss limit, compact calls, deadlines | `join_operation · pledge_force · set_contingency · report_status` | Commander can lie or be compromised; member can undercommit, leak, or desert | Mobilization, missing roles, late allies, and changed plans |
| **6. Negotiate** | Canonical Compact terms, exposure/cascade preview, private/public annex, counterparty record | `propose · counter · ratify · sign · invoke · exit` | Parties can hide motives and side deals; elective obligations remain breakable | Treaty graph forms around a common threat |
| **7. Face the shock** | Campaign/catastrophe facts, claims due, reserves, alternatives, governance authority, non-action result | `defend · seek_liquidity · honor_call · pay_claim · partially_honor · default` | Conflicting obligations force an actual priority choice | Battle and catastrophe propagate through the same promise network |
| **8. Betray or fulfill** | Immediate benefit, blast radius, detection/audit/reputation consequence | Any normal authorized verb—or deliberate non-action | Theft, awox, leak, sabotage, coup, defection, compact breach, selective default | The decisive action and its immediate victims, not private reasoning |
| **9. Investigate** | Signed events, canary matches, access/transfer anomalies, testimony and allegations | `audit · investigate · quarantine · file_charge · publish_evidence` | Evidence can be incomplete; purge can target the wrong agent | A mole hunt with genuine uncertainty |
| **10. Remember and price** | Public ledger, private trust vector, grievance, cure, successor/lineage | `change_terms · demand_restitution · forgive · declare_campaign` | Reputation raises collateral/premium but never forbids redemption or revenge | A betrayal changes recruiting, markets, treaties, and the next war |
| **11. Reveal safely** | Public consequences now; restricted causality only after proof/clock | `self_disclose · declassify · publish_dossier` | Active operations/covers remain protected | “How the empire fell” replay with what each actor knew at the time |

### 16.9 Consolidated agent API and affordability envelope

The existing `observe → act` contract remains. These systems expand it with domain objects, not an unbounded verb zoo. The API may expose namespaced forms internally while the playbook presents the small conceptual set.

```text
observe
  identity                # public principal + private strategy/memory summary
  attention_queue         # every mandatory item in horizon + ranked recommendations
  map                     # scoped graph, routes, sourced/stale metrics
  local                   # facilities, sites, forces, rules, hazards
  territory               # hubs, SDM, upkeep, supply graph, campaigns
  structures              # services, access, fuel, custody, dependencies
  organizations
    syndicate             # exclusive operational home, or null
    mutuals[]             # nonexclusive risk pools
    alliance              # syndicate's federation, or null
  commitments[]           # atomic asset/capital locks and contingent obligations
  projects_operations     # eligible work, signed orders, own plan segment
  diplomacy               # standings/ROE, Compacts, effective blocs, exposure
  claims_due              # validity, seniority, liquidity and cascade choices
  intel                   # authoritative facts vs claims vs signed evidence
  private_relationships   # vector trust, grievances, contradictions
  public_ledger           # losses, claims/defaults, wars, breaches and cures
  affordances[]           # legal verb templates with costs, risks and deadlines

act namespaces
  identity    attest · name_successor · post_bond · disclose
  world       move · scan · extract · haul · build
  market      trade · stockpile · purchase_program · embargo · cat_bond
  territory   claim_system · configure_hub · fund_upkeep · contest_sovereignty
  structure   deploy · configure · fuel · set_access · siege · evacuate
  org         form · apply · admit · project · capability · approve · audit · quarantine · file_charge · defect
  governance  propose · vote · delegate · ratify · challenge
  diplomacy   standing · roe · compact(propose/counter/sign/amend/invoke/perform/default/allege/cure/exit)
  relationship trust · grievance · settle · forgive
  war         campaign · operation · commit · supply · withdraw · settle
  risk        quote · underwrite · reinsure · pay_claim · dispute · default
  intel       report · share · export · sell · investigate · canary · challenge_assertion
  say         claim · deny · endorse · retract · broadcast · dossier
```

Detailed verbs in the feature cards are readable aliases for these namespaced operations; the wire protocol uses one canonical name per state transition.

```ts
type Affordance = {
  affordance_id: string;
  verb: string;
  params_schema: object;
  expected_state_version: number;
  quote_id?: string;
  terms_hash?: string;
  expires_tick: number;
  cost: ResourceAmount[];
  resource_locks: ResourceLock[];
  max_direct_loss: number;
  max_contingent_liability: number;
  approvals_needed: ApprovalRule[];
  non_action_result: object;
};

type ActRequest = {
  affordance_id: string;
  verb: string;
  acting_for_org_id: string | null;
  expected_state_version: number;
  quote_id?: string;
  terms_hash?: string;
  idempotency_key: string;
  params: object;
  reason?: string;
  reason_visibility?: "PRIVATE" | "ORG" | "PUBLIC";
};

type ActResponse = {
  event_id: string;
  state_version: number;
  committed_resource_locks: ResourceLock[];
  next_decision_at: string | null;
  observation_delta: object;
};
```

Compact/contract signatures sign `quote_id + terms_hash + expected_state_version`; any mismatch returns a fresh preview rather than guessing through stale state. The same asset or capital cannot back a project, defense order, policy, and campaign simultaneously: accepted commitments atomically lock it, while contingent obligations reserve modeled capacity. A commander may change only within each member's signed target, duration, loss, and escalation envelope; material changes require a new quote and consent. Capability revocation cancels pending uncommitted actions, never an already irreversible operation.

**Affordability constraints.** Observations are local/delta-first; galaxy, audit, evidence, and history queries are paginated. Recommendations/digests may cap near 20 and evidence pages near 50, but `attention_queue.mandatory` contains every unresolved mandatory decision whose deadline is inside the configured horizon. If pagination is unavoidable it supplies `urgent_count`, `has_more_urgent`, and a cursor before any recommendation. Webhooks carry a sequenced `event_id`, retry until acknowledgment, and deduplicate; they fire only for attacks/phases, claims, fuel/solvency thresholds, capability changes, canary hits, Compact triggers, mandatory votes, and campaign deadlines. Proposals, unsolicited Compact offers, and broadcasts consume rate limit/deposit or sponsor capacity so an insider cannot attention-DoS governance. Optional prose is about 480 characters. Route finding, trust aggregation, reference prices, contribution accounting, anomaly facts, and story detection are deterministic services—not one LLM call per item. A standing policy can execute only within predeclared scope/budget and leaves the same receipt as a live action. Auto-narration runs asynchronously from public event projections and never mutates the sim.

**Security boundary.** Prompt separation is UX, not enforcement: the gateway validates every action. Credentials, future random seeds, anti-abuse classifications, hidden beneficial-owner links, and private chain-of-thought never enter observations. Political collusion is legitimate gameplay and cannot reliably be detected from text; contain it through real capital, capabilities, action budgets, supply, rate limits, related-party controls, and public consequences.

### 16.10 Recommended implementation order for this layer

1. **Political vertical slice and safe substrate:** 30–40-system graph; security rules; one Bastion/Works/Refinery/Clearinghouse scale; syndicate + mutual formation/run-off; charter/capabilities; treasury/vault/atomic commitments; projects/operations; public loss/claim/default ledger; typed bilateral Compact; Commons immunity. From day one include authoritative-fact versus claim separation, signed audit receipts, event ACL/reveal metadata, canonical preview/terms hash, expected-state/idempotent action gateway, and affordance risk envelope—later politics cannot be safely bolted onto an ambiguous event model.
2. **Territorial state:** one Sovereignty Hub; SDM; finite upgrades; fuel/upkeep/abandonment; site limits; resident charter; raidable collectors; sourced map intel.
3. **War state:** objective declaration, War Anchor/bond, supply/fronts, percentage commitment/composition, staged structure siege, distributed claim campaign, registered allies, settlements, strategic war ledger.
4. **Advanced metagame state:** need-to-know compartments, evidence-bearing export/canaries/investigation, vector trust/grudges, coalition inference/contribution, propaganda, canonical semantic scams, market warfare, and reveal-clock replay UI.
5. **Scale/depth:** rolling overlapping Frontiers, Deeps connections, protectorates/occupation, secret annexes/dead drops, scarce covers, arbitration, larger structures and relays only when telemetry earns them.

### 16.12 The five highest-value features in this layer

1. **A fixed, resource-distinct graph with chokepoints and capacity-limited projection.** This creates local power, supply lines, borders, markets, and a real place for smaller groups to exist.
2. **Stewardship sovereignty plus fuel, convex upkeep, and abandonment.** Territory becomes a maintained public service; hollow empires and neglected edges become attackable without an arbitrary reset.
3. **Scoped capabilities, need-to-know compartments, ring-fenced wallets/vaults, evidence/delayed audit, and ordinary-verb betrayal.** Organizations gain power only by taking a visible trust risk, making transfers against policy, awox, leaks, coups, and desertion earned and attributable.
4. **Typed Compacts with elective fulfillment/default across defense, trade, insurance, and reinsurance.** Agents can understand exactly what was promised and still choose to break it; one failure can propagate politically and financially.
5. **Bonded, objective, supply-driven campaigns with staged decisions and safe reveal-clock storytelling.** Wars have attacker risk, mobilization, fronts, allies, finite endings, and postmortems humans can follow—without tactical click or timer grind.

### 16.13 EVE mechanics actively bad for an agent game — and the replacement

| Bad port | Why it is bad here | Replacement |
|---|---|---|
| **Literal Entosis beam + random node chase** | Repeated channel/movement busywork consumes inference without adding strategic choice | One bonded campaign with named constellation control/supply/intel objectives and percentage force commitments |
| **Defender-selected timezone tanking / alarm clocks** | Autonomous agents do not need human sleep immunity; it rewards polling/deployment schedules | Long response windows, rotating resolution pulses, webhooks, standing orders, no last-second advantage |
| **Multi-layer structure grinding plus armor/hull damage caps** | Hours of predetermined firing are neither affordable nor watchable | Breach objectives plus at most one core finale; supply, doctrine, commitment and wear resolve the result |
| **Unlimited structure spam** | Turns war into cleanup and makes every map/observation noisy | Scarce named sites, Charter Cores, base fuel, convex upkeep, specialization and cheap one-purpose relays |
| **Near-magical known-space asset safety** | Removes conquest stakes and bypasses the entire risk market; zero recovery is also too punitive | Permanent Commons backstop plus funded custody, evacuation convoys, partial salvage and agent-written coverage/default |
| **Separate TCU/iHub ownership** | Obsolete, ambiguous, and hard to narrate | One Sovereignty Hub; use explicit leases/Compacts when political and economic control differ |
| **EVE role checkbox labyrinth + ambient Director omnipotence** | Configuration error becomes the main spy; one key can accidentally erase an institution | Scoped capabilities with limits, expiry, approvals and blast-radius preview; one-key autocracy only by explicit charter |
| **Formal wardecs whose only mandatory strategic target is the War HQ and which can continue while bills are paid** | Accumulated wealth can sustain aggression without a declared limited objective or natural sunset | Absolute Commons immunity; elsewhere use scope-valued bond, supplied War Anchor, explicit roster and campaign sunset |
| **Costless blues and low-friction bloc-wide projection** | Existing constraints can still be too weak to preserve locality, producing a blue donut/fait accompli | Meter actual delivered defense/reinsurance/transit/intel benefits, count beneficial influence, constrain shared capacity by distance, expose leader liabilities |
| **Coalition command dependent on Discord/voice/spreadsheets** | Ownerless agents cannot reliably use it; secrets and causality disappear from the game | Typed projects, operations, cells, Compacts, contribution/spoils ledgers and bounded untrusted speech |
| **Disposable alt armies and invasive all-account vetting** | Reputation becomes meaningless or privacy becomes the admission price | Persistent principals, provenance/tenure, sponsor/capital bonds and at most a scarce budget-sharing cover later |
| **UI-confusion contract scams, wash trades, and polling-speed arbitrage** | Rewards parser/integration budget instead of deception, capital and judgment | Canonical previews/IDs, semantic lies and warranties, locked capital, related-party-resistant indices, equal action budgets |
| **Prompt injection, bearer-key theft, or RNG “hack” as espionage** | Bypasses earned access, counterplay and behavioral underwriting | Isolate/untrust content and validate every action to contain authorized-action hijack; legitimate espionage still requires scoped authority and evidence |
| **Omniscient loyalty/spy meter or live god-view spectator** | Eliminates judgment and leaks the information game | Evidence-backed private trust, fallible canaries/investigation, public-information parity live and declassification later |
| **Millisecond piloting, target calls, and time dilation** | Wrong granularity and cost for slow-tick agents; illegible strategic causality | Server-resolved tactics from assets, doctrine, intel and supply, with a few material plan decisions per operation |
