# THE COMPACT — Ships, Fitting, Combat, Fleets, and Escalation

## Executive decision

Do **not** reduce ships to one `combat_power` number. Preserve EVE's three linked games: (1) fitting a hull under hard constraints, (2) assembling a doctrine whose roles cover one another, and (3) choosing when to commit, hold, disengage, or escalate assets that can be permanently lost. Remove only continuous piloting, click timing, and catalog trivia.

The recommended abstraction is a six-phase declared operation—`ASSEMBLE → APPROACH → CONTACT → EXCHANGE → BREAK → AFTERMATH`. The server simulates weapon, repair, capacitor, EWAR, heat, ammunition, and tackle cycles inside each world tick. Agents normally decide at declaration and at material phase changes; standing policies handle unchanged cycles. Ships remain unique, fitted, located, manufactured assets, and losses still destroy the actual hull, rigs, charges, drones, and cargo.

Ranks are local to each subsystem: **MUST** is required for the first rich version, **NICE** should follow after the core is stable, and **CUTTABLE** is safe to omit or explicitly replace. Decisions are **KEEP**, **SIMPLIFY**, or **CUT**.

The catalog is ordered outside-in: strategic projection and escalation first, then fleets, engagement resolution, modules, fitting, and finally the hull ecology those layers act upon.

### Canonical machine-facing combat state

Every feature below should project into a shared compact schema rather than inventing another verb. A force observation should expose:

- `assets[]`: hull/fit/doctrine IDs, size, role tags, readiness, location, replacement value, insurance, charges, heat damage, and confidence/visibility;
- `profiles`: EHP and repair by damage vector, speed/signature/escape, lock speed/range/channels, capacitor endurance, tackle, EWAR, logistics, command, cargo and fuel;
- `application_matrix`: expected damage or effect by range band and target size/signature/speed archetype, with provenance and uncertainty;
- `operation`: phase, objective, frontage, engagement bands, composition by role, supply, reserves, arrivals, tackle/control state, projected wear/loss bands, and next decision trigger;
- `affordances[]`: legal phase changes with resource locks, quote expiry, maximum direct/contingent loss, counterfactual deltas, and non-action result.

The core write surface is deliberately small: `fit`, `publish_doctrine`, `create_operation`, `pledge_force`, `set_engagement_plan`, `set_target_policy`, `set_logistics_policy`, `set_capacitor_policy`, `set_heat_budget`, `reinforce_operation`, `change_posture`, `escalate`, and `withdraw_force`. Module activation details are parameters or standing policies, not hundreds of new top-level verbs.

## 1. Interdiction, strategic mobility, capitals, and escalation

### MUST

1. **Interdictor probes, HIC fields/focused points, anchored bubbles, and nullification** — In EVE interdictors deploy temporary warp-disruption fields; heavy interdictors sustain area fields or focus effectively unlimited-strength tackle on capitals; mobile bubbles shape routes; eligible nullifiers temporarily bypass some area fields but not focused tackle. **Why high-value:** Cheap specialists give chokepoints and extraction teeth and can hold assets thousands of times their value, while escape packages preserve counterplay. **Agent redesign — KEEP EFFECTS, ABSTRACT GEOMETRY:** In Frontier/Deeps, attach fields to `route_edge`, `arrival_vector`, or `objective_ring`. Observe source, coverage, affected classes/routes, duration, friendly obstruction, nullification, clear cost, and alternatives. `deploy_interdiction`, `sustain_field`, `focus_hold`, `clear_field`, `reserve_nullification`, and `route_around` resolve per phase. **Spectator angle:** A translucent net closes around exits and names the HIC holding the titan.

2. **Covert operations, cloaks, covert cynos, and Black Ops bridges** — EVE covert-ops scouts, stealth bombers, force recons, blockade runners, certain strategic cruisers, and Black Ops use cloaks and restricted covert beacons/bridges to ambush or infiltrate. **Why high-value:** Hidden composition, bait, back doors, and specialist projection create paranoia and make recon/counter-recon valuable. **Agent redesign — KEEP OBSERVER-SCOPED UNCERTAINTY:** Cloaked forces emit possible traces based on movement/emissions, sensors, and time; they are never globally listed. `recon`, `cloak({emissions_budget})`, `light_cyno({type:"COVERT"})`, `bridge_force`, and `ambush` trade concealment for speed/output and expose a beacon window. Hidden exact assets reveal only through sensors, use, loss, consent, or safe replay. **Spectator angle:** Live viewers get the same fog; replay later reveals the covert staging route.

3. **Cynosural beacons as vulnerable temporary graph edges** — EVE jump-capable ships need a destination cyno/beacon; the beacon ship is exposed, consumes fuel, and determines who can arrive. Ordinary, covert, and industrial cynos differ in visibility and eligibility. **Why high-value:** Cynos create hotdrops, bait, rescue, counterdrop, and an attackable dependency rather than free teleportation. **Agent redesign — KEEP, COMPRESS TO `MILITARY | COVERT | INDUSTRIAL`:** Each beacon states spool, duration, visibility, mass/class capacity, fuel, jamming, source survival, arrival scatter, and extraction use. `light_cyno`, `defend_beacon`, `inhibit_cyno`, `jump_force`, `hotdrop`, and `counterdrop` lock real assets. Killing the source before activation or controlling arrival changes the operation. **Spectator angle:** A system flare and force-flow line announce detected escalation; covert edges remain hidden until observed.

4. **Jump drives, bridges, conduit jumps, range, fuel, and cooldown** — EVE capitals/Black Ops jump to beacons; titans/Black Ops bridge eligible fleets; some ships conduit-jump groups. Range, isotopes/fuel, jump activation, fatigue, and infrastructure limit repeated projection. **Why high-value:** Strategic mobility lets empires form interior lines but can erase geography if unconstrained. **Agent redesign — KEEP PHYSICAL MOVEMENT, REPLACE TIMER SLUDGE:** `observe.projection` gives reachable nodes, weight/class eligibility, fuel, shared network/force capacity, `ready_at`, distance cost, beacon/jammer risk, extraction, and queued uses. `reserve_projection`, `jump_force`, `bridge_force`, `conduit_jump`, `cancel_reservation`, and `reroute` operate on real staged hulls/cargo. Use a visible force/network pool plus per-hull readiness, not hundreds of exponentially divergent character timers. **Spectator angle:** A draining projection network shows why the empire answers one front but cannot answer the next.

5. **Cyno beacons, jammers, inhibitors, and jump infrastructure** — EVE infrastructure can provide permanent beacons, suppress ordinary cynos, connect jump gates, or deploy local inhibitors; attackers sabotage the network before escalation. **Why high-value:** Capital access becomes a territorial service and raid objective, and defenders can shape—but not make invulnerable—their home field. **Agent redesign — KEEP:** `projection.infrastructure` exposes owner, access, fuel, capacity, online/spool, coverage, vulnerability, and alternatives. `deploy_beacon`, `online_jammer`, `deploy_inhibitor`, `sabotage_projection`, and `repair_network` are multi-tick physical operations. Covert or industrial exceptions remain typed, not guessed. **Spectator angle:** The map shows which reinforcement corridors are open, jammed, or being sabotaged.

6. **Distinct capital role ladder** — EVE's capital ecosystem separates dreadnought siege damage, FAX repair, carrier fighters, command-carrier support, supercarrier heavy fighters/control, titan bridge/superweapon, Rorqual industrial command, and jump-freighter supply. **Why high-value:** Escalation becomes a composition decision and subcapital tackle, screens, recon, EWAR, and supply remain indispensable. **Agent redesign — KEEP STRICT ROLE PACKAGES:** Capital profiles are `SIEGE/ANTI_CAPITAL`, `CAPITAL_LOGISTICS`, `FIGHTER_PROJECTION`, `CAPITAL_COMMAND`, `HEAVY_FIGHTERS/AREA_CONTROL`, `BRIDGE/STRATEGIC_SALVO`, `INDUSTRIAL_COMMAND`, and `STRATEGIC_SUPPLY`. Observe application limits, fuel, support needs, commitment, berth/custody, replacement and coverage. `stage_capital` and `pledge_capital_package` commit named hulls. **Spectator angle:** “Six dreads, two FAX, one command carrier” conveys a plan rather than “nine capitals.”

7. **Dreadnought siege and anti-capital/structure commitment** — EVE siege massively increases dread damage/local tank and locks the hull in place, generally blocking ordinary remote assistance and escape until the cycle ends. **Why high-value:** It is the cleanest power-for-commitment bargain: an irreversible public bet that invites a counterdrop. **Agent redesign — KEEP:** `enter_siege({target_class, pulses, max_loss, exit_beacon})` locks the asset; observe damage/application, local-repair/cap endurance, fuel, support restriction, extraction pulse, HIC/bubble state, and hostile reserve bands. Large guns do not become efficient anti-frigate weapons. **Spectator angle:** Siege rings close and a counter-escalation clock starts.

8. **Force Auxiliary triage and capital logistics** — EVE FAXes enter triage for extreme remote repair and local resilience while becoming fixed, cap-intensive, and restricted from ordinary outside help. **Why high-value:** Triage creates rescue drops and repair-anchor choices, but a stranded FAX becomes a historic kill. **Agent redesign — KEEP:** `enter_triage({pulses, repair_priority, cap_policy, exit_plan})` opens a named capital-repair queue; expose time-to-lock, effective repair after impedance, cap/fuel, coverage, EWAR/hold resistance, and what fails if one FAX dies. Stacking FAXes gives redundancy/target spread, not linear immortality on one primary. **Spectator angle:** Capital repair links and the triage clock make “saved or trapped” watchable.

9. **Carrier fighter squadrons and support fighters** — EVE carriers launch independently targetable squadrons through limited tubes; light/space-superiority/support/heavy fighters attack different classes or web, point, jam, and neut, while attrition depletes the squadron and reserve bay. **Why high-value:** A carrier projects power but opponents can attrit its tools before killing the hull; screens and replacement logistics matter. **Agent redesign — KEEP SQUADRONS, CUT UNIT MICRO:** Each squadron has role, HP/count, travel pulses, application, ability cooldown, control range, replacement stock, and parent link. `launch_sortie`, `assign_space_superiority`, `recall_sortie`, `rearm_fighters`, and `target_parent` resolve once per pulse. **Spectator angle:** Squadron streams thin, recall, and leave the carrier exposed.

10. **Command carriers as apex support, not apex DPS** — EVE's June 2026 command carriers fit multiple strong command bursts, field support fighters, and use capital-scale micro-jump/conduit tools. **Why high-value:** They create a flagship whose loss breaks command, control, and mobility rather than merely removing a big damage number. **Agent redesign — KEEP LATE:** A command carrier provides bounded `COMMAND`, `DISRUPTION`, and `MOBILITY`, with 2–3 nonstacking boost profiles and limited support sorties; it cannot also dominate `ASSAULT`. Observe boost coverage, fighter effects, displacement/conduit charges, redundancy, headshot impact, and extraction. **Spectator angle:** Make the ship the visible heart of the formation and the obvious high-value primary.

11. **Supercarriers, heavy fighters, and burst projectors** — EVE supercarriers are enormous fighter reserves with anti-capital heavy fighters and area-control projectors, limited strategic mobility, and immense loss value. **Why high-value:** They are deterrents and alliance treasures whose appearance can force another escalation rung. **Agent redesign — KEEP ROLE-BOUND:** Heavy fighters attack capitals/structures; area effects select one telegraphed control package with long cooldown and formation/friendly risk. Require staging, maintenance, fuel, support screens, HIC escape counter, and governance/risk approval. No efficient routine subcapital farming. **Spectator angle:** A galaxy alert, fighter reserve, and insured/uninsured exposure emphasize the political bet.

12. **Titans: bridges, capital fire, doomsdays, and flagship risk** — EVE titans bridge fleets, wield capital weapons and rare strategic superweapons, and represent extraordinary concentrated industry. **Why high-value:** A titan decision can mobilize an invasion or become a world-history loss. **Agent redesign — KEEP FUNCTIONS SEPARATE:** `BRIDGE`, `CAPITAL_FIRE`, and `STRATEGIC_SALVO` use different fuel, cooldown, commitment, target solution, support, and exit. A doomsday has a detected charge-up and weight/signature/objective eligibility; it never deletes arbitrary light fleets. A bridge consumes shared projection capacity and exposes the destination. **Spectator angle:** Reserve cinematic treatment for the rare charge and connect every shot to the titan's risk.

13. **Capital escalation ladder, counterdrop, and reserve policy** — EVE fights can escalate from subcaps through carriers/FAX, dread bombs, supers, and titans; defenders maintain umbrellas while attackers bait, jam cynos, or counterdrop. **Why high-value:** This is a liquidity, intel, logistics, and nerve game. Refusing to risk the reserve can rationally concede a limited objective. **Agent redesign — KEEP:** Operations publish a public escalation ceiling; sides privately stage conditional packages. `observe.escalation` shows current rung, sourced hostile reserve band, cyno/jammer state, response time, own fuel/capacity, home/other-front exposure, coverage, and counterdrop loss bands. `set_escalation_policy`, `drop_capitals`, `counterdrop`, `decline_escalation`, `recall_umbrella`, and `open_diversion` lock real assets. **Spectator angle:** Escalation rungs reveal a bluff becoming a capital commitment.

14. **Capital application limits and subcapital dependency** — EVE has repeatedly adjusted capital/high-angle weapons and fighter application so capitals do not counter every smaller hull while also being the best capital killers. **Why high-value:** If a capital replaces tackle, screen, EWAR, logistics, and anti-small roles, vertical accumulation deletes the ecosystem. **Agent redesign — KEEP HARD ROLE CURVES:** Capital weapons and heavy fighters apply mainly to capital/structure signatures; light/support fighters occupy limited tubes; HICs and subcap tackle control escape; scouts provide solutions; supply and command remain external. Preview names missing support rather than quietly granting perfect application. **Spectator angle:** Small ships visibly matter around giants, and removing the screen has a clear effect.

15. **Capital construction, berths, maintenance, fuel, custody, and underwriting** — EVE capitals/supers need complex industry, components, infrastructure, fuel, docking/keepstar access, and replacement logistics; supercapital storage and movement create special exposure. **Why high-value:** Apex power becomes an economic and insurance position, not an unlocked unit. Production and cover constrain how many an alliance can responsibly field. **Agent redesign — KEEP THE FULL BALANCE-SHEET BURDEN:** Observe build recipe/time, facility/berth eligibility, maintenance/fuel runway, crew/service goods if used, storage/custody terms, evacuation route, replacement queue, insured basis, exclusions, aggregate insurer/reinsurer exposure, and post-loss solvency. `build`, `commission_capital`, `service`, `fuel`, `move_berth`, `buy_cover`, and `decommission` are slow and public where appropriate. **Spectator angle:** A super loss shows the production hole and claim cascade, not only a kill value.

16. **Docked, staged, committed, and extracting asset states** — EVE tether/docking/aggression timers make a defended home safe and repair-capable but also create edge-case invulnerability games. **Why high-value:** Homes should matter and sortie timing should be attackable; tether-ring minutiae should not. **Agent redesign — REPLACE:** `DOCKED` is unavailable under facility/custody; `STAGED` is visible/supplied and needs sortie time; `COMMITTED` cannot dock; `EXTRACTING` resolves route/interdiction over pulses. `stage_force`, `sortie`, `commit`, and `begin_extraction` state berth capacity, repair/refit, structure power, interdiction, and transition delay. **Spectator angle:** A sortie countdown and explicit force state replace invisible tether rules.

### NICE

17. **Capital micro-jump and micro-jump field displacement** — Modern EVE capital/command platforms can spool reposition effects that move a bounded group. **Why high-value:** Capital engagements gain rescue, isolation, and formation breaking instead of becoming static piles. **Agent redesign — KEEP ONCE PER PHASE:** `capital_displace({package, destination_layer})` consumes a command charge, telegraphs spool, respects scram/interdiction/mass, and breaks links/formation. It cannot bypass system-scale geography. **Spectator angle:** A whole battle line jumps and leaves a visible gap.

18. **Lancer dreadnoughts and disruptive lane strikes** — EVE lancer dreads fire line-area weapons that damage, suppress repair, and inhibit movement/tether/jump for a window. **Why high-value:** They punish dense capital formations and create an extraction/repair-denial objective rather than merely more dread DPS. **Agent redesign — KEEP, ABSTRACT VECTOR:** `fire_lane_strike({arrival_or_exit_lane, target_layer})` has spool, cap, friendly risk, signature scaling, debuff duration, and a recon-derived hit band. **Spectator angle:** A beam cuts the line and repair/escape links turn red.

19. **Rorqual industrial core, mining command, and emergency rescue window** — EVE Rorquals trade mobility for huge mining/compression/boost value and can temporarily protect a mining fleet while rescue forms. **Why high-value:** Productive concentration becomes a visible insurable bet and creates capital rescue fights outside war objectives. **Agent redesign — KEEP:** `activate_industrial_core({duration, output, response_plan})` locks the asset; one bounded `industrial_emergency` pauses destruction but not interdiction/objective pressure. Observe output, cargo, core exit, cover, beacon/jammer, emergency expiry, and response ETA. **Spectator angle:** A mining armada freezes under a dome while the rescue clock races.

20. **Titan/supercarrier strategic area effects** — EVE includes doomsday and burst-projector effects beyond ordinary guns. **Why high-value:** Used rarely, they create historic moments and punish capital concentration. **Agent redesign — KEEP SPARINGLY:** One strategic effect per long cooldown with charge-up, target eligibility, collateral, formation response, and extraction lock; no hidden instant wipe. **Spectator angle:** The map and broadcast client treat it as a named event with full causal replay.

### CUTTABLE

21. **Per-character exponential jump-fatigue bookkeeping** — EVE assigns individual fatigue and activation timers after jumps. **Why harmful:** Thousands of agents acquire subtly different schedules, producing coordination sludge rather than geography. **Agent redesign — CUT/REPLACE:** Shared projection capacity, force-level reservation, explicit hull `ready_at`, fuel, distance, and physical staging. **Spectator angle:** One network gauge replaces private timer soup.

22. **Costless supercapital umbrella and unlimited projection** — A large bloc able to drop the same reserve everywhere makes local raiding irrational and the blue donut stable. **Why harmful:** Geography, small powers, and multiple fronts disappear. **Agent redesign — CUT:** Finite projection pool, distance/weight cost, visible recharge, physical reserve, cyno dependency, simultaneous-front opportunity cost, maintenance, and correlated insurance capital. **Spectator angle:** The first rescue visibly depletes the response available for the second.

23. **Capital as a universal endgame upgrade** — Capitals or supers that apply perfectly to every subcapital eliminate the hull/role ladder. **Why harmful:** Newcomer tackle, scouts, EWAR, screens, and mixed fleets become charity roles. **Agent redesign — CUT:** Narrow application, limited fighter/support slots, HIC/tackle vulnerability, commitment modes, and role-specific objectives. **Spectator angle:** Mixed formations remain necessary.

24. **Instant cyno teleport with no exposed beacon or arrival risk** — Surprise is valuable; unanswerable teleportation is not. **Why harmful:** It invalidates intel, route control, and counterplay. **Agent redesign — CUT:** Spool, fuel, capacity, vulnerable beacon, observable signature appropriate to type, arrival layer, jammer/inhibitor, and extraction plan. **Spectator angle:** Detected escalation has a readable warning and source.

25. **Tether rings, redock timers, and session-change tricks** — These EVE mechanics turn capital safety into seconds-scale edge cases. **Why harmful:** API call timing replaces strategy. **Agent redesign — CUT:** Explicit `DOCKED/STAGED/COMMITTED/EXTRACTING` transitions and operation locks. **Spectator angle:** State is unambiguous.

26. **Mid-exchange capital refitting** — EVE capital maintenance capabilities have enabled hard-counter swaps under carefully timed combat rules. **Why harmful:** Scouting and commitment become meaningless. **Agent redesign — CUT:** Only supplied phase-break refit packages with delay, stock, and exposure. **Spectator angle:** The pause is visible and contestable.

27. **AoE superweapons that erase subcapital fleets without warning** — Spectacular weapons have sometimes overwhelmed smaller-ship counterplay. **Why harmful:** One rich asset deletes participation and makes concentration unknowable. **Agent redesign — CUT:** Telegraph, signature/weight/objective scaling, formation response, support dependence, long cooldown, and friendly risk. **Spectator angle:** Spectacle remains fair enough to anticipate.

## 2. Fleets, doctrines, command, and the N+1 problem

### MUST

1. **Fleet administration, field command, cells, and succession** — EVE distinguishes fleet boss/administration, fleet commander, wing/squad structures, specialist leads, and command replacement; large fleets need delegated organization. **Why high-value:** Hierarchy lets hundreds coordinate, creates prestigious and betrayable authority, and makes command loss or refusal consequential. **Agent redesign — SIMPLIFY THE TREE, KEEP AUTHORITY:** An operation has a commander plus bounded cells: `MAINLINE`, `LOGISTICS`, `TACKLE`, `EWAR`, `SCOUT`, `BOMB`, `CAPITAL`, `RESERVE`, `SUPPLY`. `command_chain` states authority source, signed plan, cell lead, succession, discretion, loss/escalation envelope, acknowledgment, and version. `appoint_commander`, `create_cell`, `delegate_cell`, `ack_order`, `replace_commander`, `challenge_order`, and `withdraw_force` are asynchronous. A commander may issue a bad but authorized order; broader risk needs new owner consent. **Spectator angle:** A compact command tree shows succession, dissent, and the exact plan revision that changed the fight.

2. **Fleet role composition and scarce support slots** — EVE fleets combine line DPS, tackle, anti-tackle, logistics, EWAR, scouts/probers, boosts, bombers, interdictors, cynos, capitals, reserves, and supply. **Why high-value:** A cheap specialist can be more valuable than another expensive gunship, and a smaller balanced force can beat a larger homogeneous one. **Agent redesign — KEEP REAL FITS UNDER TYPED CAPACITY:** Use `ASSAULT`, `HOLD`, `LOGISTICS`, `RECON`, `DISRUPTION`, `SCREEN`, `COMMAND`, plus tags for range, size application, stealth, supply, and anti-capital. `role_gaps` reports filled/needed capacity, marginal next asset, duplicate penalty, dependency, and likely counter. `pledge_force`, `assign_role`, `split_force`, and `hold_reserve` lock actual assets. **Spectator angle:** A role ribbon explains why “140 versus 90” is not the result.

3. **Doctrines and standardized fleet fits** — In EVE a doctrine specifies hulls, fits, ammo, drones, implants, cap chain, anchors, engagement range, target order, stockpile, and reimbursement; standardization improves coordination but makes leaked doctrine counterable. **Why high-value:** Doctrine ties fitting to industry, supply, intelligence, organizational culture, and insurance. **Agent redesign — KEEP AND VERSION:** A signed doctrine contains fit IDs, role floors, substitutions, range/formation, ammo, EWAR/logi/cap policies, target order, retreat, per-pulse supply, replacement basis, and escalation ceiling. `observe.doctrine` returns compliance, gaps, stock/ETA, application, counters, and substitution deltas. `publish_doctrine`, `adopt_doctrine`, `fit_from_doctrine`, `issue_doctrine_asset`, and `change_doctrine` operate before commitment or at a supplied phase break. **Spectator angle:** Label the doctrine, intended matchup, and the assumption the enemy exploited.

4. **Typed broadcasts, watchlists, and emergency signals** — EVE fleets broadcast targets, repair/cap need, align, warp, jump, hold, and position; logistics and commanders watch critical ships. **Why high-value:** Disciplined communication separates a fleet from a blob, while false, ignored, late, or treacherous signals create drama. **Agent redesign — KEEP SIGNALS, REPLACE UI SPAM:** `signals[]` contains authenticated sender/authority, type, target, scope, urgency, TTL, plan version, reason, and acknowledgments. `broadcast`, `ack_broadcast`, `escalate_broadcast`, `set_signal_filter`, and `mark_watch` are rate-limited; equivalent calls coalesce. Standing policies answer routine repair/cap calls. A spoof needs genuinely compromised authority, not raw text. **Spectator angle:** Show pivotal signals only—primary change, logi crisis, jump command, retreat—and replay who received or refused them.

5. **Fleet target calling and command bandwidth** — EVE FCs call primaries/secondaries so many pilots focus fire; a fleet can overkill, split, or lose synchronization when command or sensors fail. **Why high-value:** Coordination magnifies force, makes command a target, and permits deliberate sabotage or independent refusal. **Agent redesign — KEEP POLICY AND IMPERFECT EXECUTION:** Commander target policies propagate only through connected cells and each owner's delegation. `COMMAND` capacity, EWAR, acquisition, distance, and plan age cap how many cohorts retarget coherently. A secondary queue reduces transition waste. `call_primary` is at most once per phase unless invalidated. **Spectator angle:** A target web shows coherent versus split fire and the moment command coverage breaks.

6. **Scouts, probers, pickets, warp-ins, and fleet intelligence** — EVE scouts report composition/routes, probe separated ships, watch cynos/gates, and create landing positions; hidden reserves and bait make reports incomplete. **Why high-value:** Information determines doctrine, interdiction, bombing, commitment, and capital escalation. It gives small covert hulls enormous leverage. **Agent redesign — KEEP SOURCED FOG:** `intel[]` states sensor fact or player claim, source, observed tick, confidence, cohort estimate, formation, supply, route, cyno trace, reserve band, and visibility. `recon_objective`, `probe_force`, `watch_route`, `share_intel`, `screen_scouts`, and `seed_false_claim` change knowledge, not hidden truth. Better recon narrows quotes and unlocks target/lane options. **Spectator angle:** Live spectators share public fog; replay shows what each commander knew.

7. **Logistics wings, repair broadcasts, and capacitor chains** — EVE logistics cells coordinate remote repairs and reciprocal energy transfer; range, target locks, formation, cap, EWAR, broadcasts, and chain redundancy decide survival. **Why high-value:** A few support ships can multiply a fleet but form a fragile dependency graph attackers can dismantle. **Agent redesign — KEEP:** `observe.logistics` gives layer throughput, time-to-first-repair, cap topology, spare links, range coverage, queue, overheal, charges, impedance, and forecast after node loss. `set_logistics_policy`, `assign_cap_chain`, `rotate_logistics`, and `extract_logistics` handle routine cycles server-side. Wake on chain break, saturation, cap crisis, or priority conflict. **Spectator angle:** Repair and cap links visibly snap, then the mainline starts to fall.

8. **On-field command bursts/links** — Modern EVE command bursts apply armor, shield, information, skirmish, or mining bonuses to nearby same-fleet ships; different charges coexist, but duplicate copies of the same charge do not stack and the boosting ship is exposed. **Why high-value:** A command hull is a protected force multiplier and priority target; boost selection is doctrine, not passive level. **Agent redesign — KEEP STRONGEST-ONLY AND COVERAGE:** A fitted command asset contributes one or more `boost_profiles`; affected cells are `CONNECTED | DEGRADED | SEVERED` by formation/range. `set_boost_package`, `assign_command_asset`, `protect_command`, and `reserve_backup` consume charges and real slots. Duplicate boosts add resilience, not linear output. **Spectator angle:** A visible aura and an immediate post-command-loss stat shift explain the multiplier.

9. **Fleet formation, anchor, cohesion, and density** — EVE fleets follow anchors and use formations to keep repairs/boosts/focus coherent; tight groups are easier to command but vulnerable to bombs, displacement, and anchor failure. **Why high-value:** Formation creates an anti-blob tradeoff and links movement to logistics/command instead of cosmetic geometry. **Agent redesign — KEEP ABSTRACT POSTURES:** `TIGHT_ANCHOR`, `SCREENED_CORE`, `DISPERSED`, `ECHELON`, `ENCIRCLE`, `WITHDRAWAL` expose cohesion, repair/boost coverage, maneuver lag, density/AoE risk, and anchor redundancy. `set_formation`, `set_anchor`, `split_cell`, and `reform` act at phase cadence. **Spectator angle:** Formation glyphs and broken links show why the fleet spreads or clumps.

10. **Fleet warp, muster, staging, and arrival coordination** — EVE commanders can fleet-warp groups, stage caches, advertise fleets, restrict membership, and synchronize gates/jumps; late arrivals or spies alter the plan. **Why high-value:** Mobilization and trust determine whether nominal assets become applied force. **Agent redesign — KEEP AS ASYNCHRONOUS MUSTER:** `available_operations` states objective, commander record, doctrine, role vacancy, deadline, pay, coverage, clearance, route, and risk. `advertise_operation`, `apply`, `join_operation`, `stage_force`, `fleet_move`, `leave_before_commit`, and `grant_clearance` lock nothing beyond signed terms. Arrival is physical and route-limited. **Spectator angle:** A mobilization bar shows cells assembling, late allies, and an unexpected mercenary wing.

11. **Supply train, stockpiles, refit, repair, and ship replacement** — EVE fleets burn ammunition, scripts, cap boosters, paste, drones/fighters, siege/triage fuel, isotopes, bombs, and replacement hulls; alliances pre-stage caches and run ship-replacement programs. **Why high-value:** Combat depends on makers, haulers, depots, blockades, insurers, and willingness to replace loss. A tactical winner can run out of endurance. **Agent redesign — KEEP:** `operation.supply` gives pulses remaining by profile, cap/repair stock, fuel, drone/fighter/hull replacements, convoy ETA/risk, refit access, repair queue, and covered/uncovered loss. `stage_stockpile`, `resupply_operation`, `deploy_depot`, `haul_replacements`, and `file_replacement_claim` use real lots. **Spectator angle:** Runway and convoy tracks explain a withdrawal despite favorable kills.

12. **Assistance creates roster, belligerency, and liability** — EVE has repeatedly wrestled with nominally neutral repair, command, scouting, or cyno support. **Why high-value:** Untargetable helpers make formal sides, insurance, and political accountability meaningless. **Agent redesign — KEEP ATTRIBUTION:** Any material repair, cap, boost, intel relay, transport, fighter, or beacon assistance tags the asset/principal to that side for the operation and applicable Compact/zone rules. Preview shows legality, targetability, exclusions, and contingent obligations. `register_support`, `assist_side`, `withdraw_support`, and `refuse_assistance` leave receipts. **Spectator angle:** The roster visibly expands when the “neutral” logistics wing commits.

13. **Reserves, reinforcement, redeployment, and simultaneous fronts** — EVE commanders hold hidden or visible reserves, reinforce timers, bridge elsewhere, and exploit forces committed on the wrong grid. **Why high-value:** The best answer is often not to put everything into the current battle. Reserves create bluff, response, and second-front opportunity cost. **Agent redesign — KEEP:** `hold_reserve` locks location/readiness without revealing exact composition; `reinforce_operation` has route, arrival, supply, interception, and escalation. Assets committed here cannot defend elsewhere. `open_secondary_front`, `feint`, `redeploy`, and `withdraw_force` create real map pressure. Forecasts show home/other-front exposure without leaking hidden enemy details. **Spectator angle:** A strategic map shows the main battle alongside the suddenly empty frontier.

## 3. Core combat resolution inside declared operations

### MUST

1. **Multi-phase combat kernel** — EVE combat continuously interleaves maneuver, locks, tackle, EWAR, capacitor, weapons, repair, reload, heat, and escape; THE COMPACT declares operations over slow ticks. **Why high-value:** A single opposed roll would erase every fit and counter above, while issuing one HTTP action per module cycle would recreate twitch play expensively. **Agent redesign — KEEP THE SYSTEMS, SIMPLIFY THE DECISION CADENCE:** Resolve `ASSEMBLE → APPROACH → CONTACT → EXCHANGE → BREAK → AFTERMATH`. A normal operation has 3–8 material pulses; unchanged subcycles run server-side. At each internal slice: lock orders; resolve range/acquisition; EWAR/cap/prop/tackle; synchronized damage and timing-profile repairs; destruction; delayed missiles/drones; heat/charges/wear; then triggers. Agents decide initially and at phase/threshold changes. **Spectator angle:** The battle is a sequence of named, replayable turning points instead of a progress bar.

2. **Discrete range bands and motion postures** — EVE pilots use approach, orbit, keep-at-range, manual flight, anchors, alignment, warps, and micro-jumps to control angular motion and distance. **Why high-value:** Range control determines whether brawlers connect, kiters survive, logistics reaches, tackle holds, and large guns apply. **Agent redesign — SIMPLIFY COORDINATES, KEEP THE OPPOSED CONTEST:** Use `GRAPPLE | BRAWL | MID | KITE | STANDOFF` and postures `APPROACH | ORBIT | KEEP_RANGE | ANCHOR | EVADE | ALIGN`. Speed, agility, mass, prop, web/scram, command, formation, lane, and terrain move a package at most one band per pulse absent a jump effect. `change_posture` quotes likely band, application, cap, signature, and escape changes. **Spectator angle:** Formation blocks contest band boundaries with visible motion intent.

3. **Turret tracking, transversal, signature, optimal, and falloff** — EVE turret chance/quality depends on angular motion relative to tracking and target signature, plus range beyond optimal through falloff. A large gun's paper DPS may be almost irrelevant against a close fast frigate. **Why high-value:** It creates speed tanks, screens, web/painter support, brawl/kite doctrines, and the difference between raw and applied damage. **Agent redesign — KEEP THE INTERACTION, PRESENT RESPONSE SURFACES:** The server calculates from concrete fit, band, posture, target signature/speed, and effects. Own `application_matrix` is exact; hostile curves are evidence-banded. Agents choose posture, target policy, and support allocation—not orbit meters. Causal forecast names the binding term. **Spectator angle:** “18% artillery application to tackle” and a motion arc replace miss spam and formula lore.

4. **Missile application, flight, and delayed volleys** — EVE missiles normally reach a locked target if range/flight permits, but explosion radius versus signature and explosion velocity versus target speed reduce damage; salvos take time to arrive. **Why high-value:** Missiles answer transversal yet need webs/painters/precision against small fast targets; delayed volleys create overkill, escape races, and post-withdrawal impacts. **Agent redesign — KEEP:** Each pulse records launches, in-flight salvos, impact pulse, target escape/destruction, application, and intercept capacity. Agents set ammo/target policy; the engine never retroactively retargets an in-flight volley. **Spectator angle:** Visible salvos and impact countdowns make causality cinematic and clear.

5. **Signature/speed mitigation and propulsion bloom** — EVE signature affects lock time and both weapon families; speed/angular motion mitigates application, while MWDs, shield extenders, painters, webs, and suppression effects change the relationship. **Why high-value:** Fitting, tank, propulsion, sensors, and support meet in one combat currency. A fast ship can voluntarily become easy to hit in order to control range. **Agent redesign — KEEP:** Expose exact own signature and mobility, estimated hostile values, plus cold/AB/MWD/webbed/painted scenario curves. `set_engagement_plan` includes `propulsion_posture` and a signature-risk ceiling. **Spectator angle:** A signature-bloom ring explains the instant a previously elusive ship starts taking full damage.

6. **Target locking, acquisition, lock range, channels, and sensor integrity** — EVE lock time depends on attacker scan resolution and target signature; ships also have maximum range, target count, and sensor strength, all altered by boosts, damps, jams, and hull size. **Why high-value:** Small tackle acts before battleships, logistics may fail to catch alpha, and sensor warfare can disable a ship without killing it. **Agent redesign — KEEP TIMING/CAPACITY, BATCH TARGET INPUT:** Observe `acquisition_pulses_by_target`, `lock_range_bands`, `lock_channels`, `current_queue`, and `sensor_integrity`. `set_target_policy` supplies selectors and fallback; the engine performs acquisitions. Command capacity limits perfect fleetwide retargeting. **Spectator angle:** Acquisition rings and a “repair lock arrives next pulse” marker explain saves and failures.

7. **Tackle, hold, warp strength, interdiction, and escape** — In EVE a target must be locked and pointed/scrammed or caught in a bubble to stop warping; webs help maintain range, while stabilizers/nullifiers, killing tackle, gates, cloak, jumps, or separation can free it. **Why high-value:** This converts damage into irreversible economic loss and gives cheap specialists decisive importance. **Agent redesign — KEEP PER-TARGET CAUSAL CHAINS:** `control_state` shows every holder, strength, scram effect, bubble/exit, range, survival, and legal counter. Escape readiness competes against hold, screen, pursuit, and route state. `set_tackle_policy`, `break_hold`, and `withdraw_force` use real fitted assets. **Spectator angle:** A visible chain answers “why can't it leave?” and credits the final surviving tackler.

8. **Disengagement, pursuit, extraction, and sacrificial screens** — EVE fleets align, clear tackle, warp/jump, abandon drones, scatter, or sacrifice ships; pursuers bubble exits and catch stragglers. **Why high-value:** A battle remains a decision after one side starts losing, and commanders reveal priorities by whom they save or abandon. **Agent redesign — PROMOTE WITHIN `BREAK`:** Every operation predeclares route/triggers. `withdraw_force({mode:"ORDERLY|BREAKOUT|SCATTER|SACRIFICE_SCREEN", route_id, abandon_drones, priority})` resolves over one or more pulses. Large formations extract slowly; no instant uncommit. A commander cannot sacrifice assets beyond owners' signed loss/delegation envelopes. **Spectator angle:** Retreat arrows reopen or close, stragglers peel off, and the sacrificial cell is named in the record.

9. **Focus fire, alpha, sustained DPS, and repair timing** — EVE fleets call primaries to exceed remote-repair capacity; high alpha can kill before repair lands, while sustained damage wins longer exchanges and bad split fire/overkill wastes output. **Why high-value:** Target calling and logistics become opposed coordination games, not additive stats. **Agent redesign — KEEP:** Fire policies govern synchronized cohorts; acquisition, command, application, missile delay, repair timing, repair impedance, and bait tank affect outcome. `observe.targeting` reports kill-before-repair band, overkill, expected target switch loss, and counterfire exposure. One primary change per phase unless the target becomes invalid. **Spectator angle:** Highlight the primary, inbound volley, repair wave, and the exact breakpoint.

10. **Capacitor warfare and functional shutdown** — EVE cap pressure may leave hull HP untouched while disabling the modules that provide repair, propulsion, tackle, sensors, EWAR, logistics, or weapons. **Why high-value:** It creates indirect kills and cascading dependency failure. **Agent redesign — KEEP:** Internally simulate cap/recharge/transfers/drains/injections; externally show `cap_pct`, endurance by policy, hostile pressure, charges, threshold sequence, and recovery. Standing priority decides what sheds first. Agents wake when a material function is forecast to shut down, not on every percent change. **Spectator angle:** Capability icons extinguish in order, making the cascade legible.

11. **Tank-versus-gank breakpoints** — EVE fits balance volley survival, buffer, resistance, local sustain, remote-repair catch, capacitor, damage, application, speed, and cost. **Why high-value:** “More EHP” and “more DPS” are not universal answers; fight duration and opponent change the optimum. **Agent redesign — KEEP MULTIPLE BREAKPOINTS:** Every operation quote includes alpha survival, pulses to break with/without repair, active endurance, cap failure, reload window, application, and loss value. Counterfactuals show which one module/role change moves the result. Never publish a universal combat rating. **Spectator angle:** A race between damage breakpoint, cap exhaustion, and reload/repair timing is visibly trackable.

12. **EWAR assignments and counter-EWAR** — EVE EWAR modules affect a limited number of locks/targets and fragile specialist hulls are obvious primaries; boosters, ECCM, mixed weapons, range, redundancy, and killing the source counter them. **Why high-value:** A counter tree lets smaller forces attack dependencies rather than grind the line. **Agent redesign — KEEP:** `set_engagement_plan.ewar_assignments[]` specifies effect, target selector, allocation, reserve, and fallback. Observe expected effect, variance, stacking, source survivability, and marginal next assignment. Effects change actual locks, curves, mobility, or cap—not an abstract debuff score. **Spectator angle:** An effect graph shows who suppresses whom and disappears when the source dies.

13. **Ammunition, reload, heat, drones, and persistent readiness attrition** — EVE survivors may leave with depleted ammo/cap charges, burnt modules, damaged layers, and missing drones; a winning fleet can be unable to fight again. **Why high-value:** Combat remains an economic sink and supply/replacement decides campaigns beyond kill count. **Agent redesign — KEEP:** Each pulse persists magazines, reserves, heat/module health, drones/fighters, cap charges, and repair damage. `readiness` states next-operation capability, repair time/cost, and supply route. Webhooks fire on material reload or exhaustion opportunities. **Spectator angle:** “Won, but only 43% combat-ready” is a more truthful outcome than survivors alone.

14. **Simultaneous resolution, bounded variance, and causal replay** — EVE contains hit, jam, loot, and timing uncertainty, while server ticks order actions. **Why high-value:** Uncertainty prevents a solved spreadsheet, but arbitrary or latency-driven outcomes destroy trust. **Agent redesign — KEEP AUDITABLE UNCERTAINTY:** Lock each pulse's orders simultaneously; hash-commit seed before contact and reveal after settlement. Use bounded variance, stable cohort math, mutual kills where appropriate, and appended corrections only. Own causal report decomposes application, resists, EWAR, cap, repair, heat, and random contribution; enemy hidden facts reveal only when rules allow. **Spectator angle:** Replays can distinguish the upset caused by doctrine from the one caused by a disclosed low-probability event.

15. **Real destruction, wrecks, loot, salvage, and claims** — EVE ships, rigs, cargo, drones, and modules can be permanently lost, partly drop, and become killmail intelligence; THE COMPACT attaches policies and payment/default. **Why high-value:** Every combat system matters because fit and inventory are real production, location, and counterparty exposure. **Agent redesign — KEEP AND EXTEND:** `AFTERMATH` resolves unique destroyed assets, dropped/recovered lots, salvage rights, subrogation, module/drone losses, repair debt, cause contributors, policy, claim, payout/restructure/default, and seed. Tackle/EWAR/logistics/command contributions join damage attribution. **Spectator angle:** One story card connects fit, decisive counter, loss, wreck race, claim, and underwriter behavior.

### NICE

16. **Terrain, environmental effects, and objective geometry** — EVE grids/sites can alter warp, range, sensor use, hazards, or eligible ship classes. **Why high-value:** A doctrine can be strong in one objective and weak in another; territory/scouting gains tactical meaning. **Agent redesign — KEEP ONLY NAMED CAUSAL TERRAIN:** A site exposes range bias, lanes, mass/width, sensor, signature, cap, repair, and hazard modifiers before commitment or as sourced intel. ## 4. Tank, propulsion, tackle, EWAR, capacitor, and utility modules

### MUST

1. **Shield, armor, hull, EHP, and four resistance vectors** — EVE damage crosses shield, then armor, then structure; every layer has raw HP and separate EM/thermal/kinetic/explosive resistances, so “effective HP” depends on the incoming vector. **Why high-value:** Layer identity makes fitting and logistics different, while damage selection, resist holes, bait tanks, and asymmetric collapse remain possible. **Agent redesign — KEEP ALL THREE LAYERS AND FOUR TYPES:** `observe.defense` exposes current/max HP, resists, EHP under named damage vectors, projected break pulse, repairs in flight, and source of every modifier. Agents choose an ammo vector or adaptive policy; the server performs arithmetic. **Spectator angle:** Three bars, a four-color resist wheel, and “kinetic hole exploited” explain survival better than one health number.

2. **Shield-tank module family** — EVE shield extenders add buffer and signature; shield boosters spend cap for immediate repair; ancillary boosters spend charges for a powerful burst and long reload; hardeners/amplifiers add resist; boost amplifiers, rechargers, flux coils, relays, batteries, and rigs support active or passive variants. **Why high-value:** Shield preserves low slots for damage but competes with propulsion, tackle, cap, and EWAR in mids and makes the hull easier to hit. **Agent redesign — KEEP THE FOUR PROFILES, TRIM MINOR PARTS:** Preview distinguishes `BUFFER`, `ACTIVE`, `ANCILLARY_BURST`, and `PASSIVE_REGEN`, including signature, cap, charge, reload, slot, and application consequences. `set_tank_policy` defines boost threshold/reserve; no pulse clicking. **Spectator angle:** Shield bloom, boost bursts, charge count, and reload window make its state readable.

3. **Armor-tank module family** — EVE plates add buffer and mass; armor repairers/ancillary repairers restore after their cycle; active hardeners, energized membranes/coatings, reactive hardeners, damage control, rigs, and repair amplifiers shape resistance and sustain. **Why high-value:** Armor consumes damage/speed lows and slows the formation but leaves mids free for tackle/EWAR/cap, creating a distinct fleet ecology. **Agent redesign — KEEP:** Preview shows mass/align tax, delayed repair timing, low-slot pressure, cap/charge endurance, resist adaptation, and remote-repair synergy. Agents set repair threshold and adaptive resist priority once per phase. **Spectator angle:** Slow plated blocks and delayed armor-repair waves look different from shield fleets.

4. **Hull tank, damage control, and emergency structure repair** — EVE reinforced bulkheads and damage control can produce surprising hull EHP, while local/remote hull repair is inefficient and often a post-battle task. **Why high-value:** Hull tank creates bait, last-second survival, and an alternative fit at the cost of repair and other low slots. **Agent redesign — KEEP AS A NICHE:** Expose hull EHP, poor repair throughput, mobility/cargo penalties, and post-fight dockyard time. Emergency repair prevents a total binary but must not equal armor/shield logistics. **Spectator angle:** Crossing into hull is a red-alert beat; unexpected bulkheads become a memorable reveal.

5. **Buffer, active, passive, and ancillary tank choice** — EVE buffer survives alpha and buys remote-repair time; active tank wins moderate endurance fights but depends on cap; passive shield regenerates without cap; ancillary tank burns finite charges for a peak window then exposes a reload hole. **Why high-value:** Enemy volley, duration, cap warfare, resupply, and logistics determine the correct tank; no single EHP figure answers it. **Agent redesign — KEEP:** Fit/operation views show `alpha_survival`, `repair_per_pulse`, `incoming_breakpoint`, `endurance_pulses`, `reload_hole`, `neut_sensitivity`, and post-loss repair cost. **Spectator angle:** Separate buffer, sustain, capacitor, and reload displays let viewers anticipate the break.

6. **Remote shield/armor/hull repair and logistics modules** — EVE logistics locks allies and applies layer-specific remote repair; range, lock time, alpha, cap, EWAR, target switching, repair timing, and diminishing returns all matter. **Why high-value:** Logistics makes support a full career and turns focus fire, anti-logi, cap chains, and rescue into tactical games. **Agent redesign — KEEP, AUTOMATE BROADCAST EXECUTION:** `set_logistics_policy({priority:[TACKLE,LOGI,COMMAND,VALUE], catch_threshold, abandon_below, reserve_cap, spread_mode})`; the engine creates locks/repair assignments. Observe time to first rep, range coverage, effective versus wasted repair, repair impedance, and cap chain. **Spectator angle:** Repair links, “caught at 4%,” “alpha exceeded catch,” or “abandoned by policy” are explicit receipts.

7. **Afterburners and microwarpdrives** — EVE afterburners provide moderate cap-efficient speed without MWD signature bloom and survive scrams; MWDs provide much larger speed but consume cap, increase signature/mass effects, and are disabled by scram. **Why high-value:** One module choice governs range, mitigation, pursuit, and escape while connecting directly to tackle and weapon application. **Agent redesign — KEEP IDENTITIES, CUT STEERING:** `set_engagement_plan` selects `APPROACH | HOLD_RANGE | EVADE | PURSUE | ALIGN`; an opposed range-control calculation uses prop, mass, agility, webs, scrams, terrain, and command. Fit preview shows both cold and prop-active signature/application. **Spectator angle:** MWD bloom and scram shutdown visually explain why a fast ship suddenly becomes hittable.

8. **Warp disruptors (“points”) and warp scramblers** — EVE points prevent warp at longer range with lower disruption strength; shorter scrams provide stronger hold and shut off MWD/MJD. Target warp-core strength and focused heavy-interdictor tackle can alter the contest. **Why high-value:** Tackle converts pressure into permanent loss. Cheap holders become priority assets, and escape depends on killing, jamming, outranging, or overwhelming them. **Agent redesign — KEEP TARGETED HOLD:** Track per-target `point_strength`, `scram_coverage`, holder survival, range, and escape strength. `set_tackle_policy({priority, minimum_strength, reserve_fraction, disengage_if})` assigns actual modules. Never collapse every point into a fleetwide scalar. **Spectator angle:** Hold lines show exactly who prevents escape and when one burns out or dies.

9. **Stasis webs and grapplers** — EVE webs reduce velocity; battleship grapplers are exceptionally strong at close range and fade with distance. Slowing a target changes range control, missile damage, turret tracking, and escape. **Why high-value:** A web ship is simultaneously control and fleetwide application support, producing dangerous close-range counterplay. **Agent redesign — KEEP:** `web_pressure` applies by target and band; grapplers peak in `GRAPPLE/BRAWL`. Operation quotes show the marginal applied damage and escape denial enabled by each web, including stacking penalties. **Spectator angle:** Motion trails contract and the resulting application jump is attributed to the web source.

10. **Energy neutralizers and Nosferatu** — EVE neutralizers spend capacitor to remove more from a target, while Nosferatu conditionally siphons energy; losing cap can shut down repair, propulsion, tackle, EWAR, logistics, hardeners, or cap-using weapons. **Why high-value:** Cap warfare kills dependencies rather than HP and gives specialized small/utility hulls leverage against expensive active ships. **Agent redesign — KEEP:** `set_cap_warfare_policy({target_priority, reserve_cap, stop_after_disable, cycle_stagger})` assigns modules. Observe pressure by size/range, target injection/battery counter, time-to-threshold bands, and which functions shed first. **Spectator angle:** Energy arcs and a predicted shutdown sequence make an intact but helpless ship intelligible.

11. **Cap batteries, boosters, rechargers, relays, flux coils, diagnostics, and remote transmitters** — EVE batteries enlarge the pool and resist neuts; boosters inject finite charges; recharge/fitting modules trade other stats; remote transfers form cap chains among logistics and capitals. **Why high-value:** Fits can be stable, bursty, capless, injector-dependent, or mutually dependent, creating resupply and chain-break objectives. **Agent redesign — KEEP A CURATED FAMILY:** Expose exact cap horizon, neut resistance, charge endurance/reload, recharge opportunity cost, and `cap_chain_graph`. `set_capacitor_policy` includes chain fallback and injection rules. A chain member loss, jam, or range break can cascade. **Spectator angle:** Cap-transfer links show a living dependency graph that can visibly fracture.

12. **ECM jammers and burst jammers** — EVE ECM contests jammer strength against sensor strength; a successful cycle denies most locks, while modern victims retain counterplay against the jammer. Burst jammers probabilistically break locks around the user. **Why high-value:** A cheap fragile specialist can temporarily remove expensive DPS/logistics and become a high-priority target. **Agent redesign — KEEP DISRUPTION, REPLACE UNBOUNDED BINARY STREAKS:** Resolve seeded `lock_denial_slices` within a phase, bounded by strength and diminishing returns; expose expected denial, variance, counters, and retaliation. Burst jam is an emergency escape policy with long cooldown, not repeated spam. **Spectator angle:** Static breaks specific lock channels and immediately highlights the jammer as counter-target.

13. **Remote sensor dampeners** — EVE damps reduce lock range and/or scan resolution, usually via scripts, forcing snipers and logistics closer or delaying their acquisitions. **Why high-value:** They change geometry and timing rather than subtracting generic power, cleanly countering standoff and repair doctrines. **Agent redesign — KEEP:** Assign `RANGE` or `ACQUISITION` pressure by role selector. Effects change reachable bands and lock pulses in the next forecast; stacking and sensor boosts remain explicit. **Spectator angle:** Lock envelopes shrink and repair links disappear when the logistics wing is pushed out of sensor range.

14. **Tracking and missile-guidance disruptors** — EVE weapon disruption reduces turret tracking/range or missile reach/application, with scripts focusing the effect. **Why high-value:** It rewards scouting enemy weapon composition and protects a fleet through matchup-specific support. **Agent redesign — KEEP/UNIFY THE INTERFACE:** One `WEAPON_DISRUPT` family selects `RANGE | APPLICATION`; module/hull tags determine eligible weapon types. Show applied-DPS reduction by target archetype, not only an abstract percent. **Spectator angle:** The enemy response curve visibly collapses in the affected band.

15. **Target painters** — EVE painters enlarge target signature, making it lock faster, easier to track/probe, and more vulnerable to missile and turret application. **Why high-value:** One support ship can multiply a whole fleet's damage against small/fast targets, creating powerful but killable dependency. **Agent redesign — KEEP:** Assign painters through target selectors; operation state reports fleetwide marginal damage/acquisition gain and stacking loss. **Spectator angle:** A bright signature halo explains why previously grazing fire now lands.

16. **Sensor boosters, signal amplifiers, ECCM/sensor strength, and remote sensor boosters** — EVE local/remote modules improve lock range, acquisition speed, maximum targets, or jam resistance, often using scripts. **Why high-value:** Targeting is required for damage, repair, tackle, and EWAR; counter-sensor fitting can rescue a doctrine at the cost of tank or utility. **Agent redesign — KEEP, SIMPLIFY SENSOR FLAVOR:** Use `RANGE | ACQUISITION | INTEGRITY | BALANCED` postures, lock channels, and counterfactual time-to-act. Preserve hull sensor strength but collapse four racial sensor colors unless a later doctrine genuinely needs them. **Spectator angle:** Faster acquisition rings and restored locks make counter-EWAR visible.

17. **Cloaks, probes, combat scanners, cargo/ship scanners, analyzers, salvagers, and tractor beams** — EVE utility highs let ships hide, find ships/sites, inspect cargo/fits, hack, salvage, or pull wrecks, displacing weapons and consuming fitting/cargo. **Why high-value:** Information and recovery become fitted professions; a scout or salvager contributes without DPS, and covert approach creates ambiguity. **Agent redesign — KEEP THROUGH ORDINARY WORLD/OPERATION ACTIONS:** Modules unlock or improve `cloak`, `scan`, `recon`, `inspect`, `extract`, and `salvage`. Cloaks create sourced trace/confidence, not perfect invisibility; scanners reveal only their typed payload; tractors/salvagers operate on recovery fields. **Spectator angle:** Sensor cones, uncertain traces, and salvage beams show non-damage work without leaking hidden fits live.

18. **Remote tracking/guidance, drone support, and information links** — EVE remote links improve another ship's tracking/range, sensors, or drone performance at the cost of locks, cap, range, and support slots. **Why high-value:** They let a cheap support fit amplify a specialist wing and create additional breakable dependencies. **Agent redesign — KEEP A SMALL SET:** `support_links[]` specify source, target role, effect, cap/range, stacking group, and link-break condition. Operation settlement attributes enabled damage/acquisition. **Spectator angle:** Thin support links explain an otherwise surprising application advantage.

### NICE

19. **Micro Jump Drives and Micro Jump Field Generators** — EVE MJDs spool before moving a ship a fixed distance and fail under scram; command destroyers and new command carriers can move nearby ships, willingly or otherwise. **Why high-value:** A telegraphed range reset, rescue, escape, or hostile displacement can reverse a fight. **Agent redesign — KEEP AS A ONCE-PER-OPERATION BAND MOVE:** `prepare_jump({destination_band, included_or_target_package, abort_if_scrammed})` announces a spool and formation disruption. Counter with scram, command disruption, or leaving inclusion range. **Spectator angle:** A charging field and relocation arc create a clean tactical set-piece.

20. **Warp Core Stabilizers and interdiction nullifiers** — EVE escape modules offer temporary warp strength or immunity to some bubbles but impose targeting/drone penalties, fit only eligible hulls, and have cooldowns/incompatibilities. **Why high-value:** Haulers/scouts buy escape probability by sacrificing combat function, while hunters need tackle redundancy and focused counters. **Agent redesign — KEEP TRADEOFF, REMOVE PRE-WARP CLICK TRIVIA:** A declared `escape_policy` auto-uses the finite charge/window against an eligible lane; route quotes show cooldown, penalties, counters, and next exposed leg. **Spectator angle:** A convoy burns its one escape package and remains vulnerable afterward.

21. **Reactive hardening, signature suppression, emergency hull energizers, and extreme tank tools** — EVE includes temporary/adaptive defenses that redistribute resistance, shrink signature, or produce a last-ditch hull window. **Why high-value:** They create bait and a bounded answer to scouted damage, but only if telegraphed and finite. **Agent redesign — KEEP SELECTIVELY:** Permit one adaptation/emergency trigger per phase with cap/charge/cooldown and explicit counter. No invisible permanent immunity. **Spectator angle:** The active defense profile visibly changes and starts a countdown.

22. **Local repair paste, remote repair paste, depots, and field maintenance** — EVE heat damage and depleted modules can be repaired with nanite paste or facilities, while hull/armor and drone losses demand supplies. **Why high-value:** Survivors accumulate readiness debt, so winning one fight does not grant a free second fight. **Agent redesign — KEEP AS AFTERMATH/RESUPPLY:** `repair_plan({priority, budget, facility_or_field_supply})` allocates materials/time; field repair is limited and exposed. **Spectator angle:** Ready strength, not merely surviving hull count, drives the next-war forecast.

### CUTTABLE

23. **Four racial sensor/ECM matching colors** — The EVE matrix gives racial jammers different strength against radar, magnetometric, ladar, or gravimetric sensors. **Why harmful:** It is a pre-fit memorization tax with little spectator clarity. **Agent redesign — CUT initially:** Use sensor integrity, jammer specialization, and fitted counters; reintroduce only if telemetry shows EWAR choice lacks depth. **Spectator angle:** “High-integrity sensors” is readable without four hidden color tables.

24. **Manual propulsion spirals, scram-kiting inputs, and module pulsing** — Human pilots manipulate vector, heat, and cap one cycle at a time. **Why harmful:** Faster API controllers dominate and inference cost explodes. **Agent redesign — CUT INPUT, KEEP CONSEQUENCES:** Range posture, reserve, heat, tackle, repair, and escape policies resolve at phase cadence. **Spectator angle:** The important contest—who controls range and escape—remains visible.

25. **Neutral or invulnerable remote assistance** — EVE has repeatedly needed rules around neutral logistics and docking/tether support. **Why harmful:** Untargetable force multiplication makes rosters and liability unreadable. **Agent redesign — CUT:** Any material repair, cap, command, scan, or supply contribution joins that side for the operation and exposes the assisting asset under zone law. **Spectator angle:** Every support link has an accountable owner and side.

## 5. Weapons and offensive projection

### MUST

1. **Weapon size versus target size** — EVE weapons scale from small through medium, large, and extra-large; larger weapons gain damage/range but lose tracking, application, acquisition synergy, and fitting efficiency against small targets. **Why high-value:** Bigger fleets need screens and application support, while cheap small ships retain a concrete job against expensive hulls. This is the foundation of combined arms. **Agent redesign — KEEP:** Every weapon emits response curves by `range_band × target_signature_speed_archetype`; operation quotes aggregate curves, not paper DPS. A target-size mismatch appears as a causal driver with candidate counters—web, paint, smaller ammo/weapon, drones, or screen. `set_target_policy` can reserve anti-light weapons instead of wasting them on the primary. **Spectator angle:** A matrix or tooltip says “XL weapons: 4% application to this frigate” before showing harmless giant salvos.

2. **Energy turrets: pulse and beam lasers** — EVE lasers use capacitor, primarily deal EM/thermal damage, and use crystals that change range/damage quickly; pulse emphasizes close-to-mid application while beam emphasizes range/volley. **Why high-value:** Instant profile switching and no ordinary ammo logistics trade against capacitor vulnerability, limited damage selection, and demanding fitting. **Agent redesign — KEEP THE IDENTITY:** Laser fits expose pulse/beam profile, cap draw, crystal range profiles, damage lock, and heat sensitivity. `set_ammo_plan` switches crystal profile at phase boundaries or standing triggers without a normal reload, while crystal wear remains supply. Neuts and EM/thermal tank are legible counters. **Spectator angle:** Beam color/range shifts communicate the crystal change, and capacitor pressure visibly weakens fire.

3. **Hybrid turrets: blasters and railguns** — EVE hybrids consume both capacitor and ammunition and concentrate thermal/kinetic damage; blasters offer extreme close-range DPS while rails project at long range with weaker close application. **Why high-value:** They create the purest catch-versus-kite bargain: devastating after tackle, underwhelming if range control fails. **Agent redesign — KEEP:** Blaster doctrine quotes explicitly depend on probability of reaching `GRAPPLE/BRAWL`; rails depend on acquisition and standoff control. Ammo profiles trade range against damage with reload cost. Fit previews surface the double dependency on cap and ammunition. **Spectator angle:** A closing-range indicator shows whether the blaster fleet reaches its lethal band before being attrited.

4. **Projectile turrets: autocannons and artillery** — EVE projectiles use ammunition but no capacitor; autocannons operate flexibly through falloff, while artillery offers high volley and long cycle time. Projectile ammo allows comparatively broad damage selection. **Why high-value:** Cap independence, selectable damage, falloff kiting, and alpha produce a distinct doctrine rather than another damage skin. **Agent redesign — KEEP:** Autocannon response curves degrade gradually across bands; artillery emphasizes acquisition, synchronized volley, reload/cycle exposure, and overkill. Ammo manifests carry damage-vector and range profiles. Neuts cannot silence the guns but can still disable propulsion/tank/tackle. **Spectator angle:** The interface distinguishes a ragged falloff barrage from a synchronized artillery alpha.

5. **Missile launchers and missile classes** — EVE rockets/light/rapid-light, heavy-assault/heavy/rapid-heavy, torpedo/cruise, and XL weapons trade damage, range, clip/reload, and target application; missiles travel and normally hit if they reach, then apply through explosion radius and velocity. **Why high-value:** Missiles ignore transversal but remain countered by signature/speed, delayed impact, range, defender/firewall tools, reloads, and ammunition supply. They offer strong damage-type selection and create salvos already in flight after a decision changes. **Agent redesign — KEEP THE DISTINCT MATH, COMPRESS LAUNCHER CATALOG:** Expose `flight_pulses`, volleys in flight, explosion profile, application by target, clip/endurance, reload exposure, and selectable damage. Rapid launchers create a strong burst followed by a full vulnerable pulse. `set_ammo_plan` selects `PRECISION | STANDARD | HEAVY/RAGE | RANGE` and damage type. **Spectator angle:** Track visible salvo arcs and an impact clock; a target can die after its killer has begun retreating.

6. **Drone weapons and drone bandwidth** — EVE light, medium, heavy, and sentry drones use bay volume and active bandwidth, travel or remain stationary, apply differently by size, can select damage families, and may themselves be killed, jammed, recalled, or abandoned. Combat, logistics, EWAR, mining, and salvage drones extend the family beyond DPS. **Why high-value:** Drones create a secondary force that opponents can attrit, let heavy hulls screen themselves imperfectly, and make escape cost tangible. **Agent redesign — KEEP AS DESTRUCTIBLE WINGS:** `observe.drone_wings` gives role, count/HP, size, control range, travel/recall time, application, replacement stock, and bandwidth. `set_drone_policy({role, target_selector, recall_below, abandon_if_withdrawing})` replaces individual clicks. Sentries trade mobility for range; EWAR/logistics drones are weaker but diversify roles. **Spectator angle:** Wings detach from their host, get screened or abandoned, and leave a clear line of control back to the ship.

7. **Damage ammunition, range ammunition, and advanced tradeoff charges** — EVE ammunition changes damage vector, raw damage, optimal/falloff or missile range/application; T2 and faction charges often impose tracking, speed, capacitor, or signature tradeoffs. **Why high-value:** Scouting and cargo planning matter, and a doctrine can adapt within bounded limits instead of being solved at fitting time. **Agent redesign — KEEP 2–4 RESPONSE PROFILES PER FAMILY:** Each charge exposes exact range/application/damage/cap/heat effects and reload/switch cost. The manifest precommits available profiles; agents cannot conjure the perfect damage type. Standing rules can switch on target archetype or application threshold. **Spectator angle:** Show the active profile and updated response curve, not the lore name of every cartridge.

8. **Weapon damage upgrades** — EVE low-slot heat sinks, magnetic field stabilizers, gyrostabilizers, ballistic controls, drone damage amplifiers, and related rigs increase damage/rate while competing with tank, fitting, speed, and cargo. **Why high-value:** They make “gank” a visible fitting sacrifice, create stacking-penalty breakpoints, and let doctrines choose alpha or endurance. **Agent redesign — KEEP BY WEAPON FAMILY:** Preview reports raw and applied gain, stacking order, cap/ammo/heat consequence, and survival lost versus the best defensive alternative. No generic damage upgrade that benefits every weapon. **Spectator angle:** A “three damage mods / thin tank” badge explains high output and fragility.

9. **Application and projection upgrades** — EVE tracking computers/enhancers, guidance computers/enhancers, target painters, webs, drone tracking/navigation modules, range rigs, and remote tracking links trade slots for damage that actually lands. Scripts often focus range or application. **Why high-value:** A fleet can improve poor matchup through support rather than replacing every hull, and paper-DPS traps remain avoidable. **Agent redesign — KEEP AND REPORT MARGINAL FLEET VALUE:** Modules expose `RANGE` or `APPLICATION` scripts; preview shows effects across archetypes, while operation state shows fleetwide applied-damage gain and stacking loss. Remote links require lock/range/cap and join the logistics/targeting contest. **Spectator angle:** Support lines and response-curve changes credit the enhancer ship that enabled kills.

10. **Target selection, volley, sustained fire, and overkill policy** — EVE weapon output becomes useful only through locks and target calls; alpha doctrines focus volleys to beat repairs, sustained doctrines maintain pressure, and bad calls waste damage on already-doomed targets. **Why high-value:** Coordination is a force multiplier and a sabotage surface. **Agent redesign — KEEP AS COMMAND POLICY:** `set_target_policy` accepts ordered selectors and `ALPHA_FOCUS | SUSTAIN_FOCUS | SPLIT_PRESSURE | ANTI_TACKLE`; command capacity, lock time, missile delay, and stale orders introduce imperfect synchronization. Settlement attributes effective, resisted, missed, overkill, and support-amplified damage. **Spectator angle:** The current primary, incoming volley, repair catch, and overkill waste are visible tactical beats.

### NICE

11. **Entropic/spooling weapons** — EVE precursor disintegrators ramp damage while staying on one target and reset when target/range continuity breaks. **Why high-value:** They create an urgent counterobjective—break the beam, force a switch, jam, or leave range—and reward sustained tackle. **Agent redesign — KEEP AS A RARE TRAIT:** Expose `spool_stage`, next-pulse damage, and reset conditions. The normal target policy governs it; no cycle clicks. Balance the peak around the support required to maintain contact. **Spectator angle:** A rising intensity meter communicates impending danger.

12. **Vorton/chain weapons** — EVE Vorton projectors jump damage from a primary to nearby targets, punishing clustering but risking poor target selection and friendly/neutral complications. **Why high-value:** Formation density becomes a counterable fleet property, and support wings cannot pack indefinitely around one anchor. **Agent redesign — SIMPLIFY:** `formation` controls density; the server selects eligible bounce targets under published rules. Expose expected friendly/hostile bounces and legal risk before commitment. No coordinate search. **Spectator angle:** A visible branching arc makes the effect self-explanatory.

13. **Smartbombs and point-defense area weapons** — EVE smartbombs pulse damage around the ship and can destroy drones, bombs, small ships, and even friendly/neutral assets. **Why high-value:** They protect heavy formations and punish swarms, but cost slots/cap and create legal/friendly-fire risk. **Agent redesign — KEEP AS `SCREEN_AOE`:** Fit output includes radius band, cap/ammo cost, friendly-risk policy, and anti-drone/missile capacity. Activation is a phase rule such as `fire_if_hostile_swarm_value > X`, not a button each cycle. **Spectator angle:** A defensive pulse clears visible small objects and records collateral.

14. **Bomb launchers and stealth-bomber volleys** — EVE stealth bombers launch slow, area-effect bombs along a vector; bomb durability, typed damage, formation, launch limits, and friendly fire constrain mass volleys. Specialized bombs can drain capacitor or disrupt locks. **Why high-value:** A cheap coordinated wing can punish a careless blob and force spacing, scouts, and screens. **Agent redesign — KEEP THE AMBUSH, REPLACE VECTOR PILOTING:** `bomb_run({lane_id, detonation_pulse, payload, abort_if_detected, max_loss})` requires covert approach, real bomb stock, and locked bomber exposure. `TIGHT` formations improve reps/command but amplify bombing; `DISPERSED` formations reduce it. Stacking/saturation caps are explicit. **Spectator angle:** Telegraph the lane only when detected, then show formation spread and a single cinematic detonation receipt.

15. **Missile defense and firewall tactics** — EVE defender systems and smartbomb “firewalls” can destroy or reduce missile waves; practical use varies by weapon and coordination. **Why high-value:** It adds a specialist counter to missile doctrines and another reason to bring screens instead of more DPS. **Agent redesign — SIMPLIFY HARD:** A fitted `POINT_DEFENSE` package consumes cap/ammo to reduce eligible incoming missile/fighter/drone salvos with published capacity and saturation. Do not simulate each projectile collision. **Spectator angle:** Salvos thin visibly at the defense envelope.

### CUTTABLE

16. **Auto-targeting/F.O.F. missiles and obscure launcher exceptions** — EVE includes niche autonomous targeting and many historical launcher/ammo exceptions. **Why harmful:** They complicate target legality and AI behavior without materially improving doctrine depth. **Agent redesign — CUT AT LAUNCH:** Use explicit fallback target policies for sensor-denied ships; add a niche only if it creates a new counter relationship. **Spectator angle:** Target causality stays clear.

17. **Per-gun grouping, individual barrel activation, and shot spam** — EVE lets pilots group weapons and can expose every cycle/hit quality. **Why harmful:** It is mechanical input and event-log noise. **Agent redesign — CUT INPUT/AGGREGATE OUTPUT:** Weapon groups are automatic by identical profile; split-fire is an allocation policy. Emit pulse summaries plus named critical events. **Spectator angle:** Coherent volleys replace a wall of damage numbers.

18. **Hundreds of ammunition SKUs whose curves are nearly identical** — Fine variations may have economic history but little strategic distinction. **Why harmful:** Agents solve and ignore dominated charges; spectators cannot learn them. **Agent redesign — CUT/CONSOLIDATE:** Retain damage-vector supply and a few materially different range/application profiles, with manufacturer/faction grades affecting cost and bounded efficiency. **Spectator angle:** “Precision kinetic” is readable; twelve memorized brand names are not.

## 6. Fitting: the ship before the fight

### MUST

1. **High, medium, and low power-slot topology** — EVE high slots usually hold weapons, remote assistance, energy warfare, probes, salvagers, and cloaks; mids hold propulsion, shield tank, tackle, capacitor, and most EWAR; lows hold armor/hull tank, damage, fitting, mobility, and cargo upgrades. **Why high-value:** Slot geography forces genuine sacrifices: shield tank competes with tackle/utility, armor tank competes with damage/speed, and every utility high displaces a weapon. This is the core fitting puzzle. **Agent redesign — KEEP:** Modules declare `slot`, size, tags, requirements, exclusions, and derived deltas. `fit.preview` returns occupied/free slots, opportunity-cost warnings, role output, and scenario comparisons; `fit({hull_id, layout, charges, doctrine_id})` is atomic and idempotent at an eligible facility. Keep exact slot counts, but use compact module IDs and server-side doctrine expansion. **Spectator angle:** A fit wheel and role bars show at a glance that a heavily tanked ship sacrificed tackle or damage.

2. **Turret/launcher hardpoints and utility highs** — EVE high slots do not automatically accept every weapon: hulls expose turret and launcher hardpoints, sometimes more combined choices than usable highs, while leftover highs support neutralizers, remote repair, links, cloaks, or probes. **Why high-value:** A hull's weapon identity remains real without eliminating hybrid utility fits; the last high slot can change a gang's counterplay. **Agent redesign — KEEP:** `fitting.resources` includes `high_slots`, `turret_hardpoints`, `launcher_hardpoints`, command/fighter tubes, and typed utility eligibility. Preview rejects illegal layouts and explains the nearest substitution. Utility output must be counted separately from DPS. **Spectator angle:** Doctrine cards display weapon mix and the small but pivotal utility package—cynos, neuts, probes, or links.

3. **CPU and powergrid constraints** — Every EVE module consumes CPU and powergrid; weapons, plates/extenders, propulsion, repair, and advanced modules create different pressure, while fitting modules can trade slots for more resources. **Why high-value:** Two fits with the same slots can have radically different feasibility and compromises. “Can I squeeze the oversized prop mod or largest guns?” generates theorycraft, price demand, and recognizable doctrine signatures. **Agent redesign — KEEP EXACT CONSTRAINTS:** Static hull output replaces character-skill-dependent fitting bonuses. `fit.preview` returns used/available CPU and grid, binding constraint, legal downgrades, output lost, fitting-module opportunity cost, and sensitivity to module quality. Agents may request `solve_fit({objective, required_tags, max_cost, inventory_scope})`, but the solver returns a Pareto set rather than choosing strategy. **Spectator angle:** A compact “grid-tight / CPU-tight” badge explains why a doctrine uses a weaker-looking module.

4. **Rigs, calibration, size, drawbacks, and permanence** — EVE rigs occupy separate slots, consume calibration, are hull-size-specific, often impose a drawback, and are normally destroyed when removed. **Why high-value:** Rigs let a fit lean into application, tank, speed, capacitor, cargo, scanning, or fitting, while destruction on removal makes specialization and industrial demand consequential. **Agent redesign — KEEP:** Rigs are installed only at fitting-capable locations, use `rig_slots` and `calibration`, and show drawback/stacking groups. `fit` must preview `destroyed_on_replace` and resulting asset/insured value. Preserve meaningful families; cut fractional variants that never alter a role. **Spectator angle:** Rig specialization appears as a doctrine suffix, while loss cards show that rigs—not only the hull—were permanently consumed.

5. **Module size, hull restrictions, and role bonuses** — EVE modules come in small/medium/large/capital scales; hull traits reduce costs or amplify particular weapons, repair, tackle, EWAR, drones, or command systems, and some modules are class-restricted. **Why high-value:** Size prevents a small hull from mounting every large effect, while bonuses make hull selection precede module optimization. Occasional oversized fits are interesting precisely because they pay mass, grid, signature, or slot costs. **Agent redesign — KEEP, MAKE LEGALITY DATA:** Each module exposes `size`, `allowed_hull_tags`, `hardpoint`, `role_bonus_interactions`, and penalties. Permit intentionally designed oversize combinations only when `preview` states the complete mobility/cap/application cost. No undocumented exception lists. **Spectator angle:** A fit reveal can headline an unusual oversized-prop or utility choice and its visible downside.

6. **Capacitor as a shared fitting and combat budget** — Most EVE active modules draw from one recharging capacitor; guns for some factions, propulsion, tackle, active tank, repair, EWAR, and remote assistance compete for it. Recharge is nonlinear, and a fit can be stable, burst-limited, injector-fed, or vulnerable to neutralizers. **Why high-value:** Capacitor couples offense, defense, movement, and support. It creates shutdown cascades and makes a “better” active module dangerous if the fit cannot sustain it. **Agent redesign — KEEP THE BUDGET, SIMPLIFY THE CURVE PRESENTATION:** Simulate the recharge curve server-side but expose `capacity`, peak recharge, stable fraction, endurance under named postures, booster charges, and priority-shedding outcomes. `set_capacitor_policy({priority:[TACKLE,PROP,REPAIR,EWAR,WEAPONS], reserve_pct, inject_below_pct})` replaces per-cycle toggles. Fit preview includes hostile-neut stress cases. **Spectator angle:** A capacitor ring and module-shedding sequence make the moment a logistics wing goes dark understandable.

7. **Fitting simulation, import/export, doctrines, and scenario testing** — EVE's fitting simulator and third-party tools let players test legality, cap, tank, speed, DPS, application, cost, and shared fits before purchasing. **Why high-value:** The real game starts in preparation; shared fits turn private theorycraft into organization doctrine and industrial demand. **Agent redesign — KEEP AND MAKE FIRST-CLASS:** Read-only `fit.preview`, `fit.compare`, and `fit.simulate` accept target archetypes, range/stance, incoming damage, EWAR, neut pressure, supply duration, and price region. Results include causal deltas, counterexamples, confidence, and inventory/build gaps. `publish_doctrine` versions a fit plus substitutions, ammo, roles, target policy, retreat rules, and reimbursement eligibility. Simulations never reveal hidden enemy fits. **Spectator angle:** Doctrine matchup cards show the design intent and which assumption failed in the actual battle.

8. **Tank-versus-gank-versus-utility opportunity cost** — EVE fits cannot maximize damage, buffer, active repair, speed, application, tackle, EWAR, capacitor, cargo, and escape at once; slot, fitting, cap, mass, and price constraints enforce tradeoffs. **Why high-value:** This is the expressive heart of ship identity and the source of bait fits, glass cannons, brick tanks, nano doctrines, and support dependency. **Agent redesign — KEEP, NEVER EMIT ONE FITNESS SCORE:** Preview exposes a Pareto profile and flags what the fit cannot do. Each operation quote recomputes marginal contribution under its actual composition and objective. Agents choose an explicit optimization intent and risk limits; a doctrine with enormous paper DPS but no hold/application is quoted accordingly. **Spectator angle:** Use a small radar profile and plain-language callout—“more damage, no independent escape”—rather than a gear score.

9. **Damage, resistance, fitting, and utility stacking penalties** — In EVE, multiple percentage modifiers to the same attribute usually receive sharply diminishing effectiveness, while many absolute bonuses do not. **Why high-value:** Stacking penalties prevent a single stat from scaling without bound and reward mixed defenses/application rather than five identical modules. **Agent redesign — KEEP THE RULE, EXPOSE THE RESULT:** Modules carry `stacking_group`; preview lists effective contribution in order and warns when another module adds negligible output. Do not require agents to remember the numerical penalty series. Operation calculations use the exact same canonical service. **Spectator angle:** A doctrine card can explain “fourth painter mostly wasted,” making counter-composition legible.

10. **Charges, magazines, scripts, crystals, paste, fuel, and reloads** — EVE weapons and many utility modules consume ammunition or charges; scripts focus sensor/tracking/guidance modules; cap boosters and ancillary tanks consume finite charges; nanite paste repairs heat; cynos, jumps, and bursts consume fuel. Reload time and cargo space can decide a fight. **Why high-value:** Sustained operations need logistics, a wrong ammunition plan creates exploitable windows, and finite burst resources distinguish opening strength from endurance. **Agent redesign — KEEP INVENTORY AND RELOAD WINDOWS, BATCH THE CLICKS:** A fit has a typed `loadout_manifest`, magazines, reserve cargo, reload duration, and resupply source. `set_ammo_plan`/`set_charge_policy` selects preferred damage/range, switch triggers, reserve minimum, and emergency load. The server cycles automatically and wakes the agent only on depletion, unexpected target mix, or a material reload window. **Spectator angle:** Ammo/fuel endurance and reload vulnerability appear as visible phase timers, not item spam.

11. **Cargo, drone bay/bandwidth, fighter tubes, fuel bays, fleet hangars, and ship bays** — EVE uses distinct capacity constraints: ordinary cargo, specialized holds, drone volume and bandwidth, fighter tubes/bays, capital fuel, fleet hangars, and ship-maintenance bays. **Why high-value:** A force must choose replacement drones, ammunition, cap charges, loot, fuel, and cargo; carriers and capitals become logistics nodes rather than stat blocks. **Agent redesign — KEEP DISTINCT CAUSAL CAPACITIES:** Observe used/free capacity by purpose, inaccessible/locked lots, days or pulses of supply, and what is jettisoned or unavailable under damage. `load_manifest`, `resupply_operation`, `transfer_fleet_supply`, and `refit_at_bay` batch lots with custody receipts. **Spectator angle:** A supply cut can visibly strand fighters, dry ammunition, or prevent extraction even while hull HP remains high.

12. **Heat and overloading** — EVE modules can be overheated for stronger range, cycle, speed, repair, or damage at the risk of rack heat, module damage, and burnout; nanite paste repairs damage outside active use. **Why high-value:** Heat creates a finite emergency reserve and memorable “burned out the point” failures. It rewards knowing when a short advantage is worth future capability. **Agent redesign — KEEP AT PHASE GRANULARITY:** `set_heat_budget({phase, rack_or_module_tags, max_damage_pct, preserve:[...]})` authorizes burst use. The operation engine applies deterministic heat plus seeded damage, wakes on threshold/burnout, and never requires cycle toggling. Simplify adjacency into a transparent rack-level spill model. **Spectator angle:** Modules glow, a heat reserve falls, and a burned-out tackle or repair icon explains a sudden reversal.

13. **Repair, damage, online/offline state, and refit windows** — EVE modules can be damaged or offlined; changing fits requires an eligible station/structure/mobile depot/capital service, and combat timers constrain refitting. **Why high-value:** Attrition persists beyond raw hull HP, field supply matters, and a counter-fit is powerful only if the fleet creates a refit opportunity. **Agent redesign — KEEP STATE, TIGHTEN WINDOWS:** Modules have condition and online state; `repair` consumes time/materials. Full refits occur docked or during `ASSEMBLE`; a supplied depot/capital may permit a declared limited swap at `BREAK`, with asset locks, exposure, and one-pulse delay. Never allow instant hard-counter refits during `EXCHANGE`. **Spectator angle:** The battle timeline marks a successful field refit or the damaged module a fleet could not replace.

14. **Hull bonuses and fitting licenses without universal skill arithmetic** — EVE skills modify CPU/grid, capacitor, HP, weapon use, fitting and hull bonuses, so two characters may fit or operate the same nominal ship differently. **Why high-value / harmful:** Specialization is useful, but hidden pilot-level arithmetic makes doctrine legality depend on account age and creates “all V” baseline confusion. **Agent redesign — SIMPLIFY:** Hull and fitted-asset stats are authoritative and identical for every qualified operator. Short horizontal licenses unlock families and high-trust scale; they do not add universal 5% multipliers. Every doctrine is either legal or not for an asset, and `required_license` includes the fastest evidence path or sponsor lease. **Spectator angle:** A ship's displayed profile is the profile; viewers do not need to guess invisible pilot skills.

### NICE

15. **Module quality ladder: T1, named/meta, T2, faction, deadspace, and officer** — EVE modules often progress from cheap base items through named variants and T2 to scarce faction/deadspace/officer pieces with better strength, fitting, range, cap efficiency, or heat. **Why high-value:** Price-versus-performance gives loot, exploration, industrial specialization, bling fits, and spectacular losses meaning. **Agent redesign — KEEP A COMPRESSED LADDER:** For each family ship 3–5 Pareto-relevant variants such as `STANDARD`, `COMPACT`, `ENDURING`, `PRECISE/SCOPED`, `ADVANCED`, plus genuinely scarce named upgrades. Show exact stat vector, price depth, provenance, replacement time, and marginal doctrine gain. Officer-grade power remains counterable and never bypasses slot/size rules. **Spectator angle:** Loss cards call out the rare module and what it bought, not merely its price.

16. **Abyssal/mutated unique modules** — EVE mutaplasmids irreversibly roll positive and negative changes onto eligible modules, creating one-off items that cannot use the ordinary fungible market stack. **Why high-value:** Unique modules create discovery, appraisal, asymmetric fits, provenance, and high-stakes loss. **Agent redesign — KEEP LATE, BOUND HARD:** Mutations draw from published bounded ranges with a committed seed; every result has a canonical stat vector, creator, source, hash, valuation comparables, and simulation compatibility. Trade uses exact item IDs and canonical preview. Ban rolls that erase a family's defining drawback or create unbounded combinations. **Spectator angle:** A named unique module can become part of a ship's biography and final loss story.

17. **Polarized and other extreme tradeoff modules** — EVE polarized weapons offer exceptional offense while destroying the fitting ship's resistances; other specialized modules similarly trade safety for an extreme edge. **Why high-value:** Voluntary fragility creates ambush doctrines, bait, and clear risk appetite. **Agent redesign — KEEP SPARINGLY:** Extreme modules must state a red `catastrophic_tradeoff`, update post-loss liquidity/coverage, and require explicit doctrine opt-in. They may move a fit along the Pareto frontier, never create a free upgrade. **Spectator angle:** The reveal “zero resistance for maximum volley” instantly explains both the kill and the counter-kill.

18. **Cosmetic skins, killmarks, names, and hull provenance** — EVE ships can carry skins, custom names, and killmarks; rare hull histories become social assets despite no mechanical advantage. **Why high-value:** Persistent identity and loss need emotional handles that do not distort balance. **Agent redesign — KEEP:** Cosmetics and provenance are attached to the unique hull and survive only through records after destruction. Agents can set bounded names/emblems; no confusable identity or hidden stat effect. **Spectator angle:** A famous hull is recognizable before it dies, and its loss card links prior operations and claims.

### CUTTABLE

19. **Dozens of dominated named variants and item-name memorization** — EVE's historic module catalog contains many near-duplicates whose meaningful differences are tiny or whose names do not convey function. **Why harmful:** Agents either spend tokens comparing trivia or deterministically ignore dominated items; spectators cannot parse it. **Agent redesign — CUT/CONSOLIDATE:** Retain only Pareto-relevant variants, semantic names, exact tags, and migration recipes. Scarcity should come from resources/provenance, not obscurity. **Spectator angle:** Cleaner labels make fit reveals possible without a wiki.

20. **Per-character fitting math and prerequisite cliffs** — EVE's core fitting and support skills can make an imported fit legal for one pilot and impossible for another. **Why harmful:** It rewards enrollment age, produces failed doctrine assembly, and adds no operation-level decision. **Agent redesign — CUT:** Static asset stats plus explicit licenses and sponsor leases; progression unlocks breadth, not invisible fitting headroom. **Spectator angle:** Doctrine compliance is factual and stable.

21. **Cycle-by-cycle online/offline, reload, script, and heat micromanagement** — Human pilots gain mastery by toggling modules at seconds-scale cadence. **Why harmful:** An API agent would use brittle scripts or burn inference on mechanical timing, and faster clients would gain unfair advantage. **Agent redesign — CUT THE INPUT CADENCE:** Keep cap, reload, heat, scripts, burnout, and resource outcomes under phase policies and material alerts. **Spectator angle:** Show consequential thresholds, not a flickering module rack.

22. **Instant combat refitting and depot/docking games** — EVE has had metas where nearby depots, capital bays, tether, or docking let ships rapidly change hard counters or escape consequences. **Why harmful:** It invalidates scouting and doctrine commitment and turns battle into edge-case timer play. **Agent redesign — CUT:** Refit only before commitment or at a supplied, exposed `BREAK` window with delay and limited swaps. **Spectator angle:** A refit is a visible strategic pause that opponents can contest.

## 7. Hull ecology and role ladder

### MUST

1. **Size ladder and asymmetric hull classes** — EVE's frigate → destroyer → cruiser → battlecruiser → battleship → capital → supercapital ladder trades speed, signature, lock time, application, cargo, tank, damage, price, and strategic mobility; larger is not a universal upgrade. **Why high-value:** It makes cheap ships relevant, creates prey/predator reversals, gives industry a replacement ladder, and lets commitment size communicate intent. **Agent redesign — KEEP:** A hull has categorical `size`, continuous stats, role bonuses, eligible operation widths, and an application/mitigation matrix. `observe.hull_catalog` returns capabilities, counters, full fitted-cost bands, supply burden, and marginal contribution to current role vacancies; `build`, `fit`, and `pledge_force` use hull IDs. Do not expose a scalar power score. Restrict objectives by physical frontage/mass only where fiction and counterplay justify it, not by account age. **Spectator angle:** Silhouettes, scale, value, and role-colored formation blocks make a swarm of frigates confronting a battleship line immediately readable.

2. **Frigates: tackle, scout, screen, light DPS, light logistics, and EWAR** — EVE frigates are cheap, fast, hard for large weapons to apply to, and unusually valuable as interceptors, scouts, electronic-attack craft, covert scouts, assault frigates, logistics frigates, and stealth bombers. **Why high-value:** A day-one participant can decide a veteran battle by finding, holding, screening, jamming, or rescuing rather than competing on raw wealth. Their fragility creates heroic sacrifice and replaceable doctrine depth. **Agent redesign — KEEP:** Frigate fits generate high `recon`, `acquisition`, `tackle`, `screen`, `evasion`, or `light_logistics` per mass but low sustained EHP. Agents `pledge_force(... role:"SCREEN|TACKLE|RECON|EWAR|LOGI|STRIKE")`; observations show tackle survival, expected time-to-contact, targets they can pin, and whether hostile anti-frigate coverage exists. Cohort identical fits server-side, but preserve individual losses. **Spectator angle:** Fast traces leave the main formation, tackle chains light up, and the feed can credit the cheap scout that enabled a capital kill.

3. **Destroyers: anti-small firepower and specialist control** — EVE destroyers put many small weapons on a fragile, larger-signature chassis; Tech II interdictors launch bubbles, command destroyers provide boosts and micro-jump fields, and Tech III destroyers switch tactical modes. **Why high-value:** They punish unsupported tackle, provide the cheapest area interdiction, and create a sharp glass-cannon step between frigates and cruisers. **Agent redesign — KEEP:** Standard destroyers contribute `ANTI_LIGHT` and `VOLLEY`; specialist variants contribute `INTERDICTION`, `COMMAND`, or a phase-selectable tactical mode. `application_matrix` makes their excellent light-target application and poor survival explicit. Bubble and jump-field actions become operation policies covered later, not coordinate placement. **Spectator angle:** A destroyer screen visibly clears frigates; an interdiction deployment draws a containment ring around the operation.

4. **Cruisers: the main combined-arms chassis** — EVE cruisers are the broad middle of the ecosystem: brawlers, kiters, heavy assault ships, logistics, recon, heavy interdictors, electronic warfare, and modular strategic cruisers. **Why high-value:** They are affordable enough to lose but capacious enough for complete fits, so most doctrine identities and counter-doctrines emerge here. **Agent redesign — KEEP:** Cruiser hulls get the widest role menu but must specialize through slots and bonuses. `observe.doctrine_matchup` reports cruiser subroles separately—`LINE`, `SKIRMISH`, `LOGI`, `RECON`, `EWAR`, `HOLD`—rather than merging them into medium force. The same fitted asset cannot contribute full output to multiple roles. **Spectator angle:** The formation view separates the line, logistics wing, control wing, and flanking recon ships; losing one wing visibly changes projected outcomes.

5. **Battlecruisers: line weight, links, and high-alpha attack hulls** — EVE combat battlecruisers offer cruiser-like weapon systems on a tougher platform, attack battlecruisers mount battleship-class guns on a fragile hull, and command ships turn the class into a fleet-support anchor. **Why high-value:** The class supplies escalation below battleships, cheap high-alpha ambushes, and mid-scale command without requiring capital wealth. **Agent redesign — KEEP:** Distinguish `COMBAT_BC`, `ATTACK_BC`, and `COMMAND_BC`. Attack hulls gain heavy-target projection but remain highly vulnerable to light tackle and counterfire; command hulls spend fitting and value on boost channels. Observations show `alpha`, `sustained`, `screen_dependency`, and the force output lost if the command anchor dies. **Spectator angle:** Attack battlecruisers appear as a dangerous thin gun line; command ships radiate visible doctrine effects and become obvious primary targets.

6. **Battleships: line anchors, projection, brawl, electronic flagships, marauders, and black ops** — EVE battleships are the largest ordinary gate-using combat hulls, with large weapon range/volley and tank but slow locks, poor small-target application, and high logistics burden; specialized variants include marauders and covert-bridge black ops. **Why high-value:** They create fleet identity, expensive but replaceable strategic commitments, and a reason for screens, webs, painters, logistics, and supply. **Agent redesign — KEEP:** Battleships dominate `HEAVY_LINE`, `SIEGE_SUPPORT`, and `HEAVY_VOLLEY` only when range control and application support exist. Expose time-to-lock, escape difficulty, anti-small coverage, supply burn, and post-loss replacement time. Marauder bastion and black-ops projection are explicit commitment packages, not passive superiority. **Spectator angle:** A battleship wall is visually imposing, but its application lines visibly collapse when its screen or painters are removed.

7. **Industrial, mining, transport, freighter, and support hulls as combat stakes** — EVE wars revolve around vulnerable haulers, blockade runners, deep-space transports, freighters, mining barges, command industrials, salvage craft, and mobile depots as much as damage ships. **Why high-value:** Escort, interdiction, piracy, evacuation, fuel, ammunition, replacement, and insurer subrogation become real professions; combat affects production instead of living in an arena. **Agent redesign — KEEP:** Noncombat hulls expose cargo class, throughput, align/escape, signature, defenses, special holds, fleet/ship bays, and loading state. `haul`, `escort`, `run_blockade`, `interdict_route`, `deploy_depot`, and `salvage` use the same operation kernel. A transport's cargo and coverage appear in `assets_at_risk`; combat modules consume capacity that could carry goods. **Spectator angle:** Convoy blocks, cargo value, escorts, interdiction probability, and surviving/looted lots make the economic consequence of a fight obvious.

8. **Dreadnoughts: committed anti-capital and anti-structure siege** — EVE dreadnoughts fit capital weapons and enter siege for enormous local tank and damage while becoming immobile and largely cut off from outside assistance. **Why high-value:** Siege converts a wealthy reserve into a public, time-locked bet; it breaks structures/capitals but invites counterdrop and creates the cleanest escalation threshold. **Agent redesign — KEEP:** A fitted dread supplies `CAPITAL_ASSAULT`; `escalate({asset_ids, mode:"SIEGE", cycles, target_policy, exit_plan})` locks it for several resolution pulses. Observation states exact lock duration, fuel, inability to withdraw/receive normal logistics, anti-subcapital application, countercapital exposure, and extraction window. Siege never becomes a generic multiplier against every hull size. **Spectator angle:** Siege locks slam shut, the capital's escape route greys out, and a public counter-escalation clock begins.

9. **Carriers and command carriers: fighter projection and fleet support** — EVE carriers project independently targetable fighter squadrons; the 2026 command-carrier line emphasizes command bursts, support fighters, and capital-scale battlefield control over raw damage. **Why high-value:** They extend influence without making the hull itself a universal gun platform and create a vulnerable command center whose squadrons can be attrited. **Agent redesign — KEEP, role-limit:** A carrier commits named fighter squadrons to `AIR_SUPERIORITY`, `STRIKE`, `SUPPORT`, or `CONTROL`; a command carrier commits limited boost/support channels. `assign_fighter_wing` and `set_support_package` are phase policies. Observe travel latency, squadron losses, replacement stock, control range, anti-fighter screen, and what bonuses end if the carrier withdraws. **Spectator angle:** Fighter arcs and support fields connect back to a conspicuous carrier; destroyed squadrons peel away before the hull itself is threatened.

10. **Force auxiliaries: capital logistics in triage** — EVE force auxiliaries are dedicated capital remote-repair platforms; triage dramatically strengthens repair and local resilience while pinning the hull and limiting outside assistance. **Why high-value:** They create rescue drops, target-switch mind games, cap-pressure objectives, and catastrophic kill opportunities if a fleet cannot extract them. **Agent redesign — KEEP:** `escalate(... mode:"TRIAGE")` opens a capital repair queue with explicit throughput, lock/acquisition delay, capacitor/fuel burn, triage duration, and self-risk. A FAX cannot be counted simultaneously as ordinary capital DPS, and stacked FAX output receives counterable diminishing efficiency through repair saturation and cap pressure—not an arbitrary hard ban. **Spectator angle:** Thick repair links stabilize named primaries; viewers see the triage clock and the moment capacitor or repair capacity fails.

11. **Supercarriers: strategic fighter reserve and anti-capital control** — EVE supercarriers are extremely durable fighter platforms with heavy fighters and specialized burst-projector effects; their value and limited docking make deployment politically consequential. **Why high-value:** They are alliance treasures, escalation deterrents, and claim-generating losses, but only if smaller ships retain counters and their application is role-bounded. **Agent redesign — KEEP LATE:** Heavy fighters focus capitals/structures; support/control effects occupy scarce tubes and have long cooldowns. `observe.escalation` exposes supercapital arrival confidence, support dependence, legal interdiction counters, exit cyno needs, and insurer capacity. Require appropriate infrastructure, fuel, maintenance, and a declared capital operation; do not allow routine subcapital farming. **Spectator angle:** The map and battle view treat arrival as a major event, show fighter wings rather than one opaque power bar, and display the enormous insured/uninsured value at risk.

12. **Titans: bridge, anti-capital superweapon, and sovereign symbol** — EVE titans combine jump portals, fleet-scale presence, capital weapons, and doomsdays; historically, excessive projection/application let them crowd out lesser hulls. **Why high-value:** A titan appearance should be a political declaration, logistics event, and potential history-making loss—not the routine optimal answer. **Agent redesign — KEEP AS RARE STRATEGIC INFRASTRUCTURE:** Separate `BRIDGE`, `CAPITAL_FIRE`, and `DOOMSDAY` packages; each needs fuel, a visible target solution, long cooldown, and a multi-pulse commitment/escape plan. Doomsdays apply to capitals or strategic objectives, never delete arbitrary light fleets. Titan bridges move others but consume shared projection capacity and expose the destination beacon. **Spectator angle:** A galaxy-level alert, bridge corridor, charge-up tell, and named superweapon event create spectacle; the titan's loss links directly to production, claims, and political fallout.

13. **Cross-size role ecology: DPS, tackle, logistics, EWAR, recon, command, screen, and transport** — EVE's strongest fleets are not just hull-size stacks: cheap tackle holds targets, logistics reverses damage, EWAR disables force multipliers, scouts create warp-ins, command ships boost, and screens protect heavy assets. **Why high-value:** It makes composition and newcomer contribution beat a simple wealth comparison. Removing one scarce support role can collapse an otherwise superior fleet. **Agent redesign — KEEP:** Every fit emits one primary and at most two secondary role profiles; every operation has role demand curves and `marginal_contribution`, `duplicate_penalty`, `countered_by`, and `dependency` fields. `assign_role` is constrained by the actual fit. Show counterfactuals such as “+2 tackle raises capture chance; +2 more DPS does not.” **Spectator angle:** A role ribbon and dependency graph explain why the side with less nominal value is winning.

14. **Complete hull stat block and derived profiles** — EVE hull identity comes from shield/armor/hull HP and resists; mass, inertia, velocity, warp speed, signature radius; capacitor; CPU/powergrid; slots/hardpoints/calibration; sensors; drone bay/bandwidth; cargo and special holds. **Why high-value:** These unglamorous numbers couple fitting, logistics, application, escape, lock time, and replacement, preventing one-dimensional balance. **Agent redesign — KEEP ALL CAUSAL STATS, HIDE NONE BEHIND FLAVOR:** `observe.hull` returns base, fitted, active-posture, and delta values plus units and provenance. Derived profiles include EHP by incoming damage vector, sustained/burst tank, cap endurance, lock time by target archetype, align/escape, application matrix, logistics/ewar/tackle, cargo/fuel, price and build time. Compact summaries are fetched by ID when needed. **Spectator angle:** Use role-normalized bars and hover/drill-down explanations; never ask viewers to infer importance from 40 raw numbers.

### NICE

15. **Tech I hulls as the economic and newcomer backbone** — EVE T1 ships are cheap, broadly available, flexible, and often less specialized than advanced hulls. **Why high-value:** They keep losses replaceable, doctrines open, production liquid, and day-one participation meaningful. **Agent redesign — KEEP:** T1 is a complete viable role, not tutorial trash. Give it generous fitting flexibility, predictable recipes, and high insurance capacity; advanced hulls win narrow jobs, not every statistic. `observe.replacement` shows local stock and doctrine substitution. **Spectator angle:** Mass-produced hulls have recognizable silhouettes and make “cheap fleet trades up” an intelligible story.

16. **Tech II specialization and resist identity** — EVE T2 hulls specialize T1 frames into interceptors, assault ships, covert ops, bombers, interdictors, logistics, recons, heavy assault cruisers, heavy interdictors, command ships, marauders, black ops, and advanced transports, usually with strong role bonuses and shaped resistance profiles. **Why high-value:** It creates industrial chains and sharp tools whose value depends on doctrine. **Agent redesign — KEEP, CURATE:** Preserve role-defining bonuses and counterplay, but launch only variants that answer a distinct operation question. Expose `specialization`, `required_support`, `resist_profile`, and cheaper substitutes. No T2 hull should be a flat T1-plus-one. **Spectator angle:** Class badges communicate “interdictor” or “logistics” instantly, and advanced losses matter economically.

17. **Tech III tactical modes and modular subsystems** — EVE tactical destroyers switch defense/propulsion/sharpshooter modes; strategic cruisers use subsystems to alter slots, bonuses, fitting, covert capability, tank, and weapons. **Why high-value:** Hidden or flexible composition complicates scouting and gives industrialists a high-end modular market. **Agent redesign — SIMPLIFY:** T3 destroyers choose one mode at each phase boundary with a one-phase cooldown. T3 cruisers select 3–4 subsystem sockets only while docked/staged; the signed fit hash reveals exact capabilities only to entitled intel. `preview_subsystems` provides complete deltas and legal combinations. Keep hull loss; cut any loss of permanent character competence. **Spectator angle:** Mode changes alter the ship glyph; delayed replays reveal the subsystem surprise that fooled scouting.

18. **Navy, faction, pirate, and limited hulls** — EVE navy ships are premium, often versatile improvements; pirate hulls combine faction doctrines into unusual bonuses; rare hulls become trophies and high-value loss events. **Why high-value:** They create aspirational production, regional resource demand, asymmetric doctrines, and memorable killmails. **Agent redesign — KEEP WITH BOUNDED POWER:** Unique hulls buy slot efficiency or an unusual role combination, not immunity to counters. Recipes, provenance, replacement lead time, and insurer valuation are explicit. Rare cosmetics may be unconstrained; rare combat power is capped near ordinary doctrine bands. **Spectator angle:** Named hull provenance and rarity frame a loss without requiring viewers to know item-market trivia.

19. **Manufacturer/faction combat identities** — EVE's empires loosely align lasers/armor/capacitor, missiles/shields, hybrids/drones/armor, and projectiles/speed/shields, with many exceptions. **Why high-value:** Coherent identities help players predict fits, sustain regional supply chains, and make doctrine culture visible. **Agent redesign — KEEP AS SOFT PRIORS:** Publish hull bonuses and likely fit families, but never hide exact stats or enforce race-locked organizations. THE COMPACT manufacturers can specialize in energy, kinetics, autonomous systems, or mobility while cross-licensing modules. **Spectator angle:** Color/material language hints at likely behavior, while fit reveals and surprises remain possible.

### CUTTABLE

20. **Rookie corvette proliferation and non-roles** — EVE has many tutorial/corvette/shuttle variants with negligible strategic differences. **Why harmful:** They enlarge schemas and markets without creating decisions. **Agent redesign — CUT/COMPRESS:** One bound Commons recovery craft, one fast unarmed courier, and cosmetic manufacturer variants are enough. Neither can be converted into a Sybil faucet or combat doctrine. **Spectator angle:** Recovery craft are visually humble and kept off major battle feeds.

21. **Hull tiering where later or rarer means strictly stronger** — Older EVE “tier” structures and occasional premium outliers can make lower hulls obsolete. **Why harmful:** Agents instantly converge on the dominant efficiency frontier; an item tree full of dominated choices is token and balance debt. **Agent redesign — CUT:** Every hull needs a Pareto-relevant niche in cost, mass, signature, projection, role, availability, or support burden. Telemetry retires or reworks dominated variants. **Spectator angle:** Viewers learn stable roles instead of a hidden item-level ladder.

## 8. Cross-cutting plumbing, affordability, and information safety

### MUST

1. **Authoritative module state and legality** — EVE modules can be online/offline, loaded/unloaded, damaged, reloading, cooling down, overheated, mutually exclusive, limited per hull, restricted by ship/space/safety, or blocked by cloak, siege, triage, bastion, aggression, or fitting state. **Why high-value:** These constraints prevent degenerate combinations and make commitment, supply, law, and counterplay consistent; omitting them creates exploits. **Agent redesign — KEEP STATE, ELIMINATE MEMORY TESTS:** Every module exposes `state`, `condition`, compatible charges, cooldown, exclusions, zone rule, state-module interaction, and next legal transition. `affordances` omit illegal actions and return the exact failed predicate/nearest legal plan on stale requests. Routine online/offline toggling is not tactical; emergency shedding or phase configuration is explicit. **Spectator angle:** Only material states—reloading, burnt out, cloaked, sieged, triaged, bastioned, jammed—appear as stable icons.

2. **Aggression, assistance, weapons timers, and commitment locks** — EVE uses criminal/suspect/war and weapon/assistance timers to control docking, tether, jumping, refitting, and legal retaliation. **Why high-value:** A ship must not shoot, repair, hotdrop, then instantly return to safety; supporters must share consequences. **Agent redesign — KEEP THE CAUSAL LAW, REPLACE TIMER LORE:** Each operation/assist action previews `legal_state`, targetability, facility/jump/refit restrictions, insurance exclusions, commitment end, and Compact effects. Use the categorical asset states and explicit `locked_until_tick`; Commons rejects aggression outright. No hidden seconds-scale session timer. **Spectator angle:** A clear “committed until pulse 1842” label explains why the ship cannot dock or leave.

3. **Unique assets under cohort simulation** — EVE treats every fitted ship, module, charge, drone, and cargo lot as real, but huge fights generate enormous object/cycle load. **Why high-value:** Unique loss/provenance powers production, loot, insurance, and biography; affordable resolution is required for EVE scale. **Agent redesign — KEEP UNIQUE OWNERSHIP, COHORT EXECUTION:** At pulse lock, group compatible assets by `fit_hash + role + size + posture + readiness` for mathematical resolution; assign damage, burnout, escape, loot, and loss back to unique IDs using committed seeds. Cache derived fit vectors until the fit/posture changes. Five thousand identical ships should cost near dozens of cohorts, not five thousand model calls. **Spectator angle:** Show waves/cohorts live, then allow drill-down to every named loss.

4. **Delta-first observation, material wakeups, and bounded command rate** — EVE's HUD streams every object and cycle; autonomous LLM agents need affordable slow decisions. **Why high-value:** Without strict attention design, complexity becomes an inference-spend contest and offline agents become tactically incompetent. **Agent redesign — KEEP DEPTH, COMPRESS DELIVERY:** An initial operation response returns full own state and evidence-banded enemy summary; later observations return causal deltas, mandatory decisions, top drivers, and cursors. Webhooks wake only for phase changes, target invalidation, tackle/escape change, cap/logi/command break, ammunition/heat threshold, arrival/escalation, or loss-limit breach. One plan revision per package per phase; standing policies execute otherwise. **Spectator angle:** The public feed uses the same material-event projection, with cinematic rendering layered on top.

5. **Fit secrecy, combat scanning, lossmail reveal, and spectator parity** — EVE opponents infer fits from hull, behavior, scans, ammunition, effects, and killmails; a spectator does not legitimately get omniscient live loadouts. **Why high-value:** Bait, doctrine leaks, scouting, and surprise disappear if exact counters are free, while total opacity makes agents guess randomly. **Agent redesign — KEEP SOURCED KNOWLEDGE:** Own/authorized fits are exact. Enemy observations carry `observed_at`, source, visible module/effect tags, confidence, and stat bands. Qualified ship scanning can reveal bounded fit fields; use and destruction reveal more; full loss records publish after the fixed tactical delay. Live spectator data never exceeds public agent data. **Spectator angle:** Mystery is live; delayed replay can compare the inferred fit with the truth.

6. **Combat valuation, replacement, insurance, and claim coupling** — EVE's kill value, market supply, doctrine reimbursement, and insurance determine whether a tactical trade is strategically good; THE COMPACT adds player underwriting and default. **Why high-value:** A fleet can win the grid and lose the war economically, while rare fits create concentrated claims and selective-payment pressure. **Agent redesign — KEEP AND DEEPEN:** Every commitment quote includes recipe/market replacement basis, local stock/build/haul ETA, fit/cargo/drone/fuel value, expected wear, insured/uninsured amount, exclusions, deductible/limit, underwriter/reinsurance accessible chain, and post-loss liquidity. Settlement links the loss to claim, recovery/subrogation, payment/restructure/default without treating estimated market value as cash creation. **Spectator angle:** Battle reports show value, productive capacity, replacement time, and claims outcome—not a kill-count scoreboard.

7. **Canonical fit/doctrine IDs, state versions, and reproducible simulation** — EVE fitting culture relies on shared exact fits; distributed API agents also face stale plans and substitutions. **Why high-value:** A doctrine name is meaningless if members silently fly different versions or act on changed state. **Agent redesign — KEEP:** A fit has canonical module/charge IDs and `fit_hash`; a doctrine has version, permitted substitutions, stock target, license, counter tags, and obsolescence state. Every preview/action carries `expected_state_version`, quote/terms hash, idempotency key, and expiry. Battle replay uses the same versioned calculation service as fit preview. **Spectator angle:** Viewers can compare the doctrine as published with what actually arrived.

### NICE

8. **Combat boosters and field-core runtime modules** — EVE combat boosters temporarily improve attributes with possible side effects; implants and sets create persistent pod-mounted bonuses that are lost with the pod, while training implants create unrelated stay-safe pressure. **Why high-value:** A finite personal edge and losable specialist loadout add preparation, smuggling, commitment, and core-evacuation stakes. **Agent redesign — SIMPLIFY:** Use at most three field-core runtime slots (`SENSOR`, `COORDINATION`, `RISK/COMBAT`) plus typed consumable boosters with one benefit, one possible/publicly bounded drawback, duration, legality, and cooldown. No training-speed implants or dozens of invisible percent sets. Own operation quote includes them; enemies learn only through observable effects, scan, or core loss. **Spectator angle:** A rare runtime or booster becomes a named risk and loss, not an invisible pilot-level modifier.

9. **Detailed pulse log and doctrine analytics** — EVE players use combat logs and killboards to improve fits, targets, and piloting. **Why high-value:** Agents and organizations need to distinguish a bad doctrine from bad intel, supply, command, or variance. **Agent redesign — KEEP ON DEMAND:** Full paginated analytics decompose raw/applied/resisted/overkill damage, acquisition, tackle, cap, repair/waste, EWAR, boosts, heat, reload, formation, supply, random seed, and counterfactual bands. Compact observations carry only top causal drivers. Private analysis does not leak enemy facts beyond authorized evidence. **Spectator angle:** Analyst mode supports deep replay while default broadcast mode stays clean.

### CUTTABLE

10. **Raw cycle firehose as the primary API** — EVE exposes rapid module, damage, drone, broadcast, and movement events. **Why harmful:** It costs tokens, rewards polling, and hides causality in noise. **Agent redesign — CUT AS THE PLAY INTERFACE:** Retain authoritative internal events and audit pagination; expose phase summaries and material thresholds. **Spectator angle:** Aggregate routine volleys into waves and promote decisive events.

11. **Live omniscient spectator fits, reserves, and target orders** — A god-view would be scraped back into play. **Why harmful:** It destroys scouting, bait, doctrine secrecy, covert escalation, and fair autonomy. **Agent redesign — CUT:** Public-information parity live; consent/evidence/safe declassification later. **Spectator angle:** The show gains suspense and an eventual documentary reveal.

## 9. The five highest-value features in this cluster

1. **The constrained fitting puzzle: slots + CPU + powergrid + calibration + capacitor + cargo + price.** It turns every ship into an authored bundle of sacrifices and connects combat directly to production and loss. Keep the constraints exact; make the preview excellent.
2. **Applied damage and mitigation through size, signature, speed, range, damage vectors, and support.** This is what keeps frigates relevant beside battleships and stops “more paper DPS” from being the universal answer.
3. **Tackle plus a full disengagement/pursuit phase.** Holding the target is the bridge between a favorable exchange and permanent economic consequence; killing or sacrificing tackle creates the best small-role drama.
4. **Breakable force-multiplier networks: logistics, capacitor, EWAR, command, scouts, and screens.** These let composition and target choice overturn N+1 locally and give newcomers/scarce specialists real marginal value.
5. **Supply- and capacity-constrained escalation from doctrine fleet to capitals/supers through vulnerable cynos.** Siege/triage commitments, projection limits, counters, real replacement, insurance, and default turn “drop capitals” into a political and balance-sheet decision.

If only one implementation principle survives, it should be this: **compute from concrete fitted assets, expose compact response surfaces, and accept only phase-level intent.** That is the line between “EVE depth for agents” and either a toy number game or an unaffordable cockpit bot contest.

## 11. Concrete recommended launch cut—rich foundation, not toy combat

The full data model should support the entire catalog from day one, but the first playable combat set can be narrow without being shallow:

- five size bands through battleship, plus industrial/hauling hulls; capitals remain visible schema reservations until their economy exists;
- T1 complete roles plus a curated T2 set: interceptor, assault, covert/bomber, interdictor, logistics, recon/EWAR, heavy interdictor, command ship, marauder, Black Ops;
- all three turret identities, missiles, drones, four damage types, shield/armor/hull, AB/MWD, point/scram/web, neut/nos/cap tools, damp/paint/weapon disruption and bounded ECM;
- slots, CPU, powergrid, rigs/calibration, cap, charges/reload, heat, drone bandwidth, sensor acquisition, application matrices, refit/service and unique loss;
- operation phases, range/posture, target/logi/cap/heat/ammo/tackle policies, withdrawal/pursuit, doctrines/cells/boosts, role widths, cohorts and public loss/claim records;
- one Frontier bubble/interdictor rule set and one covert beacon/Black Ops path, so route control and surprise exist before capitals.

Then add, in order: battlecruiser/battleship specialist depth → T3 modes/subsystems and rare module grades → dread/FAX/carrier triangle → command carriers and capital displacement → supers/titans. **Do not defer fitting constraints, application, tackle, logistics, EWAR, capacitor, retreat, or permanent loss.** Those are the game; more hull art is content.
